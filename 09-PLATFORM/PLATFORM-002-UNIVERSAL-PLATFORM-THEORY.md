## SECTION 1 — PLATFORM THEORY

**PTH-01 (grounds UPL-01/02/13).** Platform is the theory of **composition-over-behavior**. Where Engineering theory explains *existence* and Runtime theory explains *behavior-over-existence*, Platform theory explains how behaving constructs are **composed for purpose**: assembled into capabilities, realized as components, exposed as services, surfaced as experiences, combined by composition, interconnected by integration, and governed as platforms. A platform is not a new kind of existence and not a new kind of behavior; it is a **structure of purpose** built by reference over the frozen foundations. Platform theory is stated as decidable, reproducible, record-based properties — never as engines, products, or technologies.

---

## SECTION 2 — PLATFORM EXISTENCE MODEL

**PTH-02 (grounds UPL-02/03/04/05).** A platform construct **exists** when it is identified (ENG-001), borne as an object (ENG-002), typed (ENG-004), connected via relationships/references (ENG-005), carries value (ENG-003), and — where it acts — behaves as a runtime construct (RUNTIME-\*). Platform existence is thus **derivative existence**: it adds no existence primitive; it is a *compositional standing* of already-existing, already-behaving constructs. A platform construct that cannot be reduced to identified, typed, related, behaving foundation constructs does not exist under this theory.

---

## SECTION 3 — PLATFORM PURPOSE THEORY

**PTH-03 (grounds UPL-06/08).** Purpose is the distinguishing concern of Platform. A **capability** is potential purpose (what can be done); a **service** is exposed purpose (purpose offered under contract); an **experience** is consumed purpose (purpose delivered through an interaction surface). Purpose is always **typed and decidable** — a platform construct declares which purpose it serves, and that declaration is a checkable property, never an implicit assumption. Purpose composes: the purpose of a platform is the well-founded composition of the purposes of its capabilities, components, and services.

---

## SECTION 4 — PLATFORM CAPABILITY THEORY

**PTH-04 (grounds UPL-06).** A **capability** is a typed, identified, composable unit of potential behavior. Capabilities are the **atoms** of platform composition: every service exposes one or more capabilities, every component realizes one or more capabilities, and every platform is a governed bundle of capabilities. A capability references (does not contain) the runtime behaviors that realize it and the types that classify it. Capabilities compose acyclically (UPL-10) and are never redefinitions of runtime behavior — they are *compositional wrappers of purpose* over behavior.

---

## SECTION 5 — PLATFORM INTERACTION THEORY

**PTH-05 (grounds UPL-08/09/11).** Interaction is how platform constructs meet: a **service** interacts through its explicit contract; an **experience** interacts through its interaction surface; an **integration** interacts across platform boundaries via ENG-005 references and runtime coordination (events/orchestration). All interaction is **contract-mediated and record-based**: no platform construct interacts through hidden behavior or an undeclared channel. Interaction introduces no new connection construct (UPL-11); it reuses ENG-005 relationship/reference and RUNTIME event/coordination by reference.

---

## SECTION 6 — PLATFORM COMPOSITION THEORY

**PTH-06 (grounds UPL-10).** Composition is the **central operation** of Platform. It is the typed, well-founded combination of capabilities, components, and services into higher constructs. Founding composition is **acyclic**: the composition graph is a DAG, so every composite is reducible to a finite tree of foundation-grounded leaves. Composition is **associative under typing** (composing typed constructs yields a typed construct) and **closed** (the composition of platform constructs is a platform construct). Non-founding (peer) associations may form graphs but never create a founding cycle.

---

## SECTION 7 — PLATFORM LIFECYCLE THEORY

**PTH-07 (grounds UPL-14; facet: Lifecycle).** Every platform construct progresses through an explicit, recorded, **forward-only** lifecycle (declared → active → superseded/retired), reusing ENG-000 lifecycle and ENG-005 lineage and the RUNTIME lifecycle concern. Lifecycle transitions are recorded and governed; breaking change is realized as **supersession** (a new-identity successor), never silent mutation. A construct's lifecycle state is a decidable property of its records.

---

## SECTION 8 — PLATFORM GOVERNANCE THEORY

**PTH-08 (grounds UPL-12/15).** Platform governance is **declarative, evaluative, and non-enforcing**. It reasons about whether platform constructs conform to UPL-01…15 and records conformance judgments against ENG-002 objects; it enacts nothing and confers no authority (ID-01, AUTH-06). Governance theory reuses the RUNTIME policy concern (policy as declarative constraint) and adds platform-level composition constraints. Governance is a *judgment function over records*, not an operational controller.

---

## SECTION 9 — PLATFORM EVOLUTION THEORY

**PTH-09 (grounds UPL-14).** Platforms evolve **additively**. New capabilities, components, services, experiences, compositions, and integrations append without redesigning or renumbering existing constructs. Evolution preserves acyclicity and typing; it never introduces a new primitive or redefines a frozen concept. Breaking evolution is supersession under ENG-000 change control. The platform concern set is **open**: PLATFORM-006…014 and future concerns extend the theory without re-founding it.

---

## SECTION 10 — PLATFORM INTELLIGENCE THEORY

**PTH-10 (facet: Intelligence; grounds UPL-02).** Platform intelligence is the **knowledge aspect** of composition: the capacity to reason over the platform's own capabilities, components, services, and their relationships as recorded knowledge. It reuses the UKB knowledge assets and the RUNTIME agent concern **by reference** as inputs; it defines no AI engine, model, or product (UPL-13). Intelligence is a decidable, record-based property (what can be derived from the platform's recorded structure), never a live runtime observation.

---

## SECTION 11 — PLATFORM QUALITY THEORY

**PTH-11 (facet: Quality; grounds UPL-08/10/12).** Platform quality is the degree to which platform constructs satisfy their declared contracts, compose well-foundedly, and conform to UPL-01…15. Quality is **measured on evidence** (recorded properties), is **decidable**, and is **non-coercive** (evaluative, per UPL-12). Quality attributes (e.g., composability, contract-completeness, traceability, reuse-fidelity) are typed properties, never technology benchmarks.

---

## SECTION 12 — PLATFORM VALIDATION THEORY

**PTH-12 (grounds UPL-03/07/08/10).** Validation is the **decidable checking** of a platform construct against the constitution: is it typed (UPL-03), objecthood/identity-bound (UPL-04/05), contract-explicit (UPL-07/08), acyclically composed (UPL-10), reference-only integrated (UPL-11), and technology-free (UPL-13)? Validation is deterministic and reproducible from records; a construct is valid iff it passes all applicable checks. Invalidity routes to a Gap Report.

---

## SECTION 13 — PLATFORM CERTIFICATION THEORY

**PTH-13 (facet: Certification; grounds UPL-15; STATUS-001 §1 DOMAIN-D).** Certification is a **DOMAIN-D** judgment recorded by a certification determination (program-level: PLATFORM-GOV-002). Certification theory holds that certification is (a) domain-isolated (never inferred from architecture coverage of source assets), (b) evidence-based, and (c) non-constitutive. A platform construct is *certifiable* when it is complete, consistent, dependency-closed, and conformant; it is *certified* only when a certification determination records it as such.

---

## SECTION 14 — PLATFORM RUNTIME THEORY

**PTH-14 (facet: Runtime; grounds UPL-02).** Every platform construct's **behavior** is a RUNTIME construct, reused by reference: a service's operation is a runtime execution/workflow; an experience's session is a runtime context; an integration's exchange is a runtime event/coordination; governance's evaluation is a runtime policy judgment. Platform theory adds **composition** over these behaviors and redefines none of them. The platform-runtime boundary is strict: Platform composes; Runtime behaves; the frozen RL-F2 program is the sole source of behavioral semantics.

---

## SECTION 15 — PLATFORM SUCCESS THEORY

**PTH-15 (grounds UPL-14/15; STATUS-001).** A platform **succeeds** when it composes behaving constructs into governed, contract-explicit, reusable capabilities/services/experiences that deliver declared purpose — while introducing no primitive, redefining no foundation, selecting no technology, and conferring no authority. Success is measured in **DOMAIN-B** by physical existence of the platform roadmap artifacts and in the design domains by completeness, consistency, coverage, and conformance — never by counting source-asset coverage as completion (STATUS-001 §2).
