## PREAMBLE

P.1 This instrument IS the permanent meta-constitutional instrument of UCOS Ω∞. It answers the one question that no existing instrument of the corpus answers: **what makes an artifact constitutional at all.**

P.2 Every existing constitution of UCOS Ω∞ declares supremacy over exactly one operational domain and disclaims every other domain. CEP-000 §1.4 declares that it "SHALL NOT author constitutional content, and SHALL NOT confer authority over the substance of the UCOS Ω∞ Constitution". CEP-000 §5.5 presupposes a Tier-1 Constitutional Authority. No instrument in the corpus defines the class of object that Tier 1 consists of, enumerates its members, or governs the admission of a new member. That vacancy IS the jurisdiction of this instrument, and it is the only jurisdiction of this instrument.

P.3 This instrument SHALL therefore be **meta** and never **substantive**. It governs the form, identity, standing, relationship, and lifecycle-recognition of constitutions as objects. It SHALL NOT decide what any constitution says.

P.4 This instrument SHALL exercise its jurisdiction exclusively by **delegation to existing owners**. Where a concern already has a canonical owner in the corpus, this instrument SHALL bind to that owner by reference and SHALL NOT re-legislate it. The binding table is Article LXXXII, and it is normative.

P.5 This instrument SHALL be **Zero-Finite**. It SHALL NOT assume a finite number of constitutions, constitutional kinds, hierarchy depths, lifecycle states, repositories, technologies, languages, runtimes, infrastructures, intelligences, universes, or civilizations. Every enumeration in this instrument is an OPEN enumeration unless explicitly declared CLOSED, and every CLOSED enumeration is closed by a stated invariant, never by convenience.

P.6 This instrument SHALL bind every constitutional artifact of UCOS Ω∞ that exists, and every constitutional artifact that will ever exist, from the moment of its ratification under CEP-006 — and not before. Its present status is PROPOSED. It asserts no ratified standing it does not hold. The identity of the authority competent to ratify it is an open constitutional question recorded as CMG-OQ-01.

## ARTICLE I — VISION

I.1 The vision of Constitutional Meta Governance IS a UCOS Ω∞ in which **no constitutional object can exist without a knowable authority, a knowable owner, a knowable lifecycle position, and a knowable place in a single acyclic order of precedence** — for every constitution that exists now and for every constitution that will ever exist.

I.2 The vision SHALL hold at unbounded scale. It SHALL remain true at one constitution and at an unbounded number of constitutions; across one repository and across an unbounded federation of repositories; under one intelligence and under an unbounded population of intelligences; within one universe of discourse and across an unbounded set of universes.

I.3 The vision SHALL be achieved by **recognition and allocation**, never by accumulation. Meta governance grows by recognizing new constitutional kinds and allocating them to owners; it SHALL NOT grow by absorbing the domains it allocates.

I.4 The vision SHALL be considered realized when, for every constitutional artifact in the corpus, the four questions of Article LXXIX.2 are answerable deterministically from recorded fact and without human interpretation.

I.5 The vision SHALL never be declared complete in the sense of closed. Constitutional completeness under this instrument means **no open gap**, not **no possible future concept**. The distinction is normative and is drawn in Article LXXVI.

II.1 The mission of this instrument IS to constitute the meta-constitutional layer of UCOS Ω∞ once, canonically, and non-duplicatively, and to bind every constitutional concern of the corpus either to itself or to an existing canonical owner.

## ARTICLE III — PURPOSE

III.1 The purpose of this instrument IS to eliminate five classes of constitutional defect that the corpus is otherwise structurally unable to detect: **unrecognized constitutionality**, **parallel authority**, **orphan governance**, **dangling superior reference**, and **undecidable precedence**.

III.2 Unrecognized constitutionality SHALL mean an artifact that functions as constitutional law without being recognized, registered, or bound as a constitution. This instrument makes recognition a condition of effect (Article XII.5).

III.3 Parallel authority SHALL mean two or more instruments holding authority over the same concern. This instrument makes single canonical authority a verifiable invariant (CMG-INV-02) enforced mechanically under Article L.

III.4 Orphan governance SHALL mean a governed concern with no owner, or an owner with no governed concern. This instrument makes both conditions detectable and both a validation failure (CMG-INV-03).

III.5 Dangling superior reference SHALL mean a subordination declared toward an instrument that cannot be located. This instrument requires every declared superior to resolve to a located artifact or to a recorded VACANT slot with a named closure procedure (Article XVII.4).

III.6 Undecidable precedence SHALL mean two instruments whose relative rank cannot be computed. This instrument establishes the single Constitutional Precedence Lattice (Article XVI) reconciling every precedence statement presently declared in the corpus.

III.7 The purpose SHALL NOT include the creation of new governance work. Where a defect class is already owned, this instrument SHALL bind to the owner and add nothing.

## ARTICLE IV — DEFINITIONS

IV.1 **Constitution** — a normative artifact that (a) declares its own authority, (b) declares its own scope of governance, (c) declares its own conflict rule, (d) binds subjects other than itself, (e) is amendable only by a declared amendment mechanism, and (f) is recognized in the Constitution Registry. All six conditions ARE REQUIRED. An artifact satisfying (a)–(e) but not (f) IS a **Latent Constitution** and SHALL be treated under Article XII.5.

IV.2 **Constitutional Artifact** — any artifact that is a Constitution, or that is normatively derived from a Constitution and carries binding force by that derivation (a Law, Principle, Theory, Invariant, Ontology, Taxonomy, Registry Model, Charter, Determination, Certification, Ratification, Freeze Record, or Amendment).

IV.3 **Constitutional Authority** — the recognized capacity to bind subjects within a declared scope. Authority is always **scoped**, always **recorded**, and never **inherent**.

IV.4 **Meta-Constitutional Matter** — the class of questions whose subject is a constitution rather than a governed thing: what a constitution is, whether an artifact is one, what standing it holds, who owns it, how it relates to others, and where it sits in precedence. This class IS the exclusive jurisdiction of this instrument.

IV.5 **Substantive Matter** — the class of questions whose subject is a governed thing rather than a constitution. This class IS excluded from the jurisdiction of this instrument absolutely.

IV.6 **Domain Owner** — the single instrument holding authority over a named concern. Recorded in Article LXXXII and in `CMG-REGISTRY.json`.

IV.7 **Delegation** — the recorded, bounded, revocable recognition by this instrument that a named concern is owned elsewhere. Delegation transfers nothing; it **acknowledges** an existing allocation and SHALL NOT create authority.

IV.8 **Precedence Lattice** — the single partial order over all constitutional artifacts by which any conflict between two of them resolves deterministically (Article XVI).

IV.9 **Constitution Registry** — the authoritative record of which artifacts ARE constitutions, their identity, standing, owner, precedence rank, and delegation bindings (Article XV).

IV.10 **Constitutional Kind** — a recognized category of constitutional object (Article XIII). The set of kinds IS OPEN.

IV.11 **Vacant Authority Slot** — a declared superior authority that no located artifact presently occupies. A vacancy IS a recorded fact, not a defect to be hidden, and carries a mandatory closure procedure.

IV.12 **Zero-Finite** — the design property that no ceiling is assumed on any dimension of growth, and that no enumeration is closed except by explicit invariant. Interpretation of unboundedness in this corpus is owned by AUTH-INF-001 and is consumed here by reference.

IV.13 **Constitutional Gap** — a meta-constitutional question that is (a) within jurisdiction, (b) not answered by this instrument, and (c) not delegated to a located owner. Recorded as CMG-GAP-nn.

IV.14 **Open Constitutional Question** — a question this instrument identifies but declines to decide because deciding it would exceed its jurisdiction or would require an authority that does not exist. Recorded as CMG-OQ-nn and requiring explicit ratification.

IV.15 Where a term used here is already defined by a located instrument of the corpus, the located definition governs and the definition here SHALL be read as a reference, never as a redefinition. CONST-11 remains the canonical constitutional glossary of the repository-closure corpus; this Article SHALL NOT displace it.

## ARTICLE VI — CONSTITUTION THEORY

VI.1 A constitution IS not a document. A constitution IS a **binding relation** between an authority, a scope, and a set of subjects, of which the document is the canonical record. This distinction is load-bearing: it is why a document can exist without being constitutional, and why constitutionality can lapse without the document changing.

VI.2 Constitutional force SHALL arise from three conditions holding simultaneously: **declared authority** within a **bounded scope** over **identifiable subjects**. Removal of any one condition removes constitutional force and SHALL move the artifact out of effect under Article XXVI.

VI.3 A constitution SHALL be **self-limiting**. An instrument that does not declare what it does not govern is indistinguishable from an instrument claiming universal authority, and universal authority is prohibited by CMG-L-03. Every constitution IS REQUIRED to carry an explicit negative scope.

VI.4 A constitution SHALL be **non-self-elevating**. No constitution may be the sole source of its own superiority. This principle is already law for Program Authority at CEP-000 §5.4 and is generalized here to all constitutional artifacts as CMG-L-04.

VI.5 A constitution SHALL be **decidable**. Every obligation it imposes SHALL be capable of a determinate PASS or BLOCKED evaluation by the validation authority (CEP-004 Article IV.3). An undecidable obligation IS a defect, not a high standard.

VI.6 A constitution SHALL be **finite in text and unbounded in reach**. Its text SHALL be bounded so that it can be read, validated, and frozen; its reach SHALL be unbounded so that it does not require amendment for each new subject. The mechanism reconciling the two IS configuration-driven subject binding (CMG-L-08).

VI.7 A constitution SHALL be **durable under supersession**. The superseded text SHALL survive as historical truth; only its effect ceases. Preservation is owned by CEP-007 Articles XIII–XV and is consumed here by reference.

VI.8 The theory admits **constitutions about constitutions** — meta-constitutions — and requires that they be strictly meta. A meta-constitution that decides substance collapses into a substantive constitution and creates parallel authority. This instrument's own jurisdiction limit (Article XIX) exists to prevent exactly that collapse in itself.

## ARTICLE VII — META GOVERNANCE THEORY

VII.1 Meta governance IS the governance of governance: the allocation, ordering, and recognition of authorities, as distinguished from the exercise of authority. Meta governance SHALL allocate and SHALL NOT exercise.

VII.2 A corpus of domain-scoped constitutions is **locally complete and globally incomplete**. Each instrument answers its own domain fully and disclaims all others; the disclaimers do not compose into an answer for questions that fall between domains. Meta governance exists to close that residue and only that residue.

VII.3 The residue SHALL be computed, not assumed. The residue IS the set of concerns for which no located instrument declares ownership. Article LXXVIII records the computation for the present corpus; Article LXXIX requires its recomputation on every change.

VII.4 Meta governance SHALL be **subtractive in ambition**. Every concern that can be delegated SHALL be delegated. The measure of a healthy meta layer IS the smallness of its own retained ownership, not its breadth.

VII.5 Meta governance SHALL be **orthogonal, not superior, to process governance**. Authority over *what counts as constitutional* and authority over *how constitutional work is performed* are different axes. CEP-000 holds the process axis and holds it supremely. This instrument holds the recognition axis. Neither axis subsumes the other, and Article XVII.5 provides the resolution procedure where a question appears to fall on both.

VII.6 Meta governance SHALL be **acyclic by construction**. A meta layer that depends on an instrument that depends on the meta layer is undecidable. This instrument therefore declares no dependency on any instrument for its own recognition, and every instrument that depends on it does so downward only (CMG-INV-05).

VII.7 Meta governance SHALL be **evidence-bound**. Every allocation, recognition, precedence rank, and vacancy SHALL be recorded as evidence under CEP-008 and SHALL be reproducible from the repository alone. A meta-governance claim that cannot be recomputed from repository state IS not a claim but an opinion.

VII.8 Meta governance SHALL survive its own absence of finality. Where no authority competent to ratify the meta layer exists, the meta layer SHALL operate PROVISIONALLY under CEP-006 Article I.4 rather than assert a finality it does not hold. This instrument does exactly that (P.6).

## ARTICLE VIII — GOVERNANCE PHILOSOPHY

VIII.1 **Knowledge Once.** A constitutional truth SHALL be stated in exactly one place and referenced everywhere else. Restatement is duplication, duplication is drift, and drift is constitutional failure.

VIII.2 **Extend before create.** The default disposition toward any new constitutional concern IS EXTEND an existing owner. CREATE IS permitted only after a recorded discovery that no owner exists. The discovery record IS REQUIRED, not advisory.

VIII.3 **Recognition over permission.** Meta governance SHALL make constitutionality visible rather than gate it politically. Its instrument of power is the registry, not the veto.

VIII.4 **Determinism over judgement.** Where a question can be decided by rule, it SHALL be decided by rule. Human judgement is reserved for questions that no rule can decide, and every such question SHALL be recorded as an open question rather than resolved silently.

VIII.5 **Honesty over completeness.** A recorded gap IS constitutionally superior to a concealed one. An instrument that declares its vacancies, its provisional standing, and its undecided questions IS more complete than one that asserts closure it cannot evidence.

VIII.6 **Preservation over correction.** Historical constitutional truth SHALL never be edited away. Correction proceeds by amendment and supersession with lineage, never by mutation.

VIII.7 **Neutrality absolutely.** Meta governance SHALL be neutral to technology, platform, infrastructure, language, database, cloud, vendor, intelligence, and civilization. Naming any of these in constitutional law IS PROHIBITED (CMG-L-09).

VIII.8 **Configuration over enumeration.** Where a set will grow, the law SHALL state the rule of membership and the configuration SHALL carry the members. Hard-coded membership in constitutional law IS a finite assumption and IS PROHIBITED.

## ARTICLE X — GOVERNANCE LAWS

X.1 **CMG-L-01 (Recognition).** An artifact SHALL exercise constitutional force only while it is recognized in the Constitution Registry. An unrecognized artifact SHALL NOT be cited as constitutional authority.

X.2 **CMG-L-02 (Single Authority).** Two constitutional artifacts SHALL NOT hold authority over the same concern. A detected overlap IS a defect and SHALL be resolved under Article LIV before either instrument advances.

X.3 **CMG-L-03 (Bounded Scope).** A constitutional artifact SHALL declare a positive scope and a negative scope. An artifact declaring no negative scope SHALL be treated as claiming universal authority, which IS PROHIBITED.

X.4 **CMG-L-04 (Non-Self-Elevation).** A constitutional artifact SHALL NOT be the sole source of its own superiority, SHALL NOT grant itself authority it was not allocated, and SHALL NOT expand its own jurisdiction by interpretation.

X.5 **CMG-L-05 (Downward Precedence).** Precedence SHALL flow downward only. A subordinate SHALL NOT override a superior, and a superior SHALL NOT derive standing from a subordinate.

X.6 **CMG-L-06 (Acyclicity).** The constitutional dependency graph and the precedence lattice SHALL both be acyclic. A cycle IS an undecidability and SHALL halt advancement of every artifact in the cycle.

X.7 **CMG-L-07 (Delegation Fidelity).** A delegation SHALL name the concern, the owner, and the basis, and SHALL create no authority. A delegation to an unlocated owner IS void and SHALL be recorded as a vacancy under Article XVII.4.

X.8 **CMG-L-08 (Configuration-Driven Binding).** The subjects bound by a constitutional artifact SHALL be determined by rule and configuration, never by an enumeration embedded in constitutional text.

X.9 **CMG-L-09 (Neutrality).** A constitutional artifact SHALL NOT name a technology, platform, infrastructure, programming language, database, cloud, vendor, product, or intelligence model as a condition of its law.

X.10 **CMG-L-10 (Evidence Binding).** Every meta-constitutional assertion SHALL be bound to evidence under CEP-008 and SHALL be recomputable from repository state alone.

X.11 **CMG-L-11 (Preservation).** Superseded, deprecated, retired, and archived constitutional text SHALL be preserved with lineage. Deletion of constitutional history IS PROHIBITED.

X.12 **CMG-L-12 (Provisional Standing).** An artifact SHALL NOT declare a standing higher than the standing conferred by a located authority. Where no such authority exists, the standing IS PROVISIONAL.

X.13 **CMG-L-13 (Jurisdiction Error).** An apparent conflict between this instrument and a domain owner SHALL be classified as a jurisdiction error and SHALL resolve in favour of the domain owner, voiding this instrument to the extent of the conflict.

X.14 **CMG-L-14 (No Parallel Machinery).** This instrument SHALL NOT create a second registry, lifecycle, traceability store, evidence store, validation engine, certification engine, ratification mechanism, or governance body where one exists.

X.15 The law set IS OPEN and extensible by amendment under Article XLIII. No law SHALL be repealed while any artifact depends on it; repeal proceeds by supersession under Article LXXII.

## ARTICLE XII — CONSTITUTION CLASSIFICATION

XII.1 Every constitutional artifact SHALL be classified along four independent axes, and the four axes together SHALL determine its treatment under this instrument:
- **Axis A — Kind**: what category of constitutional object it is (Article XIII).
- **Axis B — Standing**: the strength of force it presently carries (XII.2).
- **Axis C — Reach**: the breadth of subjects it binds (XII.3).
- **Axis D — Layer**: its position in the precedence lattice (Article XVI).

XII.2 The Standing values SHALL be: **META** (governs constitutionality itself), **FOUNDATIONAL** (governs a domain of substance), **DERIVED** (binds only by derivation from a superior), **INTERPRETIVE** (binds the reading of others without enacting), **DECLARATIVE** (records fact and asserts no force), and **LATENT** (functions as law without recognition). The value set IS OPEN.

XII.3 The Reach values SHALL be: **CORPUS-WIDE**, **PROGRAM-SCOPED**, **DOMAIN-SCOPED**, **ARTIFACT-SCOPED**, and **FEDERATION-SCOPED**. The value set IS OPEN. FEDERATION-SCOPED is declared now, before any federation exists, because a reach ceiling would be a finite assumption.

XII.4 Classification SHALL be **declared by the artifact and verified by the registry**, never inferred. An artifact that does not declare its classification SHALL be treated as LATENT until it does.

XII.5 A **LATENT** artifact — one that satisfies IV.1(a)–(e) but is not registered — SHALL NOT be cited as constitutional authority (CMG-L-01), SHALL be recorded as a finding under Article LII, and SHALL be brought to recognition or explicitly reclassified as DECLARATIVE. Latency IS a detectable defect, not a status.

XII.6 A **DECLARATIVE** artifact SHALL assert no force. The repository convention `AUTHORITY = NONE (DERIVED TRUTH)`, used pervasively across the corpus, IS the canonical expression of DECLARATIVE standing and SHALL be read as such. Derived truth SHALL always be reconciled against repository reality and SHALL never be treated as a source of authority.

XII.7 An **INTERPRETIVE** artifact SHALL bind how other artifacts are read and SHALL enact nothing by itself. AUTH-INF-001 IS the located INTERPRETIVE instrument of this corpus and IS the sole owner of constitutional interpretation. This instrument SHALL NOT interpret; where interpretation is required it SHALL refer.

XII.8 Classification SHALL be revisable only by amendment of the artifact's own declaration, followed by registry update. Reclassification by the registry alone IS PROHIBITED, because it would make the registry a source of authority rather than a record of it.

## ARTICLE XV — CONSTITUTION REGISTRY MODEL

XV.1 The **Constitution Registry** IS the authoritative record of which artifacts ARE constitutions and of the meta-facts about them. It IS a record, never a source of authority (XII.8).

XV.2 The Registry SHALL record, per artifact, exactly: identity, kind, standing, reach, precedence rank, declared positive scope, declared negative scope, owner, steward, lifecycle state, version, lineage predecessor, superior references, dependency references, delegation bindings, evidence references, and canonical home path. No further field IS REQUIRED and any further field IS OPTIONAL and configuration-declared.

XV.3 The Registry SHALL NOT be a new store. It IS a **projection over existing stores** and SHALL be materialized as the derived artifact `00-CMG/CMG-REGISTRY.json`, whose authoritative inputs are the constitutional artifacts themselves and the located registries of the corpus. Where the projection and an input disagree, the input governs.

XV.4 Registry entries SHALL be **append-only in identity** and **mutable in state**. An entry SHALL never be deleted; an artifact leaving effect changes state under Article XXVI and retains its entry.

XV.5 The Registry SHALL be **recomputable**. It SHALL be regenerable from repository state alone by the validator of Article L, and a Registry that cannot be regenerated IS invalid.

XV.6 Registry membership SHALL be **declaration-driven**: an artifact enters the Registry because it declares the six conditions of IV.1(a)–(e) and is admitted, not because a curator listed it. Curated membership would make the Registry a source of authority and is prohibited by XII.8.

XV.7 The Registry SHALL record **vacancies** as first-class entries. A vacancy entry carries the declared superior reference, the fact that no artifact occupies it, and the closure procedure. Concealing a vacancy IS PROHIBITED (CMG-P-06).

XV.8 The Registry SHALL be **federation-ready**. Registry identity SHALL be qualified by namespace (Article XXXIII) so that two repositories may federate without identifier collision, and no clause of this Article assumes a single repository.

## ARTICLE XVI — AUTHORITY HIERARCHY

XVI.1 There SHALL be exactly one **Constitutional Precedence Lattice** for UCOS Ω∞. Multiple disjoint precedence statements presently exist in the corpus; this Article reconciles them into one order without amending any of them.

XVI.2 The lattice SHALL be defined by **rank tiers**, descending:

| Tier | Name | Content | Located status |
|---|---|---|---|
| T0 | **Constitutional Source Corpus** | The frozen source material from which constitutional intent derives | Located, frozen, non-normative-as-law |
| T1 | **Constitutional Authority** | The ratified constitution of substance presupposed by CEP-000 §5.5 Tier 1 | **VACANT** — see XVII.4 and CMG-OQ-02 |
| T1M | **Meta-Constitutional Authority** | This instrument — recognition of constitutionality; orthogonal to T1, not superior to it | Located (PROVISIONAL) |
| T2 | **Program Authority** | CEP-000 and its derived CEP instruments; supreme over constitutional-engineering process | Located |
| T2I | **Interpretive Authority** | AUTH-INF-001; binds reading, enacts nothing; orthogonal to T2 | Located |
| T3 | **Domain Authority** | Domain constitutions governing substance within one domain | Located |
| T4 | **Execution Authority** | Agents and engines acting within a gate | Located |
| T5 | **Derived-Truth Authority** | State, checkpoints, evidence, reports, registries, projections — assert nothing | Located |

XVI.3 The tier ordering SHALL be read as: T0 informs but does not bind as law; T1 binds substance and is presently vacant; T1M binds recognition; T2 binds process; T2I binds reading; T3 binds domains; T4 acts; T5 records. **Every tier SHALL be subordinate to every tier above it, except where an orthogonality is declared in XVI.4.**

XVI.4 Declared orthogonalities SHALL be exactly: **T1M ⟂ T2** (recognition versus process, per VII.5); **T2I ⟂ T2** and **T2I ⟂ T1M** (reading versus enactment, per AUTH-INF-001's own precedence declaration). An orthogonal pair has no relative rank and SHALL NOT be resolved by rank; conflicts between orthogonal instruments resolve by jurisdiction under Article XVII.5.

XVI.5 The lattice SHALL be consistent with, and SHALL NOT amend, the precedence statements already declared in the corpus: the four-tier Program authority order of CEP-000 §5.5; the conflict ladder of CEP-002 Article 23.2 (CEP-000 over CEP-001 over CEP-002 over subordinate governance instruments, and among equals the earliest ratified prevailing); the per-domain subordination chains declared in the front matter of CEP-003 through CEP-010; and the located precedence chain declared for the control-tower meta-instruments. Each of those statements IS a **refinement within a tier** of this lattice and remains fully in force.

XVI.6 Within a tier, ranking SHALL be decided by, in order: (a) an explicit subordination declared by the lower instrument; (b) the earliest ratified definition prevailing, per CEP-002 Article 23.2; (c) declared orthogonality; (d) escalation as an open question under Article LVII. Rank SHALL NOT be decided by recency, by document size, by program prominence, or by any property not enumerated here.

XVI.7 The lattice SHALL be acyclic (CMG-INV-05) and SHALL be verified acyclic on every change by topological sort in the validator of Article L.

XVI.8 The tier set IS OPEN. A new tier SHALL be admitted under Article LXXVI by declaring its position relative to existing tiers, and admission SHALL NOT renumber an existing tier. Tier identifiers are therefore symbolic (T0, T1, T1M, T2, T2I, …) and deliberately non-consecutive, so that insertion never forces renumbering.

## ARTICLE XVII — AUTHORITY RESOLUTION

XVII.1 **Authority resolution** IS the deterministic procedure that answers "which instrument governs this question". It SHALL be applied in the following order, and the first step that yields an answer SHALL terminate the procedure:

XVII.2 Step 1 — **Concern lookup.** Identify the Concern the question belongs to and resolve its Owner from the Registry. If exactly one Owner resolves, that instrument governs and resolution terminates.

XVII.3 Step 2 — **Jurisdiction test.** If no Concern matches, determine whether the question is meta-constitutional (IV.4) or substantive (IV.5). A meta-constitutional question with no Owner falls to this instrument. A substantive question with no Owner IS an orphan and SHALL be recorded as a gap under Article LXXVIII; it SHALL NOT be answered by this instrument.

XVII.4 Step 3 — **Vacancy handling.** If resolution reaches a tier that is VACANT, the procedure SHALL NOT skip the tier and SHALL NOT promote a lower instrument into it. It SHALL record a vacancy finding and treat every dependent determination as PROVISIONAL under CMG-L-12. The closure procedure for a vacancy SHALL be: (a) record the vacancy in the Registry; (b) record the dependent determinations rendered provisional; (c) refer the identification of the occupying authority to explicit ratification as an open question; (d) re-run resolution when the vacancy closes. T1 IS presently VACANT and is recorded as CMG-OQ-02.

XVII.5 Step 4 — **Orthogonality resolution.** Where two orthogonal instruments (XVI.4) both appear to govern, the question SHALL be decomposed into its meta component, its process component, and its interpretive component, and each component SHALL be routed to its axis owner. If decomposition is impossible, the question IS an open question under Article LVII and SHALL NOT be resolved by rank.

XVII.6 Step 5 — **Rank resolution.** Where two non-orthogonal instruments both govern, the lattice of Article XVI and the intra-tier rules of XVI.6 SHALL decide. The outcome SHALL be exactly one instrument.

XVII.7 Resolution SHALL be **recorded**. Every application of this procedure that produces a non-obvious outcome SHALL be recorded as evidence under CEP-008, so that the same question resolves identically forever after.

XVII.8 Resolution SHALL NEVER be resolved by the instrument seeking authority. An instrument SHALL NOT determine its own jurisdiction in a contested case; contested jurisdiction escalates under CEP-002 Article 12 and is disposed under CEP-002 Article 23.

## ARTICLE XVIII — AUTHORITY DELEGATION

XVIII.1 **Delegation** under this instrument IS recognition, not transfer (IV.7). This instrument holds no substantive authority and therefore has none to transfer. Every entry of Article LXXXII SHALL be read as "this concern is already owned by X, and this instrument binds to X".

XVIII.2 A delegation record SHALL carry exactly: delegation identifier, concern, owner, basis (the owner's own clause that establishes its ownership), disposition (EXTEND or REUSE), and revocation condition. A record missing any element IS void (CMG-L-07).

XVIII.3 Delegation SHALL flow **downward only** and SHALL NOT exceed the delegator's own power. This restates no new rule; it binds this instrument to the delegation model already owned by CEP-002 Article 13, which governs delegation operation in full.

XVIII.4 A delegate SHALL NOT sub-delegate beyond the recorded bounds, and ultimate accountability SHALL NOT be delegated. Both rules are owned by CEP-002 Article 13 and are consumed here by reference.

XVIII.5 A delegation SHALL be **revocable only by amendment** of this instrument, and revocation SHALL NOT strip an owner of authority it holds independently. Revocation withdraws the binding, not the ownership.

XVIII.6 A delegation to an **unlocated** owner IS void and SHALL be converted into a vacancy under XVII.4. This is the mechanism by which the corpus's dangling superior references become visible rather than inert.

XVIII.7 Delegation SHALL be **total over the residue**. Every concern this instrument identifies SHALL be either delegated or retained; a concern that is neither IS a gap under CMG-INV-03 and blocks certification under Article LXXX.

## ARTICLE XX — OWNERSHIP MODEL

XX.1 **Ownership** IS the exclusive authority over one Concern, together with the duty to keep that Concern completely and correctly governed. Ownership IS singular (CMG-INV-02) and total over its Concern (CMG-INV-03).

XX.2 An Owner SHALL be a **constitutional artifact**, never a person, team, role, organization, or agent. Persons and agents hold stewardship (Article XXI), accountability (Article XXII), and responsibility (Article XXIII), never ownership. This separation is what allows the corpus to survive unbounded change in its human and machine population.

XX.3 Ownership SHALL be **recorded** in the Registry and SHALL be discoverable without reading the owning artifact. Undiscoverable ownership IS equivalent to no ownership.

XX.4 Ownership SHALL be **acquired** only by (a) constitutive declaration in the artifact itself plus registry admission, or (b) transfer by amendment under Article XLII. Ownership SHALL NOT be acquired by usage, precedent, proximity, or assertion.

XX.5 Ownership SHALL be **transferable** only by an amendment that names the losing owner, the gaining owner, the concern, and the lineage. A transfer that leaves the concern momentarily unowned IS PROHIBITED; transfer IS atomic.

XX.6 Ownership SHALL be **relinquishable** only into another owner or into a recorded gap. An owner SHALL NOT abandon a concern silently.

XX.7 Where the corpus already records ownership — in the canonical ownership matrix, the concept-ownership register, the canonical-home register, and the artifact registry — those records ARE the located ownership facts. This instrument SHALL consume them and SHALL NOT restate them. Where they disagree with each other, the disagreement IS a finding under Article LII and SHALL be disposed by the owner of the affected concern, never by this instrument.

XX.8 The repository's `AUTHORITY = NONE (DERIVED TRUTH)` convention SHALL be read as declaring **no ownership**, not as declaring **ownership of nothing**. A derived-truth artifact owns no concern and SHALL NOT appear as an Owner in the Registry.

## ARTICLE XXI — STEWARDSHIP MODEL

XXI.1 **Stewardship** IS the delegated custody of an owned concern: the duty to maintain, review, and advance the owning artifact without holding its authority.

XXI.2 A Steward MAY be a person, role, team, agent, or automated process. The Steward set IS OPEN with respect to the nature of the steward, because a finite assumption about who or what may steward would fail on the first non-human steward.

XXI.3 Stewardship SHALL be **bounded, recorded, and revocable**, and SHALL NOT exceed the bounds recorded by the delegator. This binds to the delegation model owned by CEP-002 Article 13.

XXI.4 A Steward SHALL NOT amend the scope, authority, or conflict rule of the artifact it stewards. Those are ownership acts and require amendment under Article XLIII.

XXI.5 A concern MAY have multiple Stewards. Multiple stewardship IS permitted precisely because stewardship is not authority; multiple ownership remains prohibited.

XXI.6 An artifact with an Owner and no Steward IS **unattended**. Unattended IS a recordable finding, not a defect: an artifact may legitimately be frozen and unattended. An artifact that is unattended and **not** frozen IS a defect under Article LII.

XXI.7 Stewardship SHALL survive the disappearance of any individual steward. Continuity of stewardship is owned by Article LXXV.

## ARTICLE XXII — ACCOUNTABILITY MODEL

XXII.1 **Accountability** IS the non-transferable answerability for the constitutional correctness of an owned concern.

XXII.2 Accountability SHALL be **singular** per concern and SHALL NOT be delegated, shared, or diffused. Delegation of custody (Article XXI) does not delegate accountability; this restates CEP-002 Article 13.3 and adds nothing.

XXII.3 Accountability SHALL attach to the **authority that allocated the ownership**, not to the steward who performs the work. A steward's failure IS the allocating authority's accountability.

XXII.4 Accountability SHALL be **traceable**: for any constitutional defect, the accountable authority SHALL be computable from the Registry without interpretation.

XXII.5 Where accountability would attach to a **vacant** tier, the dependent determinations SHALL be PROVISIONAL (CMG-L-12) and the accountability SHALL be recorded as unassigned. An unassigned accountability IS a recorded fact and blocks final certification under Article LXXX; it SHALL NOT be assigned by default to the nearest located authority, because default assignment is a form of self-elevation.

XXII.6 Accountability SHALL NOT be discharged by process compliance alone. Passing every gate does not discharge accountability for a substantively wrong constitutional allocation.

---

## ARTICLE XXIII — RESPONSIBILITY MODEL

XXIII.1 **Responsibility** IS the assigned duty to perform a specific constitutional act. Responsibility IS plural, transferable, and always bounded by a named act.

XXIII.2 The distinction across Articles XX–XXIII SHALL be read as: **Ownership** = who governs the concern (an artifact); **Stewardship** = who cares for the artifact (any entity); **Accountability** = who answers for correctness (the allocating authority, singular, non-transferable); **Responsibility** = who performs a named act (any entity, plural, transferable). The four SHALL NOT be conflated, and conflation IS the most common cause of orphan governance.

XXIII.3 Every constitutional act that this instrument requires SHALL have a named responsible role, and the role SHALL be defined by function, never by identity. Naming a person or a product as responsible in constitutional law IS PROHIBITED (CMG-L-09).

XXIII.4 Responsibility SHALL be **assignable to automation**. Where an act is deterministic, responsibility SHOULD be assigned to an automated check rather than to a human, so that the act cannot be skipped. The automation binding is Article LXVI.

XXIII.5 An act with no responsible role IS an **unperformable obligation** and IS a defect under Article LII, because a constitution that requires what no one is responsible for is undecidable.

## ARTICLE XXIV — CONSTITUTION LIFECYCLE

XXIV.1 Every constitutional artifact SHALL traverse a lifecycle, and its position in that lifecycle SHALL be a recorded fact rather than an inference from its content.

XXIV.2 This instrument SHALL NOT create a lifecycle where one exists. The **operative lifecycle** of constitutional artifacts IS the artifact state model owned by CEP-001 Article VIII, refined per domain by CEP-003 through CEP-010. This Article and Articles XXV–XXVII SHALL be read as a **meta-lifecycle view**: the minimum set of lifecycle facts the Registry SHALL record, expressed so that it maps onto the located model without displacing it.

XXIV.3 The meta-lifecycle SHALL have exactly three phases: **PRE-EFFECT** (the artifact does not yet bind), **IN-EFFECT** (the artifact binds), and **POST-EFFECT** (the artifact no longer binds but is preserved). Every located state SHALL map to exactly one phase, and the mapping IS recorded in XXV.3.

XXIV.4 Constitutional force SHALL exist in the IN-EFFECT phase only. An artifact in PRE-EFFECT SHALL NOT be cited as authority, and an artifact in POST-EFFECT SHALL be citable only as history.

XXIV.5 Lifecycle position SHALL never be reversed by editing. Movement toward PRE-EFFECT from IN-EFFECT IS PROHIBITED; an artifact that should not have taken effect proceeds to POST-EFFECT by supersession or retirement, preserving the record (CMG-L-11).

XXIV.6 The lifecycle SHALL admit an unbounded number of future states without amendment of the phase model, because the phase model is defined by binding force rather than by an enumeration of states.

| CMG-T-08 | RATIFIED → FROZEN | A baseline is declared under the freeze owner |
| CMG-T-09 | PROVISIONAL → FROZEN | Permitted; the freeze preserves provisional standing and SHALL NOT upgrade it |
| CMG-T-10 | RATIFIED → AMENDING | A change instrument is admitted |
| CMG-T-11 | FROZEN → AMENDING | Permitted only by the freeze owner's supersession path, never by mutation |
| CMG-T-12 | AMENDING → RATIFIED | The amendment completes and is accepted |
| CMG-T-13 | AMENDING → PROVISIONAL | The amendment completes with no located acceptance authority |
| CMG-T-14 | any IN-EFFECT → DEPRECATED | A cessation schedule is recorded |
| CMG-T-15 | DEPRECATED → SUPERSEDED | A successor with lineage takes effect |
| CMG-T-16 | any IN-EFFECT → SUPERSEDED | A successor with lineage takes effect without prior deprecation |
| CMG-T-17 | DEPRECATED → RETIRED | Cessation completes with no successor |
| CMG-T-18 | SUPERSEDED → ARCHIVED | Preservation moves the record out of the working corpus |
| CMG-T-19 | RETIRED → ARCHIVED | Preservation moves the record out of the working corpus |
| CMG-T-20 | ARCHIVED → REGISTERED | Restoration; a restored artifact re-enters PRE-EFFECT and SHALL re-validate |
| CMG-T-21 | any PRE-EFFECT → VOID | Admission refused, or the artifact is determined never to have bound |
| CMG-T-22 | REGISTERED → DECLARED | Admission withdrawn before effect; identity is retained, never reused |

XXVI.2 **Any transition not enumerated in XXVI.1 IS PROHIBITED.** This mirrors the located model's treatment of unenumerated transitions and is stated here so that the meta-lifecycle is equally decidable.

XXVI.3 CMG-T-20 (restoration) SHALL NOT restore effect directly. A restored artifact re-enters the PRE-EFFECT phase and SHALL traverse validation and certification again, because the corpus it must be consistent with has changed. Direct restoration to IN-EFFECT IS PROHIBITED.

XXVI.4 CMG-T-09 exists to make an important rule explicit: **freezing does not confer standing**. A provisional artifact that is frozen remains provisional. Freeze preserves; it does not ratify.

XXVI.5 Every transition SHALL be **evidenced** (CMG-L-10) and **atomic**. A transition that leaves the artifact in no state, or in two states, IS a defect.

XXVI.6 No transition SHALL delete or mutate the artifact's prior recorded states. The state history IS append-only.

## ARTICLE XXVII — LIFECYCLE GOVERNANCE

XXVII.1 Lifecycle **operation** — who executes a transition, in what order, under what authorization, with what recovery — IS owned by the located execution, validation, certification, ratification, freeze, and evolution constitutions and IS NOT governed here.

XXVII.2 This instrument SHALL govern only three lifecycle questions, all meta: (a) whether a recorded state is a legal state; (b) whether a recorded transition is a legal transition; (c) whether the artifact's phase is consistent with the constitutional force it is exercising.

XXVII.3 A **phase-force inconsistency** — an artifact exercising force outside the IN-EFFECT phase, or refusing force inside it — IS the single lifecycle defect this instrument owns, and it SHALL block certification under Article LXXX.

XXVII.4 Lifecycle governance SHALL be **fail-closed**. Where the phase of an artifact cannot be determined, the artifact SHALL be treated as PRE-EFFECT and SHALL NOT be cited as authority.

XXVII.5 A lifecycle state SHALL NOT be inferred from a filename, a directory, a commit, or a status string alone. It SHALL be resolved from the artifact's declaration reconciled against the located registries, and irreconcilable inputs produce a finding rather than a guess.

## ARTICLE XXVIII — VERSIONING MODEL

XXVIII.1 Every constitutional artifact SHALL carry exactly one explicit version, and the version SHALL be a recorded fact in the artifact and in the Registry.

XXVIII.2 A version SHALL change **only by amendment** (Article XLIII). Editorial change without a version increment IS PROHIBITED, because it makes two different texts indistinguishable by reference.

XXVIII.3 A version SHALL be **immutable once frozen**, and a frozen version SHALL be content-addressed so that its identity is derivable from its content.

XXVIII.4 A superseded version SHALL be **retained**, never deleted, and SHALL be reachable from its successor by an explicit lineage link (CMG-INV-11).

XXVIII.5 A successor version SHALL be a **new artifact identity** where the successor is a different constitution, and a **new version of the same identity** where the successor is the same constitution evolved. The distinction SHALL be recorded, and the rule for choosing between them is owned by the located evolution constitution and consumed here by reference.

XXVIII.6 Every subordinate instrument SHALL cite the version of every superior instrument under which it was produced. A subordinate that cites no superior version cannot be checked for consistency and IS a defect.

XXVIII.7 Version lineage SHALL be **acyclic and append-only** (CMG-INV-05, CMG-INV-11).

## ARTICLE XXIX — SEMANTIC VERSIONING

XXIX.1 Constitutional versions SHALL be expressed as `MAJOR.MINOR` at minimum and MAY be expressed as `MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH`. The present version of every located CEP instrument is `1.0`, and this instrument adopts the same form for consistency.

XXIX.2 The semantics SHALL be defined by **effect on subjects**, not by size of edit:
- **MAJOR** — a change that alters, removes, or narrows an existing obligation, prohibition, authority, scope, identity rule, or state model; any change that could invalidate a conforming subject. MAJOR IS a breaking change.
- **MINOR** — a change that adds an obligation, kind, state, relationship, principle, law, delegation, or clarification without invalidating any conforming subject. MINOR IS additive and backward compatible.
- **PATCH** — a change that alters no normative meaning: typography, cross-reference correction, formatting. PATCH SHALL NOT change any obligation.

XXIX.3 Classification of a change SHALL be **conservative**: where a change could plausibly be MAJOR, it IS MAJOR. Under-classification is a constitutional defect; over-classification is merely costly.

XXIX.4 A MAJOR change SHALL require the full ratification path of Article XLIV and SHALL NOT be admitted as an addendum. A MINOR change MAY be admitted as an additive addendum where the located evolution constitution permits additive amendment, which IS the mechanism this corpus already uses.

XXIX.5 The version of this instrument SHALL NOT be incremented to reflect changes in the artifacts it registers. Registry content changes are recorded in the derived Registry, not in this instrument's version. Conflating the two would force perpetual amendment of the meta layer and is prohibited.

XXIX.6 Version comparison SHALL be total and deterministic, and SHALL NOT depend on release date, ordering of commits, or filesystem metadata.

## ARTICLE XXX — CONSTITUTION IDENTITY

XXX.1 Every constitutional artifact SHALL have exactly one identity, and that identity SHALL be **permanent, unique, non-reassignable, and independent of location**.

XXX.2 Identity SHALL NOT be derived from a file path, directory, repository, program, phase, technology, or storage location. An identity that changes when a file moves is not an identity.

XXX.3 An artifact without an identity SHALL be deemed **non-existent** for constitutional purposes. This restates the located rule for evidence identity and generalizes it to all constitutional artifacts.

XXX.4 Identity SHALL be **allocated once and never reused**, including after retirement, archival, or voiding. A retired identifier SHALL NOT be reissued to a different artifact under any circumstance.

XXX.5 Identity allocation SHALL be recorded in an **append-only ledger**. The corpus already operates such a ledger for universal identifiers; this instrument SHALL bind to it and SHALL NOT create a second one (CMG-L-14).

XXX.6 An artifact MAY carry both a **native identifier** (its own program identifier, e.g. `CMG-000001`) and a **universal identifier** (allocated by the located identity authority). Both SHALL resolve to the same artifact, and neither SHALL be treated as an alias of convenience.

XXX.7 A successor artifact SHALL take a **new identity** and SHALL link to its predecessor by explicit lineage; it SHALL NOT reuse, overwrite, or inherit the predecessor's identity. This is owned by the located evolution constitution and is consumed here by reference.

XXX.8 Identity SHALL be **content-verifiable**: every artifact SHALL carry a content hash so that a claimed identity can be checked against the text it names. The corpus already records content hashes for registered artifacts; this instrument binds to that mechanism.

## ARTICLE XXXI — IDENTIFIER RULES

XXXI.1 A constitutional identifier SHALL match the grammar `<NAMESPACE>-<SEQUENCE>` where NAMESPACE is an uppercase alphanumeric namespace token and SEQUENCE is a zero-padded decimal ordinal. Additional qualified forms `<NAMESPACE>-<SUBSERIES>-<SEQUENCE>` ARE permitted.

XXXI.2 Sequence width SHALL be **declared per namespace** and SHALL NOT be assumed globally. The corpus presently uses widths of two, three, four, and six digits across different namespaces; a single global width would be a finite assumption and is prohibited.

XXXI.3 Sequence width SHALL be chosen so that the namespace cannot exhaust. Where a namespace is expected to grow without bound, the width SHALL be at least six digits, and width extension by left-padding SHALL be permitted without renumbering, because `CMG-000001` and `CMG-0000001` denote the same ordinal under XXXI.4.

XXXI.4 Identifier comparison SHALL be by **namespace token and numeric ordinal**, never by string. This makes zero-padding a presentation choice and removes any ceiling created by a chosen width.

XXXI.5 An identifier SHALL NOT encode meaning beyond namespace and ordinal. Encoding phase, kind, status, owner, or version into an identifier couples identity to mutable fact and IS PROHIBITED.

XXXI.6 The `CMG` namespace IS hereby established with sequence width six, and `CMG-000001` IS allocated to this instrument. Width six follows the located six-digit precedent already used by the corpus's master knowledge book identifier, and is chosen so that the meta namespace cannot exhaust.

XXXI.7 Identifier allocation SHALL be **monotonic and gapless-by-intent but gap-tolerant**: a skipped ordinal IS permitted and SHALL NOT be backfilled, because backfilling would reuse an ordinal that a superseded record may reference.

XXXI.8 Every identifier SHALL be resolvable to a canonical home path by the Registry, and a non-resolvable identifier IS a defect under Article LXXIX.

## ARTICLE XXXII — NAMING STANDARDS

XXXII.1 The canonical filename of a constitutional artifact SHALL be `<IDENTIFIER>-<UPPER-KEBAB-TITLE>.<ext>`, and the title portion SHALL describe the artifact's Kind and subject.

XXXII.2 The document title line SHALL be `# UCOS Ω∞ — <TITLE IN CAPITALS>` and SHALL match the filename title portion in substance.

XXXII.3 Names SHALL be **descriptive, stable, and non-versioned**. A version SHALL NOT appear in a filename, because the version is a recorded fact and duplicating it in the name creates two sources of truth.

XXXII.4 Names SHALL NOT encode status, ownership, phase, or technology. A renamed file SHALL NOT change identity (XXX.2), and a status change SHALL NOT require a rename.

XXXII.5 Names SHALL be **unique across the corpus**, and two artifacts SHALL NOT share a canonical filename even in different directories, so that a name is sufficient to cite.

XXXII.6 Naming SHALL be **script-neutral in identity and expressive in title**. Identifiers SHALL use ASCII uppercase alphanumerics and hyphen only, so that they are safe in every filesystem, URL, database key, and identifier system; titles MAY use the corpus's Ω∞ mark and any other character the corpus already uses.

XXXII.7 A name SHALL NOT be reused after retirement where reuse would make two distinct artifacts indistinguishable in citation history.

## ARTICLE XXXIII — NAMESPACE STANDARDS

XXXIII.1 A **namespace** IS the authority boundary within which identifier ordinals are unique. Every identifier SHALL belong to exactly one namespace.

XXXIII.2 A namespace SHALL have exactly one **namespace owner**, which IS the constitutional artifact authorized to allocate within it. Two owners allocating in one namespace would produce duplicate identity and IS PROHIBITED.

XXXIII.3 Namespace allocation SHALL be **recorded before first use**. An identifier issued in an unrecorded namespace IS provisional and SHALL be reconciled or voided.

XXXIII.4 Namespaces SHALL be **hierarchically qualifiable** as `<ROOT>:<SUB>:<LEAF>` for federation, and unqualified identifiers SHALL be interpreted in the local root namespace. This is declared now so that federating two repositories never requires renumbering either.

XXXIII.5 The namespace set IS OPEN. A new namespace SHALL be admitted by recording its token, its owner, its sequence width, and its purpose, and admission SHALL NOT affect any existing namespace.

XXXIII.6 The namespace token `CMG` IS owned by this instrument. This instrument SHALL NOT allocate in any other namespace, and no other instrument SHALL allocate in `CMG`.

XXXIII.7 Prior to this instrument, no located artifact governed the identifier namespace of top-level meta instruments. That vacancy is closed by this Article for the `CMG` namespace only. Every pre-existing namespace of the corpus IS **recognized as legacy-owned by its established owner** and SHALL NOT be renamed, renumbered, reformatted, or brought under `CMG`. Recognition of a legacy namespace IS a registry act with no effect on the namespace itself.

XXXIII.8 A namespace SHALL NOT be retired while any identifier within it is referenced. Namespace retirement follows the preservation rule of CMG-L-11.

## ARTICLE XXXV — DEPENDENCY MODEL

XXXV.1 A **constitutional dependency** exists where an artifact cannot be validated, interpreted, or applied without another artifact. Dependency IS evaluative, not merely referential; citing an artifact for context IS not a dependency.

XXXV.2 The dependency graph over constitutional artifacts SHALL be **directed and acyclic** (CMG-L-06). A cycle IS an undecidability and SHALL halt advancement of every artifact in the cycle until broken.

XXXV.3 A cycle SHALL be broken by exactly one of: (a) extracting the shared concern into a third artifact that both depend on; (b) inverting one dependency into a delegation; (c) declaring one edge orthogonal where the jurisdictions are genuinely disjoint. A cycle SHALL NOT be broken by deleting a declaration that is true.

XXXV.4 Every dependency SHALL be **declared, versioned, and resolvable**. A dependency on an unversioned superior cannot be checked for consistency (XXVIII.6) and IS a defect.

XXXV.5 Dependency SHALL be **transitively closed** for validation purposes: validating an artifact SHALL require its transitive dependency set to be in the IN-EFFECT phase or explicitly waived under Article LV.

XXXV.6 A dependency on a **PRE-EFFECT** artifact SHALL render the dependent PROVISIONAL at most, never RATIFIED. Standing SHALL NOT exceed the standing of the weakest dependency. This rule is why this instrument, depending on a vacant T1, is itself PROVISIONAL.

XXXV.7 A dependency on a **POST-EFFECT** artifact IS a defect and SHALL be remediated by repointing to the successor recorded in lineage, or by recording a waiver under Article LV.

XXXV.8 This instrument SHALL declare **no dependency on any artifact for its own recognition** (VII.6). Its dependencies are consumption dependencies only: it depends on located owners for the domains it delegates, and those owners do not depend on it for their standing.

## ARTICLE XXXVI — IMPACT ANALYSIS

XXXVI.1 Every proposed change to a constitutional artifact SHALL be preceded by an impact analysis, and the analysis SHALL be a recorded artifact rather than a conversation.

XXXVI.2 The impact set SHALL be computed as the **transitive inverse closure** of the changed artifact over CMG-R-01, CMG-R-02, CMG-R-05, CMG-R-06, CMG-R-08, and CMG-R-16. Every artifact in the impact set IS affected until proven otherwise.

XXXVI.3 Impact analysis SHALL classify each affected artifact as **INVALIDATED**, **REQUIRES-REVIEW**, or **UNAFFECTED**, and each classification SHALL state its reason. An unclassified affected artifact blocks the change.

XXXVI.4 Impact analysis SHALL be **computable, not estimated**. Where the relationship declarations are incomplete, the analysis SHALL fail closed and report the incompleteness rather than produce a partial impact set.

XXXVI.5 The **method** of impact assessment for amendments IS owned by the located evolution constitution. This Article SHALL be read as the meta requirement that impact be computed over the relationship graph, and SHALL NOT displace the located method.

XXXVI.6 An impact analysis SHALL cover **non-artifact impact** where it exists: registries, projections, validators, gates, generators, and certifications that consume the changed artifact. Omitting machinery from impact analysis is the most common cause of silent governance drift.

XXXVI.7 Impact analysis SHALL be re-run when the change is modified. A stale impact analysis SHALL NOT satisfy XXXVI.1.

## ARTICLE XXXVII — TRACEABILITY MODEL

XXXVII.1 Every constitutional obligation SHALL be traceable **upward** to the authority that imposes it and **downward** to the artifacts, checks, and realizations that discharge it. An obligation traceable in only one direction IS half-governed.

XXXVII.2 Traceability SHALL be **derived, never authored**. It SHALL be computed from declared relationships (Article XXXIV) and SHALL NOT be maintained as an independent hand-written matrix, because a hand-written matrix is a second source of truth.

XXXVII.3 Traceability SHALL be **closed**: every obligation of every recognized artifact SHALL appear in the trace, and every trace entry SHALL resolve to a located artifact. Open ends are findings under Article LII.

XXXVII.4 The **traceability store and spine** of this corpus are located and owned; this instrument SHALL bind to them and SHALL NOT create a parallel store (CMG-L-14). The meta requirement here is the completeness property, not the storage mechanism.

XXXVII.5 Traceability SHALL survive supersession: a trace that passed through a superseded artifact SHALL be repointed to the successor and the historical trace SHALL be preserved. Traceability history IS append-only.

XXXVII.6 Traceability SHALL be **reproducible**: recomputing the trace from repository state SHALL yield an identical result. A non-reproducible trace IS not evidence.

XXXVII.7 Traceability SHALL extend across **federation boundaries** by namespace-qualified reference (XXXIII.4). A trace that terminates at a repository boundary IS incomplete, and the incompleteness SHALL be recorded rather than tolerated silently.

---

## ARTICLE XXXVIII — COMPOSITION RULES

XXXVIII.1 **Composition** IS the assembly of a constitutional whole from parts that retain their own identity, authority, and lifecycle. Composition SHALL NOT merge identities.

XXXVIII.2 A composite SHALL NOT acquire authority its parts do not hold, and SHALL NOT strip a part of authority it holds. Composition IS authority-neutral.

XXXVIII.3 A part SHALL be composable into an unbounded number of composites. Exclusive composition would make reuse impossible and IS PROHIBITED.

XXXVIII.4 A composite SHALL declare its parts explicitly, by identity and version. Implicit composition by directory, proximity, or naming pattern IS PROHIBITED.

XXXVIII.5 Composition SHALL be **acyclic**: a composite SHALL NOT be a part of itself transitively.

XXXVIII.6 The lifecycle of a composite SHALL be **bounded by its parts**: a composite SHALL NOT reach a phase stronger than the weakest phase of its parts (consistent with XXXV.6).

XXXVIII.7 Removal of a part from a composite IS a MAJOR change under XXIX.2, because it narrows the composite's obligations.

---

## ARTICLE XXXIX — INHERITANCE RULES

XXXIX.1 **Inheritance** IS the adoption by a subordinate artifact of the obligations and prohibitions of a superior, without restatement.

XXXIX.2 Inheritance SHALL be **by reference only**. Restating an inherited obligation IS duplication and violates Knowledge Once (VIII.1); the restatement will drift.

XXXIX.3 An inheritor SHALL NOT **weaken** an inherited obligation, **remove** an inherited prohibition, or **broaden** an inherited scope. An inheritor MAY **strengthen** an obligation and **narrow** a scope.

XXXIX.4 An inheritor SHALL NOT inherit **authority**. Authority is allocated, not inherited; an inheritor of obligations remains without authority unless separately allocated.

XXXIX.5 Multiple inheritance IS permitted and SHALL be resolved by the precedence lattice where inherited obligations conflict. Where the lattice yields orthogonality, the conflict IS an open question under Article LVII and SHALL NOT be resolved by inheritance order.

XXXIX.6 Inheritance SHALL be **explicit**: an artifact SHALL declare what it inherits from. Implicit inheritance from a containing program, phase, or directory IS PROHIBITED.

XXXIX.7 An inherited obligation SHALL be re-evaluated when the superior is amended. Inheritance IS live, not a snapshot.

---

## ARTICLE XL — REUSE RULES

XL.1 **Reuse** IS the binding of an existing constitutional artifact into a new context without copying it. Reuse IS the default; copying IS the exception and requires justification.

XL.2 Copying constitutional text into a second artifact IS PROHIBITED except where the copy is (a) explicitly marked as non-normative illustration, or (b) a preserved historical record. A normative copy creates parallel authority.

XL.3 Reuse SHALL be by **identity and version reference**. Reuse by paraphrase IS PROHIBITED, because a paraphrase cannot be verified equal to its source.

XL.4 Reuse SHALL NOT alter the reused artifact. Where alteration is required, the correct operations are EXTEND (Article XLI), AMEND (Article XLII/XLIII), or SUPERSEDE (Article LXXII).

XL.5 Before any new constitutional artifact is created, a **reuse discovery** SHALL be performed and recorded: whether an existing owner covers the concern, and if so why extension was insufficient. Creation without a recorded reuse discovery IS a defect.

XL.6 Reuse SHALL be **counted**: the Registry SHALL record which artifacts reuse which, so that the impact of a change is computable (Article XXXVI).

XL.7 Reuse across **federations and universes** SHALL be permitted by namespace-qualified reference. No clause of this Article assumes a single repository or a single universe.

---

## ARTICLE XLI — EXTENSION RULES

XLI.1 **Extension** IS the addition of new obligation, kind, state, relationship, delegation, or coverage to an existing artifact without altering any existing clause.

XLI.2 An extension SHALL be **strictly additive**. It SHALL NOT modify, weaken, reorder, renumber, or remove any existing clause, identifier, state, transition, or enumeration member. An extension that does any of these IS a modification (Article XLII), not an extension.

XLI.3 An extension SHALL be **appended**, and SHALL declare its provenance: what it extends, on what basis, under which amendment classification, and what it does not change. The corpus already uses an addendum form for exactly this purpose; extensions SHALL follow it.

XLI.4 An extension SHALL be **void to the extent of any conflict** with the artifact it extends or with any superior. Void-on-conflict IS declared in the extension itself, so that a defective extension cannot silently override.

XLI.5 An extension SHALL NOT introduce a new authority, a new registry, a new lifecycle, or a new namespace. Those require a new artifact under Article LXXVI.

XLI.6 Extension IS a MINOR change under XXIX.2 and SHALL increment the MINOR version.

XLI.7 Extension SHALL be preferred over creation, and creation SHALL be preferred over modification of frozen text. This ordering IS the operational form of VIII.2.

---

## ARTICLE XLII — MODIFICATION RULES

XLII.1 **Modification** IS any change to an existing clause of a constitutional artifact. Modification IS the most dangerous constitutional operation and SHALL be the most constrained.

XLII.2 Modification SHALL proceed **only** through the amendment framework (Article XLIII). Direct editing of a constitutional artifact outside an amendment IS PROHIBITED regardless of how small the change appears.

XLII.3 Modification of a **FROZEN** artifact IS PROHIBITED absolutely. The only path is supersession with lineage under Article LXXII, which produces a new artifact and preserves the frozen text.

XLII.4 Modification SHALL preserve **identifier stability**: existing clause identifiers, law identifiers, invariant identifiers, state identifiers, and transition identifiers SHALL NOT be renumbered, reused, or repurposed. A removed member's identifier SHALL be recorded as withdrawn and SHALL NOT be reissued.

XLII.5 Modification that narrows or removes an obligation IS a MAJOR change (XXIX.2) and requires the full ratification path.

XLII.6 Modification SHALL be accompanied by an impact analysis (Article XXXVI) and SHALL NOT proceed while any affected artifact is unclassified.

XLII.7 Modification of an artifact's **authority, positive scope, negative scope, or conflict rule** IS a jurisdictional change and SHALL require, in addition to amendment, a re-verification of CMG-INV-02 (no parallel authority) and CMG-INV-03 (no orphan governance) across the whole corpus.

XLII.8 Modification SHALL NOT be used to correct a jurisdiction breach in this instrument. A breaching clause IS void under CMG-L-13 by operation of law, and the amendment merely records the void.

---

## ARTICLE XLIII — AMENDMENT FRAMEWORK

XLIII.1 The **amendment mechanism** of this corpus IS located and owned. This instrument SHALL NOT create a second amendment mechanism, a second change classification, a second impact method, a second successor model, or a second evolution registry (CMG-L-14). The located owner of amendment and evolution operation IS recorded in Article LXXXII as CMG-DLG-09.

XLIII.2 This Article SHALL state only the **meta requirements** that an amendment to a constitutional artifact SHALL satisfy, in addition to whatever the located amendment owner requires:
(a) the amendment declares its change classification under XXIX.2;
(b) the amendment declares what it does **not** change;
(c) the amendment carries an impact analysis under Article XXXVI;
(d) the amendment preserves identifier stability under XLII.4;
(e) the amendment declares void-on-conflict against every superior;
(f) the amendment updates the Registry, the delegation register, traceability, and dependency records in the same change;
(g) the amendment leaves CMG-INV-01 through CMG-INV-12 satisfied.

XLIII.3 An amendment that satisfies the located mechanism but violates XLIII.2 IS **procedurally valid and constitutionally defective**, and SHALL be recorded as a finding under Article LII rather than silently accepted.

XLIII.4 Amendment of **this instrument** SHALL additionally require that the amendment not expand this instrument's jurisdiction (CMG-L-04, XIX.4). A jurisdiction-expanding amendment to the meta layer IS PROHIBITED, and the correct instrument for a genuinely new meta concern IS a new artifact admitted under Article LXXVI.

XLIII.5 Amendment SHALL NOT be used where extension suffices (XLI.7), and SHALL NOT be used where the change is properly a supersession (Article LXXII).

XLIII.6 The **immutable ground** of this instrument SHALL be Article XI (the invariants) and this Article. They ARE amendable, but an amendment to either SHALL require the full MAJOR path and SHALL state explicitly which invariant it alters and why the corpus remains checkable without it.

---

## ARTICLE XLIV — RATIFICATION FRAMEWORK

XLIV.1 **Ratification** IS the single constitutional act by which a validated and certified artifact becomes an accepted member of the corpus. Ratification operation IS located and owned, and IS recorded in Article LXXXII as CMG-DLG-06. This instrument SHALL NOT ratify anything.

XLIV.2 This Article SHALL state only the **meta requirements** on ratification: that the ratifying authority be **located**; that it be **competent** for the artifact's tier; that acceptance be **recorded** with evidence; and that standing conferred never exceed the standing the ratifier holds (CMG-L-12).

XLIV.3 Where no located competent ratifying authority exists for a tier, ratification at that tier SHALL yield **PROVISIONAL** standing only, and the vacancy SHALL be recorded under XVII.4. This is not a workaround; it is the honest recording of an absent authority.

XLIV.4 The corpus presently records that no ratification authority exists within the frozen corpus, and the identity of any out-of-corpus finality authority is undetermined. This instrument therefore SHALL NOT claim ratified standing, SHALL operate PROVISIONALLY, and SHALL record the identification of the competent authority as CMG-OQ-01 and CMG-OQ-02 in Article LXXVIII.

XLIV.5 Ratification SHALL NOT be inferred from certification, freeze, publication, registration, age, use, or absence of objection. Each of those is a distinct act with a distinct owner, and none confers acceptance.

XLIV.6 A ratification SHALL be **revocable only by supersession or retirement**, never by silent withdrawal. Revocation SHALL preserve the record of the prior ratification.

XLIV.7 Self-ratification IS PROHIBITED. No artifact, agent, or program SHALL ratify itself, and no authority SHALL grant itself ratification competence.

## ARTICLE XLV — REVIEW FRAMEWORK

XLV.1 **Review** IS the examination of a constitutional artifact against its obligations, prior to validation. Review IS advisory in outcome and mandatory in occurrence.

XLV.2 Every constitutional artifact SHALL be reviewed before it enters validation, and the review SHALL be recorded. An unreviewed artifact SHALL NOT be validated.

XLV.3 Review SHALL examine, at minimum: authority declaration, positive and negative scope, conflict rule, jurisdiction non-overlap against every existing owner, dependency declaration and acyclicity, identifier conformance, decidability of every obligation, and reuse discovery under XL.5.

XLV.4 Review SHALL be performed by an entity **other than the author**. Self-review SHALL NOT satisfy XLV.2. Where the author is an automated process, the reviewer MAY be a different automated process, provided its criteria are independently declared.

XLV.5 Review findings SHALL be **dispositioned**, not merely recorded. Each finding SHALL be accepted, rejected with reason, or deferred into the deferral record. An undispositioned finding blocks validation.

XLV.6 Review SHALL be **repeatable**: the same artifact against the same criteria SHALL yield the same findings. Non-repeatable review IS opinion and SHALL NOT gate anything.

XLV.7 The **method** of engineering and constitutional review is owned elsewhere in the corpus; this Article states the meta requirement that review occur, be independent, and be dispositioned, and SHALL NOT displace the located method.

## ARTICLE XLVI — APPROVAL FRAMEWORK

XLVI.1 **Approval** IS the recorded assent of a competent authority to a specific act. Approval IS narrower than ratification: it authorizes an act, it does not admit an artifact.

XLVI.2 Every constitutional act that changes authority, scope, ownership, precedence, namespace, or identity SHALL require explicit approval by the authority whose allocation it affects.

XLVI.3 Approval SHALL be **specific**: it SHALL name the act, the subject, the version, and the conditions. Blanket or standing approval for a class of future acts IS PROHIBITED, because it is indistinguishable from delegation of accountability (XXII.2).

XLVI.4 Approval SHALL be **recorded before the act**, never reconstructed after it. Retrospective approval SHALL be recorded as an exception under Article LV, with its own justification.

XLVI.5 Absence of objection SHALL NOT constitute approval. Silence IS not assent in constitutional matter.

XLVI.6 Approval SHALL NOT be granted by the entity performing the act. Where the acting and approving entities coincide, the act SHALL escalate one level under the located escalation model.

XLVI.7 An approval SHALL expire when its stated conditions change. An approval granted against a superseded version IS void.

## ARTICLE XLVII — PUBLICATION FRAMEWORK

XLVII.1 **Publication** IS the act of making a constitutional artifact discoverable and citable within its reach. An artifact that binds subjects who cannot discover it IS unenforceable, and unenforceable law IS a defect.

XLVII.2 Publication SHALL be a **precondition of effect**: an artifact SHALL NOT be IN-EFFECT unless it is published within its declared reach.

XLVII.3 Publication SHALL be to the **canonical home** recorded in the Registry, and the canonical home SHALL be a single location. Multiple canonical homes for one artifact IS a duplication defect.

XLVII.4 Publication SHALL preserve **citability**: a citation to an artifact and version SHALL resolve permanently. Broken citations are governance failures, not link rot.

XLVII.5 Publication SHALL NOT alter content. Rendering, formatting, indexing, and presentation are derived and non-authoritative; where a rendered form differs from the canonical form, the canonical form governs.

XLVII.6 Publication of a **PRE-EFFECT** artifact IS permitted and SHALL be marked with its state, so that a reader cannot mistake a draft for law.

XLVII.7 Publication mechanisms, portals, renderings, and indexes are located and owned in this corpus; this Article states the meta requirement and SHALL NOT specify a mechanism or a technology (CMG-L-09).

## ARTICLE XLVIII — DISTRIBUTION FRAMEWORK

XLVIII.1 **Distribution** IS the propagation of a constitutional artifact to the contexts that must consume it — other repositories, runtimes, generators, engines, and federated corpora.

XLVIII.2 Distribution SHALL be **by reference wherever possible** and **by content-addressed copy where reference is impossible**. A distributed copy SHALL carry its identity, version, and content hash so that its provenance is verifiable.

XLVIII.3 A distributed copy SHALL be **read-only** at its destination. Editing a distributed copy creates a fork of constitutional law and IS PROHIBITED.

XLVIII.4 Distribution SHALL be **drift-detectable**: every distributed copy SHALL be verifiable against its source by content hash, and a divergence SHALL be a finding under Article LII.

XLVIII.5 Distribution SHALL NOT confer standing. A copy distributed into a context does not become authoritative in that context beyond the standing it holds at source.

XLVIII.6 Distribution across **federation boundaries** SHALL carry namespace qualification (XXXIII.4) so that a distributed artifact cannot be confused with a local artifact of the same ordinal.

XLVIII.7 Distribution SHALL be **revocable**: when an artifact reaches POST-EFFECT, its distributed copies SHALL be marked accordingly. Undetectable stale distribution IS a continuity defect under Article LXXV.

## ARTICLE XLIX — COMPLIANCE FRAMEWORK

XLIX.1 **Compliance** IS the state of a subject conforming to every obligation that binds it. Compliance IS evaluated per subject per obligation, never in aggregate as a sentiment.

XLIX.2 Compliance evaluation SHALL be **decidable**: each obligation SHALL yield COMPLIANT or NON-COMPLIANT. A third outcome IS permitted only as NOT-APPLICABLE with a recorded reason.

XLIX.3 Compliance SHALL be evaluated against the **version in effect at evaluation time**, and the evaluated version SHALL be recorded with the result. A compliance result without a version reference is meaningless.

XLIX.4 **Compliance monitoring and assurance operation** IS located and owned and IS recorded in Article LXXXII as CMG-DLG-10. This instrument SHALL NOT perform compliance monitoring and SHALL NOT create a second compliance engine.

XLIX.5 This instrument SHALL own exactly one compliance concern: **meta-constitutional compliance** — whether each recognized artifact satisfies IV.1, CMG-L-01 through CMG-L-14, and CMG-INV-01 through CMG-INV-12. That evaluation IS realized by the validator of Article L.

XLIX.6 Non-compliance SHALL produce a **finding routed to the owner of the affected concern**, never a determination by the detector. Detection and disposition are separate powers, and this separation is already law in the located audit constitution.

XLIX.7 Compliance SHALL NOT be assumed from the absence of a check. An unchecked obligation IS of unknown compliance and SHALL be recorded as such rather than presumed satisfied.

## ARTICLE L — VALIDATION FRAMEWORK

L.1 **Validation operation** — criteria evaluation, gate semantics, PASS/BLOCKED outcome, remediation, revalidation, and closure — IS located and owned and IS recorded in Article LXXXII as CMG-DLG-04. This instrument SHALL NOT create a second validation authority, gate model, or criteria set.

L.2 This instrument SHALL contribute exactly one validation **subject**: the meta-constitutional conformance of the corpus. The criteria SHALL be CMG-INV-01 through CMG-INV-12 and nothing else.

L.3 The meta-constitutional validation SHALL be **mechanically executable**. Its realization IS `00-CMG/tools/cmg_validate.py`, which SHALL:
(a) parse this instrument and verify that every mandated section of the conformance map resolves to a present Article;
(b) verify that every identifier family declared in V.3 is used only as declared;
(c) load the derived Registry and verify that every entry resolves to a present canonical home;
(d) verify CMG-INV-02 by checking that the concern-to-owner mapping is injective on concerns;
(e) verify CMG-INV-03 by checking that every concern has an owner and every owner has a concern;
(f) verify CMG-INV-04 by checking that every declared superior resolves to a located artifact or to a recorded vacancy carrying a closure procedure;
(g) verify CMG-INV-05 by topologically sorting the dependency graph and the precedence lattice;
(h) verify CMG-INV-06 by checking that every artifact pair resolves to a rank or a declared orthogonality;
(i) verify CMG-INV-07 by checking recorded states and transitions against Articles XXV and XXVI;
(j) verify CMG-INV-08 by checking identifier injectivity;
(k) verify CMG-INV-11 by checking that every lineage predecessor resolves to a present artifact;
(l) exit non-zero on any finding.

L.4 The validator SHALL be **fail-closed**: an unparseable input, a missing file, or an ambiguous record SHALL produce a finding, never a pass.

L.5 The validator SHALL be **deterministic and hermetic**: identical repository state SHALL produce byte-identical output, independent of environment, clock, ordering, or locale.

L.6 The validator IS **derived-truth** (XII.6). It asserts nothing on its own authority; it recomputes what this instrument already declares. A disagreement between the validator and this instrument IS a validator defect unless this instrument is internally inconsistent, in which case it IS a defect of this instrument.

L.7 The validator SHALL NOT duplicate any check already performed by a located gate. Where a located gate already enforces a property, the validator SHALL bind to it rather than re-implement it.

## ARTICLE LI — CERTIFICATION FRAMEWORK

LI.1 **Certification operation** — eligibility, attestation, issuance, suspension, revocation, renewal, expiry, and the certification registry — IS located and owned and IS recorded in Article LXXXII as CMG-DLG-05. This instrument SHALL NOT create a second certification authority.

LI.2 This instrument SHALL define exactly one certification **subject**: **Constitutional Readiness** (Article LXXX). Its preconditions are stated there; its attestation IS performed by the located certification owner.

LI.3 Certification SHALL attest and SHALL confer nothing. A certified artifact IS not thereby ratified, frozen, published, or in effect.

LI.4 Certification SHALL be **reproducible**: re-running the attestation against unchanged state SHALL yield an identical result.

LI.5 Certification SHALL be **revoked by amendment** of its subject. An amended artifact carries no valid certification until re-certified. This is already the located rule and is consumed by reference.

LI.6 Certification SHALL NOT be self-issued. The certifying authority SHALL be distinct from the certified subject's author.

LI.7 A certification issued against a **vacant** superior authority SHALL be marked PROVISIONAL, and its provisional character SHALL NOT be lost by subsequent freeze (CMG-T-09).

## ARTICLE LII — AUDIT FRAMEWORK

LII.1 **Audit operation** — drift detection, contradiction detection, dependency-integrity checking, findings emission, and the audit registry — IS located and owned and IS recorded in Article LXXXII as CMG-DLG-11. This instrument SHALL NOT create a second audit authority.

LII.2 This instrument SHALL contribute exactly one audit **domain**: meta-constitutional audit, comprising detection of latent constitutions (XII.5), parallel authority (CMG-INV-02), orphan governance (CMG-INV-03), dangling superiors (CMG-INV-04), cycles (CMG-INV-05), undecidable precedence (CMG-INV-06), illegal lifecycle records (CMG-INV-07), identity violations (CMG-INV-08), phase-force inconsistency (XXVII.3), unperformable obligations (XXIII.5), and jurisdiction breach by this instrument itself (CMG-INV-12).

LII.4 A finding SHALL carry: identifier, detected condition, affected artifacts, the invariant or clause breached, severity, and the owner to whom it is routed. A finding missing its owner cannot be dispositioned and IS itself a defect.

LII.5 Audit of **this instrument** SHALL be performed by the located audit owner and SHALL NOT be performed by this instrument. Self-audit as the sole audit IS PROHIBITED.

LII.6 An undispositioned finding SHALL block certification under Article LXXX. Findings SHALL NOT accumulate silently.

## ARTICLE LIII — EVIDENCE FRAMEWORK

LIII.1 **Evidence operation** — evidence identity, collection, verification, preservation, provenance, and the evidence registry — IS located and owned and IS recorded in Article LXXXII as CMG-DLG-08. This instrument SHALL NOT create a second evidence store.

LIII.2 Every assertion of this instrument SHALL be **evidence-bound** (CMG-L-10). An assertion without evidence IS an opinion and SHALL NOT gate anything.

LIII.3 Evidence for meta-constitutional assertions SHALL be **recomputable from repository state** rather than captured as a narrative. Where an assertion cannot be recomputed, it SHALL be recorded as a judgement with a named accountable authority, not as evidence.

LIII.4 Evidence identity SHALL be **content-addressed and never reassigned**; evidence without unique identity SHALL be deemed non-existent. This is the located rule and is consumed by reference.

LIII.5 Evidence SHALL be **preserved** across supersession. Superseded evidence SHALL be retained with lineage and SHALL NOT be deleted (CMG-L-11).

LIII.6 Evidence SHALL NOT be produced by the authority that consumes it as the basis of its own standing. Self-generated evidence of one's own authority IS a form of self-elevation (CMG-L-04).

## ARTICLE LIV — CONFLICT RESOLUTION

LIV.1 **Conflict resolution operation** IS located and owned and IS recorded in Article LXXXII as CMG-DLG-02. Every conflict in this corpus resolves deterministically to exactly one recorded outcome under that owner's procedure. This instrument SHALL NOT create a second conflict-resolution procedure.

LIV.2 This instrument SHALL contribute exactly one input to that procedure: the **precedence lattice** of Article XVI and the **resolution order** of Article XVII, which together make the located procedure decidable in cases the corpus previously could not rank.

LIV.3 Conflict classification SHALL be, in order of test:
(a) **Jurisdiction error** — one instrument has spoken outside its scope. Resolution: the domain owner governs; the out-of-scope clause IS void (CMG-L-13). This test IS applied first because most apparent conflicts are jurisdiction errors.
(b) **Orthogonality** — the two instruments occupy different axes. Resolution: decompose the question per XVII.5.
(c) **Rank conflict** — both instruments are in scope and comparable. Resolution: the lattice and intra-tier rules of XVI.6.
(d) **Genuine contradiction** — both are in scope, of equal rank, and irreconcilable. Resolution: escalate as an open question under Article LVII; the matter IS ungoverned until decided, and SHALL be recorded as such rather than decided by default.

LIV.4 A conflict SHALL NOT be resolved by the instrument seeking to prevail (XVII.8), by recency, by omission, or by silently editing one side.

LIV.5 Every resolution SHALL be **recorded and precedential**: the same conflict SHALL resolve identically thereafter, and the record SHALL be citable.

LIV.6 An unresolved conflict SHALL be visible: CMG-R-15 (CONFLICTS-WITH) SHALL be declared and SHALL block certification (XXXIV.4). Concealing an unresolved conflict IS a graver defect than the conflict.

## ARTICLE LV — EXCEPTION HANDLING

LV.1 An **exception** IS a recorded, bounded, time-limited permission to depart from an obligation. Exceptions exist because a corpus that cannot record a departure will instead conceal one.

LV.2 An exception SHALL carry: identifier, the obligation departed from, the subject, the justification, the compensating control, the expiry condition, the approving authority, and the accountable authority. An exception missing any element IS void.

LV.3 An exception SHALL be **bounded in time or condition**. A permanent exception IS an amendment in disguise and SHALL be pursued as an amendment (Article XLIII).

LV.4 An exception SHALL NOT be granted against an **invariant**. Invariants (Article XI) admit no exception; an invariant that requires exception SHALL be amended, not waived. This is the practical meaning of invariants as immutable ground.

LV.5 An exception SHALL NOT be granted against a **prohibition** where the prohibition protects identity, preservation, acyclicity, or non-self-elevation. Those prohibitions are structural.

LV.6 An exception SHALL be **counted in certification**: a subject operating under exception SHALL NOT be certified as unconditionally compliant, and its certification SHALL name the exception.

LV.7 Expired exceptions SHALL be **detected**, not merely scheduled. An expired exception that continues in use IS a finding under Article LII.

LV.8 A matter knowingly not decided IS **not** an exception; it IS a deferral and SHALL be recorded in the deferral record. The corpus references a deferral register across multiple instruments; the **lifecycle** of that register IS owned by the located governance instrument at CEP-002 Article 27, recognized at CMG-DLG-49. The former vacancy IS recorded as CMG-GAP-02 and its disposition IS stated in Article LXXVIII.

## ARTICLE LVI — RISK GOVERNANCE

LVI.1 **Constitutional risk** IS the possibility that the governance system fails to bind what it claims to bind. It IS distinct from delivery risk, technical risk, and operational risk, and SHALL NOT be managed with the same instruments.

LVI.2 The recognized constitutional risk classes SHALL be, at minimum: **authority risk** (parallel, absent, or self-elevated authority); **recognition risk** (latent constitutions); **decidability risk** (undecidable obligations, unrankable conflicts, cycles); **drift risk** (copies, restatements, stale distribution, derived truth mistaken for source); **continuity risk** (loss of stewardship, loss of record, loss of retrievability); **finality risk** (vacant ratification authority); and **finiteness risk** (a hidden ceiling that will be reached). The class set IS OPEN.

LVI.3 Every risk class SHALL have a **detection mechanism** and an **owner**. A risk class with no detection mechanism IS not governed, and SHALL be recorded as a gap.

LVI.4 Risk SHALL be **recorded, not narrated**. A risk register entry SHALL name the class, the condition, the detection, the owner, and the mitigation, and SHALL be verifiable.

LVI.5 **Finiteness risk** SHALL receive standing attention: every enumeration, width, ceiling, tier, and identifier space in the corpus SHALL be periodically re-examined for a hidden ceiling. The corpus already maintains a determination on hidden finite assumptions; this instrument binds to it and adds the meta-level requirement that every CLOSED enumeration cite its closing invariant (CMG-INV-09).

LVI.6 Risk mitigation SHALL NOT be achieved by lowering an obligation. Mitigation adjusts the mechanism, never the law.

LVI.7 Accepted risk SHALL be **accepted by a named accountable authority** (Article XXII). Unattributed risk acceptance IS void.

## ARTICLE LVII — DECISION GOVERNANCE

LVII.1 Every constitutional decision SHALL be **recorded as an artifact**, and the record SHALL state: the question, the authority deciding, the alternatives considered, the alternatives rejected and why, the decision, the rationale, the consequences, the impact, and the supersession rule.

LVII.2 A decision SHALL be made by the authority that owns the concern (Article XVII). A decision made by a non-owner IS void, regardless of its merit.

LVII.3 Where a question is **undecidable by any located authority** — because the authority is vacant, because two orthogonal instruments both claim it, or because the corpus contains a genuine contradiction — the question SHALL be recorded as an **open constitutional question** and SHALL be routed to explicit ratification. It SHALL NOT be decided by default, by the detector, by the most convenient authority, or by silence.

LVII.4 An open constitutional question SHALL be **visible and enumerated** (Article LXXVIII) and SHALL block final certification of the matters that depend on it, while permitting provisional advancement of matters that do not.

LVII.5 A decision SHALL be **superseded, never edited**. The decision record IS append-only.

LVII.6 Decisions SHALL be **stored once**. The corpus already maintains a decision store and an architecture-decision record series; this instrument binds to them and SHALL NOT create a third (CMG-L-14).

LVII.7 A decision SHALL NOT create authority. Where a decision would allocate authority, it SHALL take the form of an amendment to the allocating instrument, so that the allocation is visible as law rather than buried in a decision record.

## ARTICLE LVIII — REPOSITORY INTEGRATION

LVIII.1 A repository IS the **storage and version-control substrate** of the corpus, not its authority. Constitutional standing SHALL NOT derive from presence in a repository, and presence SHALL NOT be mistaken for recognition.

LVIII.2 **Repository Truth** — the authoritative record of what the repository contains and means — IS located and owned by the corpus's knowledge authority. This instrument SHALL consume Repository Truth and SHALL NOT redefine, restate, or compete with it.

LVIII.3 Every recognized constitutional artifact SHALL have exactly one **canonical home** in the repository, recorded in the Registry. Two canonical homes for one artifact IS a duplication defect; zero canonical homes IS a recognition defect.

LVIII.4 This instrument SHALL occupy exactly one repository zone, `00-CMG/`, and SHALL NOT write into any other zone. Its integration with every other zone IS by reference only.

LVIII.5 Repository integration SHALL be **non-mutating with respect to the existing corpus**: admission of this instrument SHALL NOT rename, renumber, reclassify, relocate, or edit any existing artifact, identifier, namespace, register, or configuration. This is a hard condition of admission and is verified in the repository impact analysis.

LVIII.6 Repository integration SHALL be **configuration-driven**. Where the repository's classification, registration, or enforcement machinery must recognize this instrument, recognition SHALL be achieved by the artifact's own declared metadata rather than by editing shared machinery, so that no code change is required to admit a new constitutional zone (CMG-L-08).

LVIII.7 A repository boundary SHALL NOT be assumed. The corpus MAY span an unbounded number of repositories; namespace qualification (XXXIII.4) and federation-scoped reach (XII.3) exist so that no clause of this instrument requires a single repository.

LVIII.8 Frozen repository paths SHALL be treated as **read-only by law**. This instrument SHALL NOT write to any frozen path, and any change it appears to require in a frozen path IS instead a supersession under Article LXXII.

## ARTICLE LX — GENERATOR INTEGRATION

LX.1 A **generator** IS any process that produces artifacts from constitutional input. Generators SHALL be governed as consumers of law, never as sources of it.

LX.2 A generator SHALL NOT be a source of constitutional truth. Generated output IS derived truth and SHALL be reconciled against its constitutional input; where they disagree, the input governs.

LX.3 A generator SHALL consume constitutional artifacts **by identity and version**, and its output SHALL record which versions it consumed. Output that cannot name its constitutional inputs is untraceable and SHALL NOT be admitted.

LX.4 A generator SHALL be **deterministic**: identical constitutional input SHALL produce identical output. Non-determinism in a generator makes constitutional conformance unverifiable.

LX.5 A generator SHALL NOT embed constitutional content in its own code. Constitutional rules SHALL be read from the artifacts at generation time (CMG-L-08). Embedded rules are copies and will drift.

LX.6 A change to a constitutional artifact SHALL place every generator that consumes it into the impact set (XXXVI.6), and affected generated output SHALL be regenerated or explicitly classified UNAFFECTED.

LX.7 Generation frameworks of this corpus are located and owned; this Article states the meta requirements on generators and SHALL NOT specify a generation mechanism or technology.

## ARTICLE LXI — KNOWLEDGE INTEGRATION

LXI.1 **Knowledge Once** (VIII.1) SHALL govern the relationship between this instrument and the corpus knowledge base: a constitutional truth SHALL be recorded once, in its canonical home, and referenced from the knowledge base rather than copied into it.

LXI.2 The corpus knowledge authority owns knowledge content, canonical knowledge objects, decisions, handbooks, and the knowledge graph. This instrument SHALL consume them and SHALL NOT author knowledge content.

LXI.3 Every concept introduced by this instrument SHALL be **homed**: it SHALL have a canonical home discoverable by the corpus's closure machinery, so that it cannot appear as an unhomed or orphan concept. Admission of this instrument SHALL NOT increase the corpus's unhomed-concept count.

LXI.4 Concepts introduced by this instrument SHALL be registered as knowledge objects with authority `constitutional` where they are law, and `advisory` where they are guidance. Misdeclared authority in the knowledge base IS a finding.

LXI.5 The knowledge graph SHALL be the **discovery surface** for meta-constitutional facts, and this instrument SHALL NOT build a second discovery surface.

LXI.6 Knowledge integration SHALL be **bidirectional in reference and unidirectional in authority**: the knowledge base may reference this instrument freely; this instrument SHALL NOT derive authority from the knowledge base, because the knowledge base is derived truth.

## ARTICLE LXII — IMPLEMENTATION INTEGRATION

LXII.1 **Implementation** IS the realization of constitutional rule in executable form. Implementation SHALL conform to constitutional law and SHALL NOT constitute it.

LXII.2 An implementation SHALL trace to the constitutional obligation it realizes (Article XXXVII). An implementation with no constitutional trace IS ungoverned function and SHALL be recorded as a finding.

LXII.3 An implementation SHALL NOT hard-code constitutional content. Rules SHALL be read from configuration and from the artifacts (CMG-L-08); an implementation containing a copy of a constitutional enumeration IS a duplication defect.

LXII.4 Implementation admission — whether an implementation may enter Repository Truth — IS located and owned by the corpus's engineering governance authority. This instrument SHALL NOT govern admission and SHALL NOT create a second conformance standard.

LXII.5 This instrument's own implementation footprint SHALL be exactly the validator of Article L and the derived Registry of Article XV. It SHALL introduce no engine, service, runtime component, or persistent store.

LXII.6 An implementation SHALL be replaceable without constitutional change. Where replacing an implementation requires amending a constitution, the constitution has named a technology in violation of CMG-L-09.

## ARTICLE LXIII — RUNTIME INTEGRATION

LXIII.1 A **runtime** IS an executing context in which governed function operates. Runtimes consume constitutional law at execution time and SHALL NOT alter it.

LXIII.2 Constitutional artifacts SHALL be **resolvable at runtime by identity and version**, so that a running system can answer which law binds it. A runtime that cannot resolve its governing law is ungoverned in practice however well governed on paper.

LXIII.3 Runtime enforcement SHALL be **fail-closed** with respect to constitutional resolution: where the governing artifact cannot be resolved, the operation SHALL be refused rather than performed under assumption.

LXIII.4 A runtime SHALL NOT cache constitutional content beyond a version-bound, content-hash-verifiable cache. An unverifiable cache is a distributed copy and falls under Article XLVIII.

LXIII.5 Runtime state, telemetry, checkpoints, and operational memory ARE derived truth and SHALL NEVER be treated as constitutional corpus. This restates a rule the corpus already holds and is consumed by reference.

LXIII.6 The runtime constitution of this corpus IS located and owned; this Article states meta requirements only and SHALL NOT specify a runtime model or technology.

## ARTICLE LXIV — SECURITY INTEGRATION

LXIV.1 Constitutional artifacts ARE **integrity-critical assets**. Their protection concern IS integrity and authenticity of authority, not confidentiality; constitutional law is meant to be read.

LXIV.2 Every constitutional artifact SHALL be **tamper-evident**: its content hash SHALL be recorded so that unauthorized alteration is detectable. Undetectable alteration of constitutional law IS the most severe security failure in the corpus.

LXIV.3 Authority SHALL NOT be conferrable by write access. Possession of the ability to edit a file SHALL NOT constitute authority over its concern (XX.4). This separation is what prevents privilege from becoming jurisdiction.

LXIV.4 Amendment, ratification, freeze, and registry admission SHALL require **authorization distinct from repository write permission**, and the authorization SHALL be recorded.

LXIV.5 Security controls, models, and mechanisms ARE located and owned by the corpus's security authority. This instrument SHALL NOT specify controls and SHALL NOT create a second security model.

LXIV.6 Secrets, credentials, and keys SHALL NEVER appear in a constitutional artifact. A constitutional artifact IS published within its reach by law (XLVII.2), so any secret in it IS disclosed by design.

LXIV.7 Security findings against constitutional artifacts SHALL be routed to the security owner for disposition and to this instrument only where the finding concerns authority integrity.

## ARTICLE LXV — AI INTEGRATION

LXV.1 An **intelligence** — human, machine, model, agent, or any future form — MAY author, review, steward, validate, and implement constitutional artifacts. The set of admissible intelligences IS OPEN, and no clause of this instrument assumes a human author or a particular model.

LXV.2 An intelligence SHALL NOT hold **ownership** (XX.2) or **accountability** (XXII.3). It MAY hold stewardship and responsibility. This is the structural safeguard that keeps the corpus governable as its intelligence population changes without bound.

LXV.3 An intelligence SHALL NOT ratify, SHALL NOT grant itself authority, and SHALL NOT expand a jurisdiction by interpretation (CMG-L-04). The located rule that no agent may grant itself ratification authority is consumed here by reference and generalized to all intelligences.

LXV.4 Output produced by an intelligence SHALL be **evidence-bound and reproducible** to the extent its production permits, and where reproducibility is impossible the output SHALL be recorded as a judgement with a named accountable authority rather than as evidence (LIII.3).

LXV.5 Constitutional artifacts SHALL be **model-neutral**: no artifact SHALL name a model, provider, version, or capability as a condition of its law (CMG-L-09). An artifact whose validity depends on a specific model IS defective.

LXV.6 An intelligence acting as a reviewer SHALL be distinct from the intelligence acting as author (XLV.4). Identity of the intelligence SHALL be recorded so that independence is verifiable.

LXV.7 An intelligence MAY propose amendments, gaps, kinds, and open questions. It SHALL NOT admit them; admission IS an authority act.

LXV.8 Where an intelligence detects a constitutional defect, it SHALL emit a finding routed to the owner (LII.3) and SHALL NOT self-remediate constitutional law.

## ARTICLE LXVI — AUTOMATION INTEGRATION

LXVI.1 Every constitutional obligation that IS deterministically checkable SHALL be checked by automation, and the automated check SHALL be **unskippable**. An obligation checked only by intention is not enforced.

LXVI.2 Automation SHALL be **fail-closed**: a check that cannot run SHALL block, never pass. A silently skipped check is worse than no check, because it manufactures false assurance.

LXVI.3 Automation SHALL be **derived-truth** (XII.6): it enforces law, it does not constitute it. A discrepancy between a check and its constitutional source IS a check defect unless the source is internally inconsistent.

LXVI.4 Automation SHALL bind to **existing enforcement machinery** where it exists. This instrument SHALL add its check to the corpus's existing gate chain rather than establish a parallel pipeline (CMG-L-14).

LXVI.5 Automation SHALL be **configuration-driven**: adding a new constitutional artifact, kind, namespace, or delegation SHALL NOT require editing check code. Where a check must be edited to admit a new member, the check has hard-coded an enumeration in violation of CMG-L-08.

LXVI.6 Automation SHALL emit **findings, not determinations** (LII.3), and SHALL route each finding to the owner of the affected concern.

LXVI.7 The automated realization of this instrument SHALL be exactly one gate — the validator of Article L, invoked through the corpus's existing verification entry point. It SHALL introduce no second pipeline, scheduler, or daemon.

## ARTICLE LXVII — EVOLUTION FRAMEWORK

LXVII.1 **Evolution operation** — change classification, impact assessment, compatibility determination, successor modelling, migration, deprecation, retirement, lineage preservation, and the evolution registry — IS located and owned and IS recorded in Article LXXXII as CMG-DLG-09. This instrument SHALL NOT create a second evolution model.

LXVII.2 This instrument SHALL own exactly one evolution concern: the **evolution of constitutionality itself** — the admission of new constitutional kinds, new tiers, new namespaces, new relationship types, new lifecycle states, and new meta concerns. That concern IS discharged by Articles LXXVI and LXXVII.

LXVII.3 Evolution SHALL be **additive by default**. The default disposition for any new constitutional concept IS EXTEND an existing owner (VIII.2); CREATE requires a recorded reuse discovery (XL.5); MODIFY requires amendment (Article XLII).

LXVII.4 Evolution SHALL **never destroy historical truth** (CMG-L-11). Every evolutionary step SHALL leave the prior state retrievable with lineage.

LXVII.5 Evolution SHALL be **unbounded in extent and bounded in mechanism**: there SHALL be no ceiling on how far the corpus may evolve, and exactly one mechanism by which it evolves. Multiple evolution paths would permit bypass, and bypass IS PROHIBITED.

LXVII.6 The **immutable ground** of the corpus — the located universal invariants and the located evolution model — SHALL NOT be evolved by this instrument. This instrument's own immutable ground IS Article XI and Article XLIII (XLIII.6).

LXVII.7 Evolution SHALL be **recorded as law, not as intent**. A roadmap IS not an evolution; only an admitted change is.

## ARTICLE LXVIII — BACKWARD COMPATIBILITY

LXVIII.1 **Backward compatibility** IS the property that an artifact conforming to a prior version remains conforming under the current version. It IS a property of the change, not a goal to be balanced.

LXVIII.2 A change SHALL be backward compatible unless it is classified MAJOR (XXIX.2) and admitted through the full ratification path. Silent breaking change IS PROHIBITED.

LXVIII.3 Backward compatibility SHALL be **verified, not asserted**: the impact analysis (Article XXXVI) SHALL identify every subject that a change could invalidate, and each SHALL be classified.

LXVIII.4 Where backward compatibility cannot be preserved, the change SHALL provide a **migration path** (Article LXX) and a **transition period** during which both versions bind, and the dual-binding period SHALL be bounded and recorded.

LXVIII.5 Backward compatibility SHALL NOT be achieved by weakening the current version. Compatibility IS provided by migration, deprecation, and transition, never by lowering an obligation.

LXVIII.6 Admission of this instrument SHALL be **fully backward compatible**: it changes no existing obligation, invalidates no conforming artifact, and requires no existing artifact to change. Every effect it has on the existing corpus is additive recognition.

## ARTICLE LXIX — FORWARD COMPATIBILITY

LXIX.1 **Forward compatibility** IS the property that the present corpus can admit future concepts without amendment of its structure. It IS the operational form of Zero-Finite (P.5).

LXIX.2 Forward compatibility SHALL be achieved by four structural devices, and this instrument SHALL use all four:
(a) **open enumerations** — kinds, standings, reaches, states, relationships, principles, laws, namespaces, tiers, and risk classes are OPEN (CMG-P-09);
(b) **symbolic non-consecutive identifiers** — tiers are symbolic so insertion never renumbers (XVI.8);
(c) **rule-stated membership** — law states the rule, configuration carries the members (CMG-L-08);
(d) **declared admission procedure** — a named procedure exists for admitting a concept that does not yet exist (Articles LXXVI, LXXVII).

LXIX.3 A forward-incompatible construct SHALL be treated as a **hidden finite assumption** and recorded as a finiteness risk (LVI.5). Examples SHALL include: fixed-width identifier spaces without extension rules, consecutive tier numbering, closed enumerations without a closing invariant, hard-coded member lists, and single-repository or single-universe assumptions.

LXIX.4 Forward compatibility SHALL NOT be claimed by generality of language. It SHALL be evidenced by naming the admission procedure for the unknown case, which this instrument does in Article LXXVII.

LXIX.5 A future concept SHALL NOT require amendment of Article XI (the invariants) to be admitted. If it does, the concept is not a new subject but a challenge to the checking apparatus, and SHALL be escalated as an open question rather than admitted.

## ARTICLE LXX — MIGRATION GOVERNANCE

LXX.1 **Migration** IS the governed movement of subjects from a prior constitutional state to a successor state. Migration operation IS located and owned under CMG-DLG-09 and IS NOT re-legislated here.

LXX.2 Every MAJOR change SHALL carry a migration plan naming: the affected subjects, the source state, the target state, the sequence, the reversibility, the verification, and the completion criterion.

LXX.3 Migration SHALL be **verifiable at completion**: a migration SHALL declare how completeness is proven, and an unprovable migration SHALL NOT be declared complete.

LXX.4 Migration SHALL preserve **identity and lineage**: a migrated subject SHALL retain its identity or SHALL receive a new identity linked to its predecessor (XXX.7). Silent identity change IS PROHIBITED.

LXX.5 Migration SHALL be **resumable**: an interrupted migration SHALL be recoverable to a known state (Article LXXIV), and SHALL NOT leave subjects in an undeclared state.

LXX.6 Migration SHALL NOT be a precondition of admission for this instrument. This instrument requires **no migration of any existing artifact**; that is a deliberate design property and is verified in the migration strategy.

LXX.7 A migration that would edit frozen text SHALL instead proceed by supersession (Article LXXII).

## ARTICLE LXXI — DEPRECATION GOVERNANCE

LXXI.1 **Deprecation** IS the recorded declaration that an artifact remains in effect but SHALL cease, and that new dependency on it IS prohibited.

LXXI.2 Deprecation SHALL carry a **cessation condition** — a date, an event, or a successor's readiness. Deprecation without a cessation condition IS indefinite and IS PROHIBITED, because it produces law that is neither current nor ended.

LXXI.3 A deprecated artifact SHALL continue to bind its existing subjects until it reaches POST-EFFECT. Deprecation SHALL NOT silently suspend obligations.

LXXI.4 New dependency on a deprecated artifact SHALL be refused and SHALL be a finding where it occurs. Existing dependencies SHALL be enumerated and given a migration path.

LXXI.5 Deprecation SHALL be **discoverable at the point of citation**: a citation to a deprecated artifact SHALL surface its state, so that a reader cannot rely on it unknowingly.

LXXI.6 Deprecation SHALL NOT be used in place of amendment or supersession. Deprecating an artifact to avoid amending it leaves the corpus with unmaintained law in effect.

LXXI.7 Deprecation operation IS located and owned under CMG-DLG-09; this Article states meta requirements only.

---

## ARTICLE LXXII — SUPERSESSION GOVERNANCE

LXXII.1 **Supersession** IS the replacement of an artifact by a successor with recorded lineage. It IS the only path by which frozen constitutional text may be effectively changed.

LXXII.2 A successor SHALL be a **new artifact with its own identity**, linked to its predecessor by explicit lineage, and SHALL NOT reuse, overwrite, or inherit the predecessor's identity. This is the located rule and is consumed by reference.

LXXII.3 The predecessor SHALL be **retained and reachable** and SHALL move to POST-EFFECT. Deletion of a superseded artifact IS PROHIBITED (CMG-L-11).

LXXII.4 Supersession SHALL be **complete in scope**: the successor SHALL cover every concern the predecessor owned, or the uncovered concerns SHALL be explicitly reassigned or recorded as gaps. Partial supersession that silently orphans a concern violates CMG-INV-03.

LXXII.5 Every dependent of the predecessor SHALL be **repointed or waived** (XXXV.7). A dependency left pointing at a superseded artifact IS a defect.

LXXII.6 Supersession lineage SHALL be **acyclic and append-only**, and a lineage chain SHALL be traversable from any version to the original.

LXXII.7 Supersession SHALL NOT confer standing on the successor beyond what its own ratification confers. A successor to a ratified artifact IS not thereby ratified.

## ARTICLE LXXIII — ARCHIVAL GOVERNANCE

LXXIII.1 **Archival** IS the movement of a POST-EFFECT artifact out of the working corpus while preserving it retrievably. Archival SHALL NOT be deletion, and SHALL NOT be permitted for an IN-EFFECT artifact.

LXXIII.2 An archived artifact SHALL remain **identified, addressable, and retrievable** by its identifier and version. An archive from which an artifact cannot be retrieved IS a deletion by another name.

LXXIII.3 Archival SHALL preserve **content integrity**: the archived content hash SHALL match the hash recorded at the time of archival, and a mismatch IS a severe finding.

LXXIII.4 Archival SHALL preserve **relationship history**: the artifact's declared relationships, lineage, evidence, and findings SHALL be archived with it. An artifact archived without its context cannot be understood later.

LXXIII.5 Archival SHALL NOT remove an artifact's Registry entry (XV.4). The entry persists with state CMG-S-13.

LXXIII.6 An archived artifact SHALL NOT be cited as current authority. It IS citable as history only.

LXXIII.7 Archival storage location, medium, format, and technology ARE outside this instrument's scope (CMG-L-09). The requirement IS retrievability, not a mechanism.

## ARTICLE LXXIV — RECOVERY GOVERNANCE

LXXIV.1 **Recovery** IS the restoration of the constitutional corpus, or any part of it, to a known-good, self-consistent state after loss, corruption, or interrupted change.

LXXIV.2 Recovery SHALL be to a **declared baseline**. Recovery to an undeclared state IS PROHIBITED, because the recovered corpus's constitutional consistency would be unknown.

LXXIV.3 **Restoration** of an archived artifact into effect SHALL proceed only through CMG-T-20: the artifact re-enters PRE-EFFECT and SHALL re-validate and re-certify against the present corpus. Direct restoration to effect IS PROHIBITED (XXVI.3), because the corpus it must be consistent with has changed.

LXXIV.4 Recovery SHALL be **verified**: after recovery, CMG-INV-01 through CMG-INV-12 SHALL be re-verified before any artifact is treated as in effect. An unverified recovery leaves the corpus in an unknown constitutional state.

LXXIV.5 Recovery SHALL NOT reuse identifiers of artifacts lost and not recovered. A lost artifact's identifier remains allocated (XXX.4).

LXXIV.6 Recovery of **derived truth** — registries, projections, traces, indexes, caches — SHALL be by regeneration from canonical sources, never by restoring a stale copy. A restored derived store that disagrees with its sources IS a drift defect.

LXXIV.7 Recovery operation, baselines, and mechanisms ARE located and owned; this Article states meta requirements only.

## ARTICLE LXXV — CONTINUITY GOVERNANCE

LXXV.1 **Continuity** IS the property that constitutional governance persists across the loss of any person, team, organization, agent, model, tool, repository, platform, or generation.

LXXV.2 Continuity SHALL be achieved **structurally, not procedurally**: because ownership attaches to artifacts rather than to entities (XX.2), the disappearance of any entity SHALL NOT orphan a concern.

LXXV.3 Every concern SHALL be governable from the **repository alone**. Constitutional knowledge held only in a person, a conversation, a tool, or a service IS not governed, and SHALL be recorded as a continuity gap until homed.

LXXV.4 Continuity SHALL require that every stewardship, responsibility, and accountability assignment be **recorded by role**, so that a successor can assume it without reconstruction.

LXXV.5 Continuity SHALL survive **tooling loss**: the canonical form of every constitutional artifact SHALL be readable without any specific tool (XLVII.5, and the canonical-form declaration of this instrument). Derived forms MAY require tools; canonical forms SHALL NOT.

LXXV.6 Continuity SHALL survive **program completion**: when a program ends, its owned concerns SHALL be transferred to a named successor owner or recorded as gaps. The corpus already governs successor ownership; this instrument binds to it and SHALL NOT create a second successor model.

LXXV.7 Continuity SHALL be **tested**, not assumed: the ability to reconstruct the full constitutional state from repository content alone SHALL be exercised, and inability SHALL be a finding.

## ARTICLE LXXVI — FUTURE CONSTITUTIONAL EXPANSION

LXXVI.1 The corpus SHALL be capable of admitting, without structural amendment: a new constitutional **Kind**, **Standing**, **Reach**, **Tier**, **Namespace**, **Relationship type**, **Lifecycle state**, **Risk class**, **Principle**, **Law**, **Concern**, **Delegation**, **Domain**, **Universe**, **Federation**, **Repository**, **Intelligence**, and **Constitutional artifact**.

LXXVI.2 The **admission procedure** for any of the above SHALL be:
(a) **Discover** — establish that the concept is not already owned. Record the discovery, naming every existing owner examined and why each is insufficient.
(b) **Classify** — determine whether the concept is meta-constitutional (this instrument's jurisdiction) or substantive (some other owner's jurisdiction). A substantive concept SHALL NOT be admitted here.
(c) **Dispose** — choose EXTEND (bind to an existing owner), CREATE (a new artifact), or REJECT (the concept is not constitutional). EXTEND IS the default; CREATE requires the recorded discovery of (a).
(d) **Allocate** — assign the concept to exactly one owner, and record the allocation so that CMG-INV-02 and CMG-INV-03 remain satisfied.
(e) **Relate** — declare its relationships, dependencies, and precedence position; verify acyclicity.
(f) **Register** — admit it to the Registry with identity, kind, standing, reach, and state.
(g) **Verify** — re-run the validator of Article L; all twelve invariants SHALL hold.
(h) **Certify and accept** — proceed through the located certification and ratification owners; where the competent authority is vacant, standing IS PROVISIONAL.

LXXVI.3 Admission SHALL be **append-only**: it SHALL NOT renumber, rename, reclassify, or invalidate any existing member of any enumeration.

LXXVI.4 Admission SHALL NOT expand this instrument's jurisdiction (XLIII.4). A new meta concern IS admitted as a new artifact in the `CMG` namespace, subordinate to this instrument; it SHALL NOT be absorbed into this instrument if absorption would broaden Article XIX.1.

LXXVI.5 Expansion SHALL be **unbounded in count**. No clause of this instrument limits the number of kinds, tiers, namespaces, states, relationships, artifacts, repositories, universes, or intelligences, and any apparent limit SHALL be read as a defect and recorded as a finiteness risk.

LXXVI.6 Expansion SHALL NOT be achieved by **reinterpretation** of an existing member to mean something new. Reinterpretation is invisible to validation; admission is visible.

## ARTICLE LXXVII — UNKNOWN FUTURE CONCEPTS

LXXVII.1 This instrument SHALL govern concepts that do not yet exist and cannot presently be named. It SHALL do so not by anticipating them but by declaring a **total admission procedure** that applies to any concept whatsoever.

LXXVII.2 The **totality rule** SHALL be: for any concept C presented to the corpus, exactly one of the following SHALL hold, and the procedure SHALL determine which:
(a) C is already owned — the owner governs; disposition REUSE.
(b) C is within an existing owner's scope but unaddressed — disposition EXTEND that owner.
(c) C is meta-constitutional and unowned — this instrument governs; disposition CREATE in the `CMG` namespace.
(d) C is substantive and unowned — C IS an orphan; disposition RECORD AS GAP and route to the authority competent to allocate. This instrument SHALL NOT govern C.
(e) C is not constitutional — disposition REJECT with recorded reason; C is governed by some non-constitutional authority or by none.

LXXVII.3 The five outcomes of LXXVII.2 ARE exhaustive by construction: they partition on (owned / unowned) × (in-scope / out-of-scope) × (meta / substantive / non-constitutional). Exhaustiveness IS the mechanism by which an unknown concept is handled without being anticipated.

LXXVII.4 An unknown concept SHALL NOT be admitted by **default routing** to the nearest owner, the most active program, or the meta layer. Default routing manufactures parallel authority; explicit disposition prevents it.

LXXVII.5 Where the totality rule cannot be applied — because the concept's nature is genuinely undetermined — the concept SHALL be recorded as an **open constitutional question** (Article LVII.3) and SHALL be held in the deferral record. Holding IS a valid disposition; silent adoption IS not.

LXXVII.6 This instrument SHALL NOT assume that future concepts will be expressible in its present ontology (Article XIV). Where a concept requires a new ontology entity, the entity IS admitted under LXXVI.1 and SHALL NOT alter any existing cardinality (XIV.7).

LXXVII.7 This instrument SHALL NOT assume the continued existence of any present technology, repository, language, model, organization, or civilization. Every clause of this instrument IS expressed in terms of authority, concern, identity, and record — none of which depends on a substrate.

## ARTICLE LXXIX — COMPLETENESS VERIFICATION

LXXIX.1 **Completeness** under this instrument SHALL mean: no open gap within jurisdiction, no undelegated residue, no unrecorded vacancy, no unrecorded open question, and all twelve invariants satisfied. It SHALL NOT mean the absence of possible future concepts (I.5).

LXXIX.2 Completeness SHALL be verified by answering, for **every** recognized constitutional artifact, exactly four questions, each answerable deterministically from recorded fact:
(a) **What authority does it hold, and over which concerns?**
(b) **Who owns it, who stewards it, and who is accountable for it?**
(c) **What lifecycle state is it in, and is that state legal?**
(d) **Where does it sit in the precedence lattice, relative to every other artifact?**

LXXIX.3 An artifact for which any of the four questions is unanswerable IS **incompletely governed**, and its presence blocks the readiness certification of Article LXXX.

LXXIX.4 Completeness verification SHALL be **mechanical** and SHALL be realized by the validator of Article L. A completeness claim not produced by the validator IS an assertion, not a verification.

LXXIX.5 Completeness verification SHALL be **re-run on every change** to any constitutional artifact, registry, delegation, or namespace. A completeness result IS valid only for the repository state that produced it, and SHALL record that state.

LXXIX.6 Completeness SHALL be reported as a **finding list, not a score**. A percentage conceals which obligations are unmet; an enumeration does not.

LXXIX.7 Completeness verification SHALL include a **conformance check of this instrument against its own commissioning mandate**: every mandated section of the conformance map SHALL resolve to a present Article. This is checked by L.3(a).

LXXIX.8 Completeness SHALL NOT be certified while any CMG-R-15 (CONFLICTS-WITH) relationship, undispositioned finding, expired exception, or unclassified impact remains.
