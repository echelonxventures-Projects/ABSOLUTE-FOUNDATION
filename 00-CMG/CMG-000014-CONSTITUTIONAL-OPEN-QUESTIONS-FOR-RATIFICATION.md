## CMG-OQ-01 — Which authority is competent to ratify constitutional artifacts?

**The question.** CMG-000001 declares its own status PROPOSED and its standing PROVISIONAL. Ratification operation is owned by the located ratification constitution, but the corpus records that no ratification authority exists within the frozen corpus, and that the identity of any out-of-corpus finality authority is undetermined.

**Why it is not decided here.** Self-ratification is prohibited (CMG-000001 XLIV.7), and no artifact may grant itself or another ratification competence (CMG-L-04). An instrument that named its own ratifier would be ratifying itself through an intermediary.

**What an answer looks like.** A recorded designation of the authority — human, institutional, or otherwise — that may accept a validated and certified constitutional artifact into the corpus, together with the evidence form its acceptance takes.

**What turns on it.** Every artifact in the corpus. Nothing can currently be ratified.

**Present disposition.** `PROVISIONAL` standing under CMG-L-12, recorded rather than concealed.

## CMG-OQ-02 — Which artifact, if any, occupies Tier T1?

**The question.** The located charter declares itself subordinate to "the ratified UCOS Ω∞ Constitution" and places it at the top of its authority tiers. Searching the repository locates constitutional source material in the frozen source corpus as binary documents, classified as frozen source rather than ratified normative law. No ratified normative artifact occupies the tier.

**Why it is not decided here.** T1 governs **substance**. CMG-000001 has no substantive jurisdiction (Article XIX.2). Designating an occupant would be an allocation of substantive authority, and promoting a located artifact into the vacancy would be self-elevation by proxy (XVII.4).

**What an answer looks like.** Either (a) a designated artifact recorded as the T1 occupant, or (b) a ratified determination that T1 is permanently vacant, with the consequence for dependent standing stated.

**What turns on it.** The standing of all 43 recognized artifacts. The vacancy propagates through the entire dependency graph, so nothing holds non-provisional standing until it closes (CMG-000004 §7).

**Present disposition.** Recorded as vacancy `VAC-01` with the four-step closure procedure of CMG-000001 XVII.4.

## CMG-OQ-03 — Is the meta axis correctly declared orthogonal to the process axis?

**The question.** CMG-000001 places itself at tier T1M and declares `T1M ⟂ T2` — the recognition axis is orthogonal to Program Authority, neither superior nor subordinate. The alternative readings are that the meta layer is superior to process (because it defines what counts as constitutional), or subordinate to it (because process governs how all constitutional work is done).

**Why orthogonality was chosen.** The located charter claims supremacy over its own program while explicitly disclaiming authority over constitutional content, and explicitly prohibits its own self-elevation. Recognition and process are therefore genuinely different questions with genuinely different owners. Declaring a rank between them would require an authority above both — which is `CMG-OQ-02`, presently vacant.

**Why it is not decided here.** A rank declaration between two axes cannot be made by either axis (CMG-000001 XVII.8).

**What an answer looks like.** A ratified determination that the axes are orthogonal, or a ratified rank ordering between them.

**What turns on it.** The finality of the precedence lattice. If orthogonality is rejected, CMG-000001 Article XVI requires a MAJOR amendment — not a reinterpretation.

## CMG-OQ-04 — Who owns the Deferral Register lifecycle? — **CLOSED**

> **Status: CLOSED (Wave-2 · Mission E-3).** Answer: **`CEP-002`, by its Article 27.** Allocated by Governance Authority — the competent allocating authority under CEP-002 Art 1.2, 7.2, 14.2 — through amendment `CEP-002-AMD-001` under CEP-002's own Article 21 (v1.0 → 1.1). Enabled by the prerequisite amendment `CEP-001-AMD-001` (CEP-001 ADDENDUM B, v1.0 → 1.1), which added the DEFERRED exit transitions the lifecycle requires. Recorded by CMG-000001 as recognition only, at CMG-DLG-49 and LXXVIII.8. `CMG-GAP-02` is fully CLOSED. This question required no ratification act beyond the allocating authority's own; it never blocked the readiness ceiling, which remains set by `CMG-OQ-01` and `CMG-OQ-02`.

**The question.** A Deferral Register is referenced across multiple located instruments as the destination for out-of-scope discoveries, deferred findings, and matters knowingly not decided. No located instrument defined its states, entry conditions, exit conditions, review cadence, or owner. The corpus's own foundation review records this and recommends consolidation.

**Why it was not decided here.** CMG-000001 recognizes the Deferral Register as constitutional Kind `CMG-K-24` and binds deferrals to it, which is the limit of a recognition act. Selecting the owner is an ownership allocation and requires the approval of the allocating authority (XLVI.2) — so the allocation was performed by that authority, and CMG-000001 recorded it.

**What an answer looked like.** An amendment to an existing governance or evolution instrument that adds the register's lifecycle, or a recorded allocation to a new instrument. **What was done:** the former — extension of the located governance constitution, with no new instrument, register, or store.

**What turned on it.** Full closure of `CMG-GAP-02`. Until closed, deferrals were recorded in a register whose lifecycle nobody governed — matters could enter and never be reviewed out. CEP-002 Article 27 now guarantees the opposite: every entry carries a declared review point, no entry may exit without review (27.11), and no entry may exit by silence, lapse, or unilateral action (27.12).

**Non-binding view (as recorded, and as followed).** Extend the located governance constitution, which already owns deferral disposition. Extension is preferred to creation (CMG-000001 XLI.7).

## CMG-OQ-05 — Who owns program-completion ceremony?

**The question.** The located operational constitution defines a program-level COMPLETE state with an entry predicate. No located instrument defines the act by which completion is performed, recorded, or attested.

**Why it is not decided here.** Completion is a process act, and process is owned supremely by the Program Authority axis (CMG-000001 LXXXI.3). Legislating a completion ceremony in a meta instrument would be a jurisdiction breach, void by operation of CMG-L-13.

**What an answer looks like.** A process-owner determination or instrument defining the completion act, its authority, its evidence, and its irreversibility.

**What turns on it.** `CMG-GAP-06`. A reachable state with no act that performs it is an unperformable obligation (CMG-000001 XXIII.5).

## CMG-OQ-06 — Does admission of the `CMG` namespace and zone require amendment of a located registration or classification instrument?

**The question.** Recognition of `00-CMG/` was achieved without editing shared machinery: the constitution and analyses classify through an existing filename rule, the tools classify through the repository's total path-derived classifier, and every artifact self-declares its program, category and volume. Whether the registration authority nonetheless requires a formal amendment to acknowledge a new zone and a new namespace is that authority's call.

**Why it is not decided here.** The registration and classification authority is a located owner (`CMG-DLG-13`). Determining its own requirements is its concern, not the meta layer's.

**What an answer looks like.** A determination by the registration authority: either acknowledgement that metadata-driven recognition suffices, or a required amendment naming the zone and namespace.

**What turns on it.** Nothing functional — recognition already works and was verified. The question is one of formal record.

## CMG-OQ-07 — Shall the twelve meta invariants be adopted corpus-wide, or remain invariants of the meta layer only?

**The question.** `CMG-INV-01` … `CMG-INV-12` are presently invariants of the meta-constitutional layer. Adopting them corpus-wide would make every constitutional artifact mechanically checkable against recognition, single authority, non-orphan governance, resolvable superiors, acyclicity, decidable precedence, lifecycle legality, identity uniqueness, no finite ceilings, evidence reproducibility, preservation, and jurisdiction containment.

**Why it is not decided here.** Corpus-wide adoption imposes new obligations on artifacts owned elsewhere. CMG-000001 has no authority to impose obligations outside its jurisdiction (Article XIX.2), and doing so would be a MAJOR change requiring the ratification path and a migration plan.

**What an answer looks like.** A ratified determination of scope, and if corpus-wide, adoption of the migration plan in CMG-000008 §3.1.

**What turns on it.** Whether constitutional conformance is mechanically enforced across the whole corpus or only across the meta layer.

**Non-binding view.** Do not pursue until `CMG-OQ-01` and `CMG-OQ-02` are closed. Imposing corpus-wide obligations derived from an unratified instrument would compound the provisionality rather than resolve it.
