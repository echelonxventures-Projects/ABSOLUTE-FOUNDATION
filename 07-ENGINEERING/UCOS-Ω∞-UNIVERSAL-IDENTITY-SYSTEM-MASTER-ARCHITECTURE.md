## DELIVERABLE 1 — EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Universal Identity System (UIS) is the permanent identity foundation of UCOS Ω∞. Its single governing proposition is absolute:

> **EVERYTHING SHALL HAVE A UNIVERSAL UNIQUE IDENTITY. If an object exists, it possesses exactly one permanent Universal Identity. There shall never exist an unidentified object anywhere inside UCOS.**

The UIS achieves this through a small number of durable engineering commitments:

1. **Separation of identity from everything mutable.** A Universal Identity (UID) is distinct from an object's name, location, address, state, version, owner, and representation. Those may all change; the UID never does. This separation is the root of permanence and future-proofing.
2. **A partitioned, collision-free identity space.** The identity space is conceptually infinite and partitioned by allocation authority and namespace, so that any number of allocators — online, offline, or federated — can mint identities independently and concurrently with a mathematically negligible, and structurally bounded, collision probability. No central bottleneck is required for allocation.
3. **A reflexive meta-model.** The identity scheme describes itself: object types, namespaces, dictionaries, registries, relationships, and the UID scheme itself each possess a Universal Identity. The system has no unidentified internal element.
4. **Resolution as a first-class service.** Any UID resolves — deterministically, verifiably, and independently of storage technology — to the current descriptor, location(s), owner(s), lifecycle state, relationships, and provenance of the object it denotes.
5. **Registries and dictionaries as the system of record.** A federation of registries records identities and their metadata; a system of dictionaries defines the controlled vocabularies (object types, relationships, statuses, etc.). Every dictionary entry and every registry itself carries a UID.
6. **Immutability with governed lifecycle.** Identities are immutable and never reused. Objects move through a governed lifecycle (proposed → active → deprecated → retired → archived) without ever re-identifying, renaming the token, or reclaiming the identifier.
7. **Integrity, security, audit, and federation by construction.** Every identity is tamper-evident, access-controlled, fully audited, and federatable across repositories, organizations, platforms, and clouds — with no secret ever embedded in identity data (RR-07).

The UIS is **implementation-independent**: it specifies properties, invariants, and architecture, not encodings, protocols, or products. It is **authority-neutral**: every identity it defines is a technical artifact conferring no constitutional or governance standing (ID-01, AUTH-06). It is **complete for the corpus**: it is consistent with, and provides the engineering foundation beneath, the identity structures already registered (CAT-DATA-001's 10-class catalog; IMP-005's 15 canonical identity classes) and realized across the ecosystem. The result is a foundation that **never requires redesign because of growth, never exhausts, never renumbers, and never permits a duplicate permanent identity.**

## DELIVERABLE 2 — VISION

The UIS exists so that UCOS Ω∞ can grow without bound while remaining perfectly referenceable. The vision has five horizons:

- **Universality.** Nothing is too small (an attribute, a constant, a log line) or too large (a universe, a reality layer, an entire deployment) to receive a Universal Identity. Coverage is total and exception-free.
- **Permanence.** An identity, once allocated, is valid for the lifetime of UCOS. It survives migrations, refactors, mergers, technology changes, organizational changes, and paradigm shifts. Systems, storage engines, languages, and clouds may all be replaced; the identities persist unchanged.
- **Independence.** The foundation binds to no technology. It can be realized on any storage, any language, any protocol, any cloud, or a future computing paradigm not yet imagined, without altering a single identity.
- **Federation.** Independent repositories, organizations, and platforms allocate, hold, and exchange identities autonomously, yet the global identity space remains coherent and collision-free. Offline and disconnected operation is a first-class case, not an exception.
- **Self-description.** The system is reflexive and discoverable: given any identity, one can traverse to its type, its dictionary definitions, its relationships, its owners, its dependencies, and its provenance — and every one of those is itself identified.

The North Star: **a single, permanent, universal act of identification that need never be repeated, redesigned, or reconciled.**

## DELIVERABLE 4 — UNIVERSAL IDENTITY THEORY

The UIS rests on a small, rigorous theory of identity. It is stated conceptually and independently of any technology.

### 4.1 The five distinct notions

The theory's foundation is the strict separation of five notions that lesser systems conflate:

| Notion | Definition | Cardinality | Mutability |
|--------|-----------|-------------|------------|
| **Identity** | The fact that an object is a single distinct thing, numerically distinct from all others, persisting through change. | One per object, forever. | Immutable (the object *is* itself). |
| **Identifier (UID)** | The permanent token that *denotes* the identity — the name the system uses to refer to the object. | One per object, forever. | Immutable. |
| **Name / Label** | A human-friendly designation of the object. | Zero-to-many, non-unique. | Mutable. |
| **Locator / Address** | Where a representation of the object currently resides (repository, node, path, endpoint). | Zero-to-many. | Mutable. |
| **Reference** | A pointer from one object to another, expressed *using the UID*. | Many. | Mutable set; each edge itself identifiable. |

**Central theorem of the UIS:** *Identity is invariant under every change to name, location, address, state, version, owner, representation, and technology.* Because the UID denotes the identity — not any of these mutable facets — the UID is stable for the object's entire existence. This single separation is what makes the system permanent and future-proof.

### 4.2 Identity criteria (what makes objects "the same" or "different")

- **Numerical identity:** two references denote the same object iff they carry the same UID. Equality of identity is decided by the UID alone, never by attribute comparison.
- **Persistence through change:** an object remains the same object (same UID) across any mutation of its attributes, including a total change of representation or a migration across technologies.
- **Distinctness of copies:** a *copy*, *replica*, *version*, *mirror*, or *derivation* of an object is a **distinct object** with its own UID, related to the source by an explicit, identified relationship (COPY-OF, VERSION-OF, MIRRORS, DERIVED-FROM). The UIS never silently equates a copy with its source.
- **Composite identity:** an object composed of parts (e.g., a document containing sections) has its own UID; each part has its own UID; the whole–part structure is expressed by identified CONTAINS/CONTAINED-BY relationships.

### 4.3 The identity space

- The identity space is **conceptually unbounded** — it is not a fixed-width numeric range but an extensible, partitioned space. It cannot be exhausted by any realizable growth of UCOS.
- The space is **partitioned by allocation authority and namespace**, so that disjoint authorities occupy disjoint sub-spaces. Two authorities cannot mint the same UID because their partitions are disjoint by construction (Deliverable 12/13).
- Within a partition, uniqueness is guaranteed by a **collision-free uniqueness component** (an authority-local monotonic and/or high-entropy element). The theory requires the *property* of collision-freedom; it does not mandate any particular generator.
- The space supports **offline and federated allocation**: because partitions are disjoint, an authority may allocate while disconnected and still never collide with another authority.

### 4.4 Object existence and identification obligation

- **Existence implies identity (UIS-P-01).** The moment an object comes into existence within UCOS — is proposed, created, imported, generated, or discovered — it acquires a UID. There is no interval of existence without identity.
- **Prospective identity.** Objects that *may* exist (planned, designed, or generatable) may be assigned a UID upon definition; the UID's lifecycle state (Deliverable 30) records that it is not yet realized. This lets the corpus reference future objects safely.
- **No anonymous internals.** Types, namespaces, dictionaries, registries, relationships, and the scheme itself are objects and therefore identified (reflexivity, UIS-P-10).

### 4.5 Identity, trust, and authority (boundary)

Identity answers **"which object is this?"** It does **not** answer "is this actor trustworthy?" (trust — ARCH-SECURITY-001), "what may this actor do?" (authorization), or "does this actor hold standing?" (a constitutional question the UIS never touches). Per ID-01/AUTH-06, **possessing an identity confers no authority of any kind.** Authentication *binds a claimant to an identity*; authorization *permits actions*; trust *rates* an identity; none of these is identity itself, and none is constitutive.

## DELIVERABLE 7 — UNIVERSAL IDENTITY META-MODEL

The meta-model defines the *structure of identity itself* — the model of which all concrete identities are instances. It is deliberately reflexive: the meta-model is described using the very constructs it defines, and each construct is identified.

### 7.1 Meta-levels

| Meta-level | Content | Example | Identified? |
|-----------|---------|---------|-------------|
| **M3 — Meta-meta (Scheme)** | The UID scheme and the meta-model itself. | "The UIS UID scheme, v-scheme". | Yes — the scheme has a UID. |
| **M2 — Meta (Types)** | Object Classes, Namespaces, Dictionaries, Relationship Types, Status vocabularies. | "Object Class: Service". | Yes — each type has a UID. |
| **M1 — Model (Objects)** | Concrete identified objects. | "Service SVC-000123". | Yes — each object has a UID. |
| **M0 — Instance/Runtime** | Runtime occurrences/representations of M1 objects. | "the running instance of SVC-000123 in region R". | Occurrences resolve to the M1 UID + locator. |

The meta-model is **strictly stratified but reflexively closed**: M3 elements are themselves M1 objects of the "Scheme/Meta-model" object classes, so nothing escapes identification (UIL-11).

### 7.2 The Universal Identity Record (meta-structure)

Every identity is represented by a **Universal Identity Record (UIR)** — a technology-independent descriptor. The UIR is defined here as a *conceptual structure of fields*, not a schema. Its mandatory field-groups:

| Field-group | Purpose | Mutability |
|-------------|---------|-----------|
| **UID** | The permanent identifier token (Deliverable 8.1 composition). | Immutable |
| **Object Class** | The type(s) of the object (M2 reference by UID). | Immutable class binding; class set may only be extended, never contradicted |
| **Namespace** | The namespace to which the object belongs (by UID). | Immutable |
| **Origin Authority** | The allocation authority that minted the UID (by UID). | Immutable |
| **Names** | Human-friendly labels, localized. | Mutable |
| **Locators** | Current representation locations. | Mutable |
| **Lifecycle State** | Current governed state (Deliverable 30). | Mutable (governed) |
| **Ownership** | Accountable owner(s) by UID (Deliverable 19). | Mutable (governed) |
| **Relationships** | Identified edges to other UIDs (Deliverable 18). | Mutable set |
| **Dependencies** | Inward dependency edges (Deliverable 20). | Mutable set |
| **Version/Lineage** | Version and provenance links (Deliverables 21/22). | Append-only |
| **Metadata** | Controlled metadata (dictionary-typed). | Mutable |
| **Classification** | Data/security classification (ARCH-DATA-001 8 levels). | Governed |
| **Integrity Evidence** | Tamper-evidence for the record (Deliverable 25). | Append-only |
| **Certification State** | Readiness/certification (ARCH-CERT-001). | Governed |
| **Provenance/Audit** | Append-only history of all changes (Deliverable 26). | Append-only |

**Invariant:** the *UID, Object Class binding, Namespace, and Origin Authority* are immutable; everything else is a governed, versioned, append-only-audited mutable facet. This is the structural expression of UIL-04/07/13/14.

### 7.3 Reflexive closure

- The UIR structure is itself an Object Class (M2) with a UID.
- Each field-group is a dictionary-defined construct with a UID.
- The UID scheme (M3) has a UID and a version; scheme evolution is versioned (Deliverable 21) and compatibility-preserving (UIL-19).

## DELIVERABLE 8 — UNIVERSAL IDENTITY OBJECT MODEL

The object model specifies how concrete objects are typed, composed, and identified. It is the M1/M2 realization of the meta-model.

### 8.1 UID composition (conceptual, non-encoding)

A UID is an **opaque token** with a *conceptual* internal structure. Consumers depend on the token as a whole (UIS-P-06); the internal structure exists to guarantee the invariants, not to be parsed by consumers.

| Conceptual segment | Guarantees | Notes |
|--------------------|-----------|-------|
| **Authority segment** | Partitions the space by allocation authority (UIL-16). | A UID for the authority itself; disjoint per authority. |
| **Namespace segment** | Situates the UID in a namespace (Deliverable 12). | Reference by UID. |
| **Object-class hint** | Optional, non-authoritative class hint for routing/sharding. | Non-authoritative: the authoritative class is in the UIR, obtained by resolution — never by parsing. |
| **Uniqueness component** | Collision-free within (authority, namespace). | Property required (monotonic and/or high-entropy); generator unspecified. |
| **Integrity component** | Detects accidental corruption/transcription error. | A check element; not a security signature (that is in Integrity Evidence). |
| **Scheme-version marker** | Enables compatibility-preserving evolution (UIL-19). | Older UIDs remain valid forever. |

The document specifies these as **required properties**, not as a byte layout, field order, length, or alphabet. Any realization that satisfies the properties (uniqueness, opacity, permanence, authority-partitioning, integrity-checkability, versionability) is conformant. **No encoding, format, or generation algorithm is selected here** (UIS-P-07).

### 8.2 Object classes (typing)

- Every object is an **INSTANCE-OF** at least one **Object Class**; classes form a **SUBTYPE-OF** hierarchy rooted at `IdentifiableThing`.
- The canonical concrete classes are those already registered: the **CAT-DATA-001 10-class identity catalog** and the **IMP-005 15 canonical identity classes** for actor/security identity, plus the ARCH/CAT asset classes (universe, domain, capability, component, data entity, event, API, workflow, service, application, and their sub-taxonomies) for structural identity. ENG-001 **registers no new class**; it organizes the existing set under `IdentifiableThing` (UIS-P-18, UIL-20).
- Multiple classification is permitted (an object may be both a "File" and a "Generated Artifact"); the class *set* may be extended over time but never contradicted, and never in a way that changes the UID (UIL-07).

### 8.3 Object composition

- **Atomic objects** (e.g., a constant, a column) have a UID and no identified parts of interest, though they may participate in relationships.
- **Composite objects** (e.g., a document, a database, a cluster) have a UID and identified parts, each with its own UID, linked by CONTAINS/PART-OF.
- **Aggregate/collection objects** (e.g., a catalog, a suite, a package) group members by identified MEMBER-OF edges; membership is mutable and audited.
- Composition never implies identity sharing: a part is never the same object as its whole (Deliverable 4.2).

### 8.4 Object model conformance rules

1. Every object presents a valid UIR (Deliverable 7.2) with all immutable field-groups populated at creation.
2. Every object binds to ≥1 Object Class and exactly one Namespace and Origin Authority.
3. Every object is reachable by resolution from its UID (UIL-08) and by discovery from its registry (Deliverable 17).
4. No object shares a UID with any other (UIL-03); no object holds two UIDs (UIL-02).
5. Copies, versions, mirrors, and derivations are distinct objects with explicit provenance edges (Deliverable 4.2 / 22).

## DELIVERABLE 9 — UNIVERSAL IDENTITY ARCHITECTURE

The identity architecture defines the logical building blocks of the UIS and how they cooperate. It is a *logical* architecture: components are roles and contracts, not products or services in any technology.

### 9.1 Logical components

| Component | Responsibility |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Identity Space** | The conceptual, partitioned, unbounded space of all UIDs (Deliverable 4.3). |
| **Allocation Authority** | A role that mints UIDs within its partition (Deliverable 13). |
| **Namespace Authority** | A role that governs a namespace and its allocation delegation (Deliverable 12). |
| **Identity Registry** | The system of record for UIRs (Deliverable 10). |
| **Dictionary System** | The controlled vocabularies (Deliverable 11). |
| **Resolution Service** | Maps a UID → current UIR/descriptor (Deliverable 14). |
| **Validation Service** | Enforces well-formedness, uniqueness, and law-conformance (Deliverable 15). |
| **Search & Discovery Service** | Finds identities by metadata/relationships (Deliverables 16/17). |
| **Federation Fabric** | Coordinates authorities and registries across boundaries (Deliverable 27). |
| **Integrity & Audit Service** | Provides tamper-evidence and immutable history (Deliverables 25/26). |
| **Governance Function** | Administers types, namespaces, and policies (Deliverable 23). |

### 9.2 Architectural layering

```
                 Governance Function            (types, namespaces, policies — record-only)
                         │
   ┌─────────────┬───────┴────────┬───────────────┬───────────────┐
   │             │                │               │               │
 Allocation   Namespace       Dictionary       Federation      Integrity &
 Authority    Authority        System           Fabric          Audit
   │             │                │               │               │
   └──────┬──────┴────────┬───────┴───────┬───────┴───────────────┘
          │               │               │
     Identity Registry (system of record — Universal Identity Records)
          │               │               │
   Resolution        Validation      Search & Discovery
     Service          Service            Service
          │
     Consumers (every ARCH/CAT/REF/GEN/IMP artifact and future subsystem)
```

### 9.3 Architectural invariants

- **Registry is authoritative; resolution is derived.** The registry holds the truth (append-only UIRs); resolution and search are consistent derivations of it (UIS-P-22 declares each read's consistency requirement).
- **Allocation is decentralizable; the registry is federatable.** Neither allocation nor the registry requires a single global instance; both operate as a federation (Deliverables 13/27).
- **All internal components are identified** (UIL-11) and expose only governed, authenticated, authorized, audited interfaces.
- **No component confers authority** (UIL-17); every operation is non-constitutive (ID-01).

### 9.4 Consumer contract

Downstream artifacts interact with the UIS solely through four abstract contracts — **Allocate**, **Resolve**, **Validate**, **Discover** — plus the read-only **Federation** and **Audit** views. Each contract is defined by inputs, outputs, invariants, and consistency requirements; none prescribes a protocol or transport (formalized by the IMP-009 API Platform at implementation time, not here).

## DELIVERABLE 10 — UNIVERSAL REGISTRY ARCHITECTURE

The registry is the system of record for identities and their metadata. The mission enumerates many registries; the UIS organizes them as a **federated family of registries with a single logical contract**.

### 10.1 Registry family

Each of the following is a specialized registry, itself an `IdentifiableThing` with a UID, sharing one common registry contract:

Identity Registry · Namespace Registry · Object Registry · Object-Type Registry · Relationship Registry · Reference Registry · Dictionary Registry · Ownership Registry · Dependency Registry · Lifecycle Registry · Version Registry · Metadata Registry · Security Registry · Compliance Registry · Certification Registry · Audit Registry · Integrity Registry · Federation Registry · Extension Registry · Discovery Registry · Search Registry.

These are consistent with — and are the engineering generalization of — the 25 canonical registries already established by IMP-004 (the Registry Platform). ENG-001 defines the *architecture*; IMP-004 holds the *realized* canonical registries. No canonical registry is added or renamed here (UIL-20).

### 10.2 Common registry contract

Every registry SHALL provide:

- **Register** — admit a new record with complete mandatory fields, allocated UID, owner, and traceability; reject incomplete or duplicate records (Validation, Deliverable 15).
- **Read/Resolve** — return a record by UID with a declared consistency level.
- **Amend** — append a new governed version of mutable facets (never in-place erasure; UIL-13).
- **Transition** — advance lifecycle state under governance (Deliverable 30).
- **Query/Discover** — find records by metadata/relationship (Deliverables 16/17).
- **Attest** — return integrity evidence and audit history (Deliverables 25/26).

### 10.3 Registry properties

- **Append-only and versioned** (UIL-13/14): records accrete history; corrections are versions.
- **Recording, never ratifying** (RG-02): a registry entry records an engineering fact and enacts nothing (ID-01).
- **Federated** (Deliverable 27): registries may be partitioned by authority/namespace and reconciled; the logical contract hides the partitioning from consumers.
- **Consistency-explicit** (UIS-P-22): registration and uniqueness checks are strongly consistent within an authority's partition; cross-partition views may be eventually consistent, and each read declares which.
- **Recoverable** (UIS-P-23 / ARCH-BCDR-001): replicated, backed up, and restorable without re-identifying any object.

### 10.4 Registry integrity rules

No duplicate UID; no ownerless record; no orphan (every record traceable to type/authority/namespace); no stale reference; no unversioned mutation; no secret in any field (UIL-15).

## DELIVERABLE 11 — UNIVERSAL DICTIONARY ARCHITECTURE

Dictionaries define the **controlled vocabularies** the UIS uses. Every dictionary and every dictionary entry is itself an `IdentifiableThing` with a UID (mission mandate; UIL-11).

### 11.1 Dictionary family

Object Dictionary · Object-Type Dictionary · Namespace Dictionary · Domain Dictionary · Capability Dictionary · Relationship Dictionary · Reference Dictionary · Lifecycle Dictionary · Status Dictionary · Version Dictionary · Ownership Dictionary · Dependency Dictionary · Security Dictionary · Permission Dictionary · Policy Dictionary · Role Dictionary · Metadata Dictionary · Language Dictionary · Localization Dictionary · Country Dictionary · Currency Dictionary · Time Dictionary · Unit Dictionary · Event Dictionary · Message Dictionary · Error Dictionary · Validation Dictionary · Compliance Dictionary · Certification Dictionary · Registry Dictionary.

### 11.2 Dictionary entry model

Each entry carries: **UID · Dictionary (by UID) · Canonical Term · Definition · Localized Labels · Status (Deliverable 30) · Version (Deliverable 21) · Ownership · Provenance.** Entries are governed reference data (ARCH-DATA-001 reference-data model): versioned, owned, classified, and append-only.

### 11.3 Dictionary rules

- **Controlled vocabulary is authoritative.** Metadata fields that are dictionary-typed SHALL take values only from the referenced dictionary; free-text is admitted only in explicitly free-text fields (e.g., human names, descriptions).
- **Localization is separate from identity.** A term's localized labels (Language/Localization/Country dictionaries) are mutable presentation, never identity (UIL-07).
- **Dictionaries are reflexive.** The Registry Dictionary describes the registry family; the Object-Type Dictionary describes Object Classes; each dictionary entry has a UID.
- **Extension, not redefinition.** New terms are added by extension; a canonical term's meaning is never silently redefined (UIL-18); deprecations follow the lifecycle model.

## DELIVERABLE 12 — UNIVERSAL NAMESPACE ARCHITECTURE

Namespaces partition the identity space for delegation, organization, and federation without threatening global uniqueness.

### 12.1 Namespace model

- A **Namespace** is an identified container that scopes allocation and organization. Namespaces form a **hierarchy** (a namespace CONTAINS sub-namespaces), rooted at a single universal root namespace.
- Each namespace has a **Namespace Authority** (Deliverable 13) responsible for governing allocation within it and for delegating sub-namespaces.
- Namespaces are **immutable in identity** (a namespace has a permanent UID) but **mutable in membership and delegation** (governed, audited).

### 12.2 Namespaces and uniqueness

- Global uniqueness is guaranteed by the composition **(Authority, Namespace, Uniqueness component)** being disjoint across authorities and namespaces (Deliverable 8.1). A namespace never overlaps another's allocation partition.
- Namespaces enable **local autonomy**: a repository, organization, or platform operates its own namespace(s) and allocates freely within them, online or offline, without collision (UIL-16).

### 12.3 Namespace rules

- **No name-based identity.** A namespace path is a human-facing organizing label; the authoritative identity is the namespace UID (UIL-07/09).
- **Delegation is explicit and revocable-in-governance.** A parent namespace authority delegates a sub-namespace by an identified, audited grant; revocation never reclaims already-allocated UIDs (UIL-05).
- **Domain tagging honored.** Where the corpus requires domain/family tagging (RAT-10), namespaces carry the corresponding domain tags as governed metadata, not as identity.
- **Reflexive.** The Namespace Registry and Namespace Dictionary are themselves in a namespace with UIDs.

## DELIVERABLE 13 — UNIVERSAL ALLOCATION ARCHITECTURE

Allocation is the act of minting a new UID for a newly-existing object. It is designed for unlimited, decentralized, offline-capable, collision-free operation.

### 13.1 Allocation authorities

- An **Allocation Authority** is an identified role permitted to mint UIDs within one or more namespace partitions. Authorities may be organizational, per-repository, per-platform, or per-node.
- Authorities are arranged in a **delegation tree** rooted at the universal root authority; each authority holds a disjoint partition (Deliverable 12.2), so concurrent minting never collides.

### 13.2 Allocation contract

**Allocate(objectClass, namespace, requestedMetadata) → UID + provisional UIR.** The contract SHALL:

1. Verify the requester is an authorized allocation authority for the target namespace (authorization only — non-constitutive; ID-01).
2. Mint a UID whose uniqueness component is collision-free within (authority, namespace).
3. Create a provisional UIR with immutable field-groups populated (UID, Object Class, Namespace, Origin Authority) and lifecycle state = Proposed/Reserved (Deliverable 30).
4. Emit an append-only allocation audit event (Deliverable 26).

### 13.3 Allocation modes

| Mode | Description | Consistency |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Online-central** | Authority mints against a live registry partition. | Strong within partition. |
| **Distributed** | Many authorities mint concurrently in disjoint partitions. | Strong per partition; globally coherent by disjointness. |
| **Offline** | A disconnected authority mints from a pre-delegated partition/quota and reconciles later. | Locally strong; reconciliation is append-only and cannot collide. |
| **Federated** | Independent organizations mint in their own root-delegated namespaces. | Coherent by partition disjointness (UIL-16). |
| **Bulk/Generated** | A generator mints many UIDs deterministically for generated assets (e.g., the CAT formula-derived assets). | Deterministic; still unique by (authority, namespace, uniqueness component). |

### 13.4 Allocation guarantees

- **No exhaustion.** The uniqueness component space per partition is effectively inexhaustible; new partitions are always available by delegation (UIL-18).
- **No collision.** Disjoint partitions + collision-free uniqueness component ⇒ zero global collision (UIL-03/16).
- **No coordination bottleneck.** Minting requires no global lock; only partition delegation is coordinated, and that is rare and governed.
- **No reuse.** A minted UID is never returned to the pool, even if the object is never realized or is later retired (UIL-05).

## DELIVERABLE 14 — UNIVERSAL RESOLUTION ARCHITECTURE

Resolution maps a UID to the current truth about the object it denotes. It is the counterpart to allocation and the guarantor of referential integrity.

### 14.1 Resolution contract

**Resolve(UID, [asOfVersion], [consistency]) → UIR view (descriptor, class, namespace, locators, state, owners, relationships, provenance).** The contract SHALL:

- Return the **current** UIR by default, or a **historical** version if `asOfVersion` is supplied (UIL-13 makes history addressable).
- Honor the requested **consistency level** (strong within an authority's partition; eventual across the federation), and label the response accordingly (UIS-P-22).
- Return a **definitive "unknown/unallocated"** answer for a well-formed UID that was never allocated — distinct from "allocated but retired" (Deliverable 30).
- Never fabricate, and never return a secret (UIL-15).

### 14.2 Resolution properties

- **Deterministic:** the same query against the same registry state yields the same result.
- **Location-independent:** resolution returns *current* locators; callers never hard-code locations (UIL-07/09).
- **Federated:** a UID minted by any authority resolves via the federation fabric (Deliverable 27); the resolving party need not be the minting party.
- **Verifiable:** a resolution result can be checked against integrity evidence (Deliverable 25).
- **Version-aware:** resolution can target the latest version, a specific version, or the lineage (Deliverable 21/22).

### 14.3 Resolution failure semantics

Malformed UID → validation error (Deliverable 15). Well-formed but unallocated → "unallocated". Allocated but not yet realized → "reserved/proposed". Retired/archived → returns the record with its terminal state (identifier still valid and resolvable; UIL-05/12). Unreachable partition → "temporarily unresolvable" with retry/failover semantics (never a false "unknown").

## DELIVERABLE 15 — UNIVERSAL VALIDATION ARCHITECTURE

Validation enforces that every identity and every operation conforms to the laws (Deliverable 5) and the object model (Deliverable 8).

### 15.1 Validation dimensions

| Dimension | Checks |
|-----------|--------|
| **Well-formedness** | UID satisfies the required properties (Deliverable 8.1); integrity component checks out. |
| **Uniqueness** | No existing object holds this UID (UIL-03); the object holds no other UID (UIL-02). |
| **Class conformance** | Object binds to a valid, registered Object Class; class set is non-contradictory (UIL-20). |
| **Completeness** | All mandatory immutable UIR field-groups present at creation (Deliverable 7.2). |
| **Traceability** | Origin authority, namespace, owner, and lineage resolvable (UIL-10). |
| **Reference integrity** | Every reference resolves to an allocated UID (UIL-09); no dangling edges. |
| **Lifecycle validity** | Requested transition is permitted from the current state (Deliverable 30). |
| **Classification** | Exactly one data/security classification present (ARCH-DATA-001). |
| **No embedded secret** | No field contains secret/credential/key material (UIL-15). |
| **Non-constitutive** | No field encodes authority/sovereignty/constituent standing (UIL-17). |

### 15.2 Validation timing

- **Pre-allocation:** authority and namespace checks.
- **At registration:** well-formedness, uniqueness, completeness, class, classification, no-secret, non-constitutive.
- **At amendment/transition:** immutability of immutable field-groups, lifecycle validity, reference integrity, versioning.
- **Continuous:** integrity re-verification and orphan/dangling-reference sweeps (Deliverable 25/26).

### 15.3 Validation outcomes

Pass → operation proceeds. Fail → operation is rejected, void, and produces a Gap Report/audit event (ARCH-GOV-001 Law 003); no partial or "best-effort" identity is ever persisted. Validation **records readiness only** and enacts nothing (ID-01).

## DELIVERABLE 16 — UNIVERSAL SEARCH ARCHITECTURE

Search finds identities by their metadata, classes, relationships, and provenance — without ever becoming an alternative source of identity truth.

### 16.1 Search model

- Search operates over **derived indexes** of the registry (UIS-P-22: search is an eventually-consistent derivation; the registry remains authoritative).
- Searchable dimensions: Object Class, Namespace, Owner, Lifecycle State, Classification, Metadata (dictionary-typed), Relationships, Dependencies, Version/Lineage, Origin Authority, and free-text Names/Descriptions.
- Results carry the UID; consumers **resolve** the UID for authoritative current state (Deliverable 14). Search never returns identity truth that bypasses resolution.

### 16.2 Search properties

- **Scoped by authorization:** results are filtered to what the requester may see (least privilege; ARCH-SECURITY-001), and never leak restricted metadata or secrets (UIL-15).
- **Federated:** search spans federated registries (Deliverable 27) with per-source consistency labels.
- **Deterministic ranking contract:** ranking criteria are declared; identity correctness never depends on ranking.
- **No side effects:** search is read-only and audited as access (Deliverable 26).

## DELIVERABLE 17 — UNIVERSAL DISCOVERY ARCHITECTURE

Discovery is the guarantee that **every identified object can be found and navigated** — the operational face of universal coverage.

### 17.1 Discovery guarantees

- **Enumerability:** every allocated UID is enumerable through its registry/namespace (subject to authorization).
- **Navigability:** from any UID one can traverse to its class, dictionaries, owners, relationships, dependencies, versions, and provenance — all by UID (reflexive graph).
- **No dark objects:** an object that exists but is not discoverable is a failure condition; discovery sweeps detect and report orphaned or unindexed identities (Deliverable 33).

### 17.2 Discovery mechanisms

| Mechanism | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| **Namespace traversal** | Browse the namespace hierarchy to enumerate members. |
| **Class enumeration** | List all objects of an Object Class. |
| **Relationship traversal** | Follow identified edges (Deliverable 18) to related objects. |
| **Provenance/lineage traversal** | Walk CREATED-BY / DERIVED-FROM / VERSION-OF chains (Deliverable 22). |
| **Dependency traversal** | Walk DEPENDS-ON / REQUIRED-BY graphs (Deliverable 20). |
| **Federated catalog** | Discover across federated registries via the federation fabric (Deliverable 27). |

### 17.3 Discovery and privacy

Discovery is **authorization-scoped and privacy-aware**: existence and metadata are disclosed only to permitted requesters; sensitive attributes are protected (ARCH-SECURITY-001 privacy model); no discovery path reveals a secret or confers authority (UIL-15/17).

## DELIVERABLE 18 — UNIVERSAL RELATIONSHIP ARCHITECTURE

Relationships express how identified objects relate. Every relationship is **itself an identifiable, first-class object** (mission mandate; UIL-11), enabling relationships to carry metadata, versioning, provenance, and their own relationships.

### 18.1 Relationship as a first-class identified object

A relationship (edge) has: **UID · Relationship Type (dictionary-typed) · Source UID · Target UID · Direction · Cardinality · Metadata · Lifecycle State · Version · Ownership · Provenance.** Because an edge has a UID, the system can reference, audit, version, and reason about relationships exactly as it does objects.

### 18.2 Canonical relationship types

The mission's relationship vocabulary is organized into families; each type is a Relationship Dictionary entry with a UID. Inverse pairs are noted.

| Family | Types (inverse) |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Structural / Containment** | CONTAINS ↔ CONTAINED-BY; PART-OF ↔ HAS-PART; AGGREGATES ↔ AGGREGATED-BY; COMPOSES ↔ COMPOSED-BY |
| **Hierarchy / Membership** | PARENT-OF ↔ CHILD-OF; OWNER-OF ↔ OWNED-BY; MEMBER-OF ↔ HAS-MEMBER |
| **Classification** | INSTANCE-OF ↔ HAS-INSTANCE; SUBTYPE-OF ↔ SUPERTYPE-OF; SPECIALIZES ↔ GENERALIZES; EXTENDS ↔ EXTENDED-BY |
| **Realization** | IMPLEMENTS ↔ IMPLEMENTED-BY |
| **Dependency** | DEPENDS-ON ↔ REQUIRED-BY; USES ↔ USED-BY; REFERENCES ↔ REFERENCED-BY |
| **Provenance / Creation** | CREATES ↔ CREATED-BY; GENERATES ↔ GENERATED-BY; DERIVED-FROM ↔ SOURCE-OF |
| **Flow / Exchange** | IMPORTS ↔ IMPORTED-BY; EXPORTS ↔ EXPORTED-BY; CONSUMES ↔ CONSUMED-BY; PRODUCES ↔ PRODUCED-BY; PUBLISHES ↔ PUBLISHED-BY; SUBSCRIBES ↔ SUBSCRIBED-BY; CALLS ↔ CALLED-BY; INVOKES ↔ INVOKED-BY |
| **Association** | ASSOCIATES ↔ ASSOCIATED-WITH |
| **Replication / Sync** | MIRRORS ↔ MIRRORED-BY; SYNCHRONIZES ↔ SYNCHRONIZED-WITH; REPLICATES ↔ REPLICATED-BY; COPY-OF ↔ HAS-COPY |
| **Lifecycle / Version** | VERSION-OF ↔ HAS-VERSION; SUPERSEDES ↔ SUPERSEDED-BY; ARCHIVES ↔ ARCHIVED-BY; RESTORES ↔ RESTORED-BY |

### 18.3 Relationship rules

- **UID-only endpoints (UIL-09):** source and target are UIDs; relationships never use names or locators as endpoints.
- **Typed and dictionary-controlled:** relationship type is a Relationship Dictionary entry; ad-hoc types are prohibited (extension via the dictionary only, UIL-18).
- **Inverse consistency:** if the type defines an inverse, the inverse is derivable/traversable; the system keeps directed edges consistent.
- **Acyclicity where required:** dependency (Deliverable 20), classification, and provenance graphs SHALL be acyclic; general association graphs may contain cycles but are labeled as such (aligns with ARCH inward-only dependency rule AR-01).
- **Versioned and audited:** edge changes are versioned and audited (UIL-13/14); a removed edge is retired, not erased.
- **Non-constitutive:** no relationship confers authority; an OWNER-OF or MEMBER-OF edge is an accountability/organizational fact, never a grant of standing (UIL-17).

## DELIVERABLE 19 — UNIVERSAL OWNERSHIP ARCHITECTURE

Ownership binds every object to accountable owner(s). It is an accountability model, never an authority grant.

### 19.1 Ownership model

- Every object SHALL name **≥1 accountable owner** by UID; **no ownerless object may exist** (aligns with ARCH-DATA-001 §4 and IMP-005). Ownerlessness is a failure condition (Deliverable 33).
- Ownership is expressed via identified OWNER-OF edges and recorded in the Ownership Registry (Deliverable 10).
- Ownership roles are dictionary-typed (Ownership Dictionary), consistent with the registered role sets (e.g., Business/Domain/Capability/Component owner; Data Steward/Custodian). ENG-001 invents no new role (UIL-20).

### 19.2 Ownership properties

- **Mutable but governed:** ownership may change (transfer) under governance; each change is versioned and audited (UIL-13/14). The object's UID never changes on transfer (UIL-07).
- **Tiered:** an object may carry owners at multiple tiers (business/technical/operational); all are recorded.
- **Custodianship vs ownership:** custody (who holds/operates a representation) is distinct from ownership (who is accountable); both are identified and recorded.
- **Non-constitutive (UIL-17):** ownership confers operational accountability and engineering authorization scope only — never sovereignty, governance, or constituent standing (ID-01, AUTH-06).

## DELIVERABLE 20 — UNIVERSAL DEPENDENCY ARCHITECTURE

Dependencies express what an object requires in order to exist, build, resolve, or operate.

### 20.1 Dependency model

- Dependencies are identified DEPENDS-ON / REQUIRED-BY (and USES / USED-BY) edges recorded in the Dependency Registry.
- The dependency graph SHALL be **acyclic and single-direction (inward-only)**, consistent with the ARCH families' inward-only rule (AR-01). A would-be cycle is a failure condition and triggers a Gap Report.
- Dependencies are **versioned**: an edge may target a specific version or a version range (Deliverable 21); resolution honors the declared target.

### 20.2 Dependency kinds

| Kind | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Existence dependency** | The object cannot exist/be valid without the target (e.g., a component requires its capability). |
| **Build/generation dependency** | Required to build or generate (e.g., a blueprint requires its reference architecture). |
| **Resolution dependency** | Required to resolve references (e.g., a namespace requires its parent). |
| **Operational dependency** | Required at runtime (e.g., a service requires an identity service). |

### 20.3 Dependency rules

- **Registered targets only:** a dependency target SHALL be an allocated, resolvable UID (UIL-08/09).
- **No hidden coupling:** every operative dependency is declared and identified; undeclared coupling is a defect.
- **Impact-analyzable:** because edges are identified and versioned, the blast radius of any change is computable by traversal (Deliverable 17.2).
- **Non-constitutive:** dependency edges never encode authority (UIL-17).

## DELIVERABLE 21 — UNIVERSAL VERSIONING ARCHITECTURE

Versioning lets objects, types, dictionaries, relationships, and the scheme itself evolve **without ever changing a UID or breaking a reference.**

### 21.1 Versioning model

- **Identity is version-independent (UIL-07):** an object's UID is stable across all its versions. A *version* is a governed, append-only revision of the object's mutable facets, not a new object — **unless** the change is substantive enough to constitute a new object, in which case a **new object with its own UID** is created and linked by VERSION-OF / DERIVED-FROM (Deliverable 22). Governance defines the threshold; the UIS provides both mechanisms.
- **Version identifiers** follow a declared scheme (e.g., major/minor/patch semantics) recorded in the Version Dictionary; compatibility rules are explicit.
- **Version records are append-only (UIL-13):** each version is preserved and independently resolvable (`Resolve(UID, asOfVersion)`; Deliverable 14.1).

### 21.2 Scheme versioning (M3)

- The **UID scheme itself is versioned** (Deliverable 8.1 scheme-version marker). Scheme evolution SHALL preserve every existing UID and its resolution (UIL-19). Older UIDs remain valid and resolvable forever; new capabilities are additive (UIL-18).
- **Backward compatibility:** newer participants resolve older UIDs. **Forward compatibility:** older participants tolerate newer UIDs they do not fully understand (opacity, UIS-P-06, makes this safe: they treat the UID as an opaque token and resolve for meaning).

### 21.3 Versioning rules

- Never renumber (UIL-06); never reuse (UIL-05); never erase (UIL-13).
- Every version is owned, classified, audited, and (where applicable) certified.
- Deprecation and retirement of a version follow the lifecycle model (Deliverable 30) without releasing the UID.

## DELIVERABLE 22 — UNIVERSAL TRACEABILITY ARCHITECTURE

Traceability guarantees that every object's origin, lineage, and justification are recoverable — the backbone of auditability and "no orphan" (ARCH-GOV-001 Law 002).

### 22.1 Traceability dimensions

| Dimension | Trace |
|-----------|-------|
| **Origin traceability** | Object → Origin Authority → Namespace → allocation event. |
| **Type traceability** | Object → Object Class → `IdentifiableThing`. |
| **Lineage traceability** | Object → DERIVED-FROM / VERSION-OF / COPY-OF chain to its source(s). |
| **Structural traceability** | Object → CONTAINED-BY / PART-OF chain to its whole. |
| **Provenance traceability** | Object → CREATED-BY / GENERATED-BY actor/generator. |
| **Corpus lineage** | Asset → Component → Capability → Domain → Universe (ARCH hierarchy), and, for generated assets, → REF realization → CAT catalog → ARCH authority (the established backward-traceability rule). |
| **Justification traceability** | Object → the determination/requirement that authorized it (honors adjudicated RAT positions as read-only references). |

### 22.2 Traceability rules

- **Uniform backward-traceability (no orphans):** every object resolves a complete, unbroken chain to a registered authority and, where applicable, to the Universe root. A broken chain is a failure condition (Deliverable 33).
- **All edges identified:** every traceability link is an identified relationship (Deliverable 18), so traces are themselves auditable and versioned.
- **Immutable and append-only (UIL-13):** provenance is never rewritten; corrections are new provenance records.
- **Read-only over canon:** traceability references to constitutional/EES/ARCH/CAT/REF/GEN/IMP determinations are navigational and never modify them (UIL-20).

## DELIVERABLE 23 — UNIVERSAL GOVERNANCE ARCHITECTURE

Governance administers the *rules* of the UIS — object classes, namespaces, dictionaries, allocation delegation, and policies — as an engineering function that records and never ratifies (RG-02).

### 23.1 Governance objects

The following are all identified objects governed through the UIS itself: Object Classes, Namespaces, Dictionary entries, Relationship Types, Allocation delegations, Lifecycle/Status vocabularies, Policies, Standards, Validation rules, and the UID scheme version.

### 23.2 Governance operations

| Operation | Description | Constraint |
|-----------|-------------|------------|
| **Define type** | Register a new Object Class or Relationship Type. | Extension only; no contradiction of canon (UIL-20). |
| **Delegate namespace** | Grant a sub-namespace/allocation partition. | Identified, audited, revocable-in-governance; never reclaims minted UIDs. |
| **Publish dictionary term** | Add/deprecate a controlled term. | Versioned; meaning never silently redefined. |
| **Set policy** | Record a validation/classification/retention policy. | Record-only; enacts nothing (RG-02). |
| **Evolve scheme** | Version the UID scheme. | Compatibility-preserving (UIL-19). |

### 23.3 Governance properties

- **Record-only (RG-02):** governance decisions are recorded engineering facts; they confer no constitutional/governance/constituent authority (UIL-17, AUTH-06).
- **Auditable and versioned (UIL-13/14):** every governance act is append-only and attributed.
- **Subordinate:** UIS governance is subordinate to the corpus, the Technology Constitution, and the Implementation Governance Baseline; conflicts resolve in favor of the higher instrument.
- **Separation from constitutional governance:** UIS governance administers identity *engineering*; it never touches ratification, sovereignty, or EC-series matters, and cannot authorize EC-1.

## DELIVERABLE 24 — UNIVERSAL SECURITY ARCHITECTURE

Security protects identities and identity operations. It realizes the registered ARCH-SECURITY-001 model; it invents no new security construct (UIL-20).

### 24.1 Security dimensions

| Dimension | Rule |
|-----------|------|
| **Authentication** | Binds a claimant to an identity via registered methods (ARCH-SECURITY-001 / IMP-005: password, certificate, token, OAuth, OIDC, SAML, MFA, service, agent). Authentication verifies; it confers no authority (UIL-17). |
| **Authorization** | Governs who may allocate, amend, transition, resolve-restricted, or discover — under least privilege and default-deny (RBAC/ABAC/policy). Authorization permits actions; it ratifies nothing (RG-02). |
| **Confidentiality** | Restricted identity metadata is disclosed only to permitted requesters; classification (ARCH-DATA-001 8 levels) governs exposure. |
| **Integrity** | Identity records are tamper-evident (Deliverable 25). |
| **Cryptography / Keys** | Signing and key management follow the registered PKI/KMS model; keys are referenced by handle, never embedded (UIL-15). |
| **Secrets** | Secrets/credentials reside only in a managed secret store; **no secret in any UID, identity record, config, or log** (RR-07, SEC-04/05, ID-04). |
| **Privacy** | PII in identity metadata is minimized, purpose-limited, consent-governed, and retention-bound (ARCH-SECURITY-001 privacy model). |
| **Threat protection** | Detection/monitoring/response for identity abuse (spoofing, enumeration, forgery, exhaustion attempts). |

### 24.2 Security invariants

- **No embedded secrets (UIL-15):** structurally prevents the SRC-08 credential-leak defect (RR-07).
- **Least privilege everywhere:** every identity operation is authorized; no unauthenticated capability exists.
- **Identity is not a credential:** a UID is a public, non-secret reference; possessing a UID grants nothing (UIL-17). Authentication material is separate, secret-stored, and never part of the identity record.
- **Privacy-aware discovery/search:** existence and metadata disclosure is authorization-scoped (Deliverables 16/17).

## DELIVERABLE 25 — UNIVERSAL INTEGRITY ARCHITECTURE

Integrity guarantees that identity records are complete, uncorrupted, and tamper-evident.

### 25.1 Integrity mechanisms

| Mechanism | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| **UID integrity component** | Detects accidental corruption/transcription error of the token (Deliverable 8.1). |
| **Record integrity evidence** | Tamper-evidence over each UIR version (e.g., cryptographic digests/signatures — mechanism required, algorithm unspecified). |
| **Append-only history** | The record cannot be silently altered; every change is a new, evidenced version (UIL-13). |
| **Referential integrity checks** | Continuous verification that references resolve and dependency/provenance graphs remain acyclic (Deliverables 15/20). |
| **Uniqueness invariant checks** | Continuous verification that no duplicate UID has entered any partition (UIL-03). |

### 25.2 Integrity rules

- **Verifiable (UIL-14):** any party can verify a record's integrity from its evidence without trusting the presenter.
- **Federation-safe:** integrity evidence travels with federated records so cross-boundary trust does not require a central authority (Deliverable 27).
- **Detect-and-report:** integrity violations are detected, quarantined, reported (Gap Report), and never silently "repaired" in a way that erases history.

## DELIVERABLE 26 — UNIVERSAL AUDIT ARCHITECTURE

Audit provides the complete, immutable history of every identity and every identity operation.

### 26.1 Audited events

Allocation, registration, resolution (as access), amendment, lifecycle transition, ownership transfer, relationship/dependency change, dictionary/namespace/scheme governance acts, integrity checks, federation exchanges, and access to restricted metadata. Each audit event is itself an identified object (UID) with actor, timestamp, operation, before/after version references, and outcome.

### 26.2 Audit properties

- **Append-only and immutable (UIL-13):** audit records are never altered or deleted within retention.
- **Attributed:** every event names the acting identity (by UID) — human, service, or agent (ARCH-AI-001 agent identity).
- **Complete:** no state change occurs without a corresponding audit event; "silent" mutation is a failure condition.
- **Secret-free (UIL-15):** audit records never contain secrets.
- **Queryable and traceable:** audit history is discoverable (Deliverable 17) and supports reconstruction of any object's full timeline.

## DELIVERABLE 27 — UNIVERSAL FEDERATION ARCHITECTURE

Federation lets independent repositories, organizations, platforms, and clouds operate the UIS autonomously while preserving one coherent global identity space.

### 27.1 Federation model

- **Partition by authority/namespace (Deliverable 12/13):** each federated participant operates its own root-delegated namespace(s) and allocation authority; partitions are disjoint, so cross-participant collisions are impossible (UIL-16).
- **Federation fabric:** a logical mesh that routes resolution, discovery, and search across participants and carries integrity evidence with records so trust does not require a central authority.
- **Autonomy:** participants allocate, resolve locally, and operate offline; they reconcile via append-only exchange (Deliverable 13.3) that can never collide or overwrite.

### 27.2 Federation operations

| Operation | Behavior |
|-----------|----------|
| **Federated resolve** | Route a UID to its owning participant; return the UIR view with a consistency label. |
| **Federated discover/search** | Fan out across participants; merge results with per-source consistency labels. |
| **Federated exchange** | Import/export identity records with integrity evidence; imports never overwrite local truth (append-only, provenance-tagged). |
| **Trust establishment** | Explicit, non-implicit trust between participants (ARCH-SECURITY-001 trust model); confers engineering trust only, never sovereignty (AUTH-06). |

### 27.3 Federation rules

- **No single point of control (UIS-P-16):** the global space has no mandatory central master; coherence is structural (disjoint partitions), not custodial.
- **Coherence over convenience:** a participant may not mint outside its delegated partition; doing so is a governance violation and its records are rejected on exchange.
- **Sovereign-boundary respect:** cross-sovereign exchange preserves classification and privacy; provisional-boundary flags are preserved (IP-05). Federation confers no cross-sovereign authority.

## DELIVERABLE 28 — UNIVERSAL SCALABILITY ARCHITECTURE

Scalability guarantees the UIS never requires redesign because of growth.

### 28.1 Scaling dimensions

| Dimension | Mechanism |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Identity-space scale** | Unbounded, partitioned space; new partitions by delegation; no fixed width to exhaust (UIL-18). |
| **Allocation throughput** | Decentralized minting; no global lock; linear horizontal scale by adding authorities/partitions (Deliverable 13). |
| **Registry scale** | Federated, partitionable registries; horizontal partitioning by authority/namespace; vertical scaling of any partition. |
| **Resolution/search scale** | Derived, replicable, cacheable read paths; consistency labeled per read (UIS-P-22). |
| **Federation scale** | Add participants without reconfiguring existing ones (partition disjointness). |

### 28.2 Scalability guarantees

- **No exhaustion, ever** (UIL-03/18): growth adds partitions; it never fills a fixed space.
- **No renumbering under scale** (UIL-06): scaling reorganizes routing/partitioning, never identifiers.
- **Availability & fault tolerance:** replication and failover keep allocation and resolution available under partition/node failure (ARCH-BCDR-001); offline operation degrades gracefully.
- **Strong where required, eventual where appropriate (UIS-P-22):** uniqueness/allocation are strongly consistent within a partition; cross-partition views may be eventually consistent and are labeled as such.

## DELIVERABLE 29 — UNIVERSAL EXTENSION ARCHITECTURE

Extension is how the UIS admits the unknown — new object types, relationships, dictionaries, namespaces, and future paradigms — by addition, never redesign.

## DELIVERABLE 30 — UNIVERSAL LIFECYCLE ARCHITECTURE

The lifecycle governs an identity's states from proposal to archival — without ever releasing the identifier.

### 30.1 Lifecycle states

| State | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Proposed / Reserved** | UID allocated; object not yet realized (prospective identity, Deliverable 4.4). |
| **Active** | Object exists and is in use; fully resolvable and bindable. |
| **Deprecated** | Discouraged for new use; still resolvable; successor may exist (SUPERSEDED-BY). |
| **Suspended** | Temporarily held (e.g., under review); resolvable with state flagged. |
| **Retired** | No longer in use; **identifier remains permanently valid and resolvable** (UIL-05/12). |
| **Archived** | Preserved for record/recovery; resolvable; representation may be cold-stored. |
| **Destroyed (representation)** | The object's *representation* is destroyed under authorized retention; the **UID and its record persist** as a tombstone (identity is never destroyed; UIL-04/05). |

### 30.2 Lifecycle rules

- **Governed transitions only (UIL-12):** each transition is permitted by the state model, authorized, versioned, and audited.
- **No identifier release (UIL-05):** retirement, archival, and representation-destruction never free the UID for reuse; a retired UID resolves to its terminal record forever.
- **Recovery (UIS-P-23 / ARCH-BCDR-001):** archived/suspended objects can be restored to a prior consistent state without re-identifying (same UID).
- **Reversibility with history:** reversible transitions (suspend/restore) preserve full history; irreversible ones (representation-destruction under retention) are auditable and never erase the identity record.

## DELIVERABLE 31 — UNIVERSAL OPERATIONAL MODEL

The operational model describes how the UIS is run, observed, and sustained — at the level of roles and responsibilities, not products.

### 31.1 Operational roles (all non-constitutive)

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|----------------|
| **Root Namespace/Allocation Authority** | Holds the universal root; delegates partitions; never mints application UIDs directly. |
| **Delegated Authorities** | Mint UIDs within their partitions; operate registries; reconcile in federation. |
| **Registry Operators** | Run registry partitions; ensure append-only, replication, backup, integrity. |
| **Dictionary/Type Stewards** | Curate controlled vocabularies and object-class definitions. |
| **Identity Owners/Custodians** | Accountable for and operate individual objects (Deliverable 19). |
| **Governance Function** | Administers types/namespaces/policies (record-only, Deliverable 23). |
| **Auditors** | Verify integrity, audit completeness, and law-conformance. |

### 31.2 Operational processes

Allocation, registration, resolution, amendment, transition, reconciliation (federation), integrity sweeps, orphan/dangling-reference sweeps, backup/restore drills, and scheme-version rollout. Each process is observable (metrics/logs/traces per ARCH-OBS-001), audited (Deliverable 26), and recoverable (ARCH-BCDR-001).

### 31.3 Operational service levels (illustrative dimensions)

- **Allocation availability** and latency per authority/partition.
- **Resolution availability**, latency, and correctness (no false unknowns).
- **Uniqueness guarantee**: zero observed collisions (a collision is a Sev-1 incident).
- **Integrity guarantee**: zero unresolved tamper/corruption findings.
- **Federation reconciliation lag** (eventual-consistency bound, labeled).

Concrete SLO targets, tooling, and topologies are deferred to implementation (IMP platforms) and are out of scope here (UIS-P-07).

### 31.4 Operational simplicity

The design favors operational simplicity: decentralized allocation removes global bottlenecks; opacity removes parsing coupling; append-only removes destructive-edit hazards; disjoint partitions remove cross-authority coordination. The common failure modes of identity systems (collisions, renumbering migrations, reference rot, central-bottleneck outages) are **structurally excluded**, not merely mitigated.

## DELIVERABLE 36 — FUTURE EVOLUTION STRATEGY

The UIS is designed to evolve forever without redesign. The evolution strategy:

- **Extend, never redesign (UIL-18).** New object classes, relationship types, dictionaries, namespaces, and metadata dimensions are additive; existing UIDs are untouched.
- **Version the scheme, preserve the identifiers (UIL-19).** The UID scheme carries a version marker (Deliverable 8.1). New versions add capabilities (e.g., new integrity mechanisms, new partition strategies) while every prior UID remains valid and resolvable.
- **Absorb new paradigms via opacity (UIS-P-06).** Because consumers treat UIDs as opaque and obtain meaning by resolution, entirely new object kinds and computing paradigms are admitted as new `IdentifiableThing` classes without disturbing existing participants.
- **Federate outward.** New organizations, repositories, platforms, and clouds join by delegation into disjoint partitions; the global space grows without central reconfiguration (Deliverable 27).
- **Reconcile with canon through governance.** Any evolution that would touch registered canonical identity classes/registries is routed through the registered governance path (UIL-20); ENG-001 remains the stable engineering root beneath such changes.
- **Deprecate gracefully.** Superseded types/terms move through the lifecycle (deprecated → retired) with successors linked by SUPERSEDED-BY; nothing is deleted or renumbered.

**Guarantee:** no foreseeable growth — in scale, diversity, federation, or paradigm — requires the UIS to be redesigned, renumbered, or re-founded. The identity foundation is permanent.

## DELIVERABLE 37 — ENGINEERING RECOMMENDATIONS

For the programs that will realize the UIS (IMP and successors), ENG-001 recommends (recommendations only; they enact nothing):

1. **Treat the registry as the single source of identity truth**; make resolution and search explicitly derived and consistency-labeled (UIS-P-22).
2. **Realize allocation as decentralized and offline-capable first**; never introduce a mandatory global minting lock (Deliverable 13).
3. **Keep UIDs opaque at every boundary**; forbid parsing UID internals for semantics anywhere in downstream code (UIS-P-06). Obtain class/owner/state by resolution only.
4. **Bind identity records to append-only storage semantics**; corrections are versions, never in-place edits (UIL-13).
5. **Keep authentication material entirely out of identity records**; reference secrets by handle to a managed store (UIL-15, RR-07).
6. **Carry integrity evidence with federated records** so cross-boundary trust needs no central master (Deliverables 25/27).
7. **Instrument uniqueness, orphan, and dangling-reference sweeps as continuous background invariants**, not one-off checks (Deliverables 15/17/25).
8. **Preserve canonical identifiers exactly** (CAT-DATA-001 / IMP-005); introduce no new numbering scheme; route any canonical change through governance (UIL-20).
9. **Model relationships as first-class identified objects** so impact analysis, versioning, and audit apply uniformly (Deliverable 18).
10. **Adopt this document as the immutable engineering reference for identity**; downstream artifacts cite ENG-001 rather than re-deriving identity semantics.
