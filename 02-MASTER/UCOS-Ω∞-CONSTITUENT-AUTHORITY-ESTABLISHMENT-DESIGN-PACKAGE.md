# PART 1 — EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

CA-001 determined that **no legitimate constituent authority exists** for UCOS Ω∞ and that one **must be externally established** by satisfying seven minimum conditions (MC-1…MC-7). CA-002 answers: *can a minimum establishment pathway be designed, and what would it require?*

**Finding:** **Yes — a minimum establishment pathway can be fully designed.** The transformation from *potential* to *certifiable* constituent authority is a definable, finite sequence: a candidate that already possesses the *intrinsic* conditions (original legitimacy, genuine sovereign standing, exogeneity) must **volitionally assume** the role and then **secure and evidence recognition** from the governed — after which its capacities to constitute, bind, and fix precedence become exercisable, and satisfaction of MC-1…MC-7 becomes certifiable.

**But the design cannot execute itself.** Every step is **external** by nature. The pivotal, hardest condition — **MC-5 (recognition/acceptance)** — can only be produced by real-world acts of the governed, which no document (including this one) can manufacture. Therefore:
- Establishment **can be designed** (this package).
- Establishment **cannot be certified** from within this program (certification requires externally-produced evidence).
- Establishment **cannot occur without external action.**
- **EC-1 remains NOT authorized.**

**Final determination: OPTION 1 — Design Complete; External Execution Required.**

# PART 3 — ESTABLISHMENT PROBLEM DEFINITION

**The transformation to design:**

> `Potential Constituent Authority` → *(minimum externally-executable pathway satisfying MC-1…MC-7)* → `Certifiable Constituent Authority`

**Two kinds of condition** (a key design insight):
- **Intrinsic conditions** — properties a candidate either *has or lacks* independently of any act: MC-1 (original legitimacy), MC-2 (genuine sovereign standing), MC-6 (exogeneity). These are *verified*, not *manufactured*, by the pathway.
- **Enacted/relational conditions** — states produced by *doing something*: MC-7 (the volitional founding act), MC-5 (recognition by the governed), and — as consequences — MC-3/MC-4 (capacities that become *exercisable* once MC-1/MC-2 hold and the role is assumed).

**Design implication:** the pathway cannot "give" a candidate intrinsic conditions it lacks; it can only (a) **verify** intrinsic conditions, (b) **sequence** the volitional act, and (c) **secure and evidence** recognition. A candidate lacking MC-1/MC-2/MC-6 cannot be routed to certifiability by any pathway — it is simply not a valid candidate. This bounds the design honestly.

# PART 4 — MC-1 THROUGH MC-7 ANALYSIS

*(For each: Meaning · Necessity · Verification Requirement · Failure Modes. Analysis only.)*

### MC-1 — Original (Constituent) Legitimacy
- **Meaning:** The candidate's authority originates independently and is not derived from the UCOS Ω∞ corpus.
- **Necessity:** A derived authority is *constituted*, and constituted authority cannot found the order (Phase 7 circularity). Originality is the definitional core of constituent power.
- **Verification Requirement:** Evidence that the candidate's standing pre-exists and is independent of the corpus (e.g., ownership/origination of the program predating the constitutional order).
- **Failure Modes:** Authority traceable back to the corpus (circular); authority that is a re-labeling of a constituted role.

### MC-2 — Genuine Sovereign Standing
- **Meaning:** The candidate genuinely holds authority over the UCOS Ω∞ domain — not assumed, fabricated, or bypassed.
- **Necessity:** AUTH-02/03 require authority to flow from a real sovereign; AUTH-06 voids fabricated sovereignty. Without genuine standing the founding is void.
- **Verification Requirement:** Evidence of real, recognized standing over the domain (e.g., ownership, legal competence, or a genuine delegation from a pre-existing sovereign).
- **Failure Modes:** Self-assertion without basis (AUTH-06 violation); standing that does not actually reach the domain to be bound.

### MC-3 — Capacity to Constitute & Bind
- **Meaning:** The candidate can empower a ratification organ and issue outputs binding on the constituted order.
- **Necessity:** EC-1/EC-2 require establishing a ratifier whose acts are enforceable (GOV-04); a non-binding founder founds nothing.
- **Verification Requirement:** Evidence that the candidate's acts have binding force over the domain (enforceability in fact).
- **Failure Modes:** Advisory-only standing (cf. SRC-08's advisory tier, SUP-05); acts that participants can ignore without consequence.

### MC-4 — Capacity to Fix Precedence
- **Meaning:** The candidate can authoritatively resolve the dual-supremacy conflict (SUP-14) — fixing which document is senior.
- **Necessity:** RAT-11 and the ontology chain (RAT-02/03) depend on a settled seniority; the working answer is *pro tempore* only.
- **Verification Requirement:** Evidence that the candidate's precedence determination is accepted as binding by the governed.
- **Failure Modes:** A precedence call that stakeholders reject (reproduces the tie); a call made without genuine authority (void).

### MC-5 — Recognition by the Governed *(pivotal)*
- **Meaning:** Those the order would bind actually accept the candidate as the legitimate constituent authority.
- **Necessity:** Across every authority theory (Part 4 of CA-001), legitimacy has an irreducible **acceptance** component; the corpus's own trust chain (Audit → Trust → Governance, LAW-AX03/INV02) cannot start without it.
- **Verification Requirement:** **Externally-produced** evidence of acceptance by the relevant community (adoption, attestation, or equivalent recognition acts). *No document can substitute for this.*
- **Failure Modes:** Assumed/assessed recognition that is not actual; partial recognition (a subset accepts, the broader governed set does not); coerced "recognition" (not genuine acceptance).

### MC-6 — Exogeneity
- **Meaning:** The candidate sits outside the constituted order and is not bound by its operating rules (e.g., Ω-010) when performing the founding act.
- **Necessity:** Only an authority not subject to "ratification before structural change" can take the un-ratified first step (Phase 6 §5).
- **Verification Requirement:** Evidence that the candidate is not a constituted role within the corpus.
- **Failure Modes:** A candidate that is actually an internal/constituted actor (trapped by Ω-010); a candidate whose authority is conditioned on corpus rules.

### MC-7 — Volitional Assumption of the Role
- **Meaning:** The candidate *actually decides and acts* to assume constituent authority; latent capacity is inert without the deliberate act.
- **Necessity:** Establishment is an act, not a status; MC-1/MC-2/MC-6 describe a *capable* candidate, but nothing happens until it *acts*.
- **Verification Requirement:** A recorded, deliberate founding act attributable to the candidate.
- **Failure Modes:** Perpetual latency (capable party never acts); ambiguous/half-acts that cannot be relied upon; acting before recognition (premature — see Part 10).

# PART 5 — ESTABLISHMENT PATHWAY OPTIONS

*(Multiple candidate pathways, mapped to the CA-001 founding models. None is selected, recommended, or activated. Each is a description of the external steps that satisfying MC-1…MC-7 would require under that model.)*

### Pathway P-A — Proprietary / Originator Activation *(model FM-1)*
- **Shape:** A candidate with genuine origination/ownership standing (verifies MC-1/MC-2/MC-6) **volitionally assumes** the constituent role (MC-7), then **secures recognition** from the governed community (MC-5); its capacities to constitute/bind/fix-precedence (MC-3/MC-4) become exercisable.
- **MC coverage:** Intrinsic (MC-1/2/6) likely strong; MC-7 straightforward; **MC-5 is the burden** — recognition must extend to the full governed set, not just the owner.
- **Best-fit when:** the governed community is bounded and already accepts the originator's standing.

### Pathway P-B — Constituent-Assembly Convening *(model FM-2)*
- **Shape:** The relevant governed community is **assembled**; it collectively exercises constituent power (MC-1/MC-2 via popular basis), assumes the role by collective act (MC-7), and recognition is intrinsic to participation (MC-5 strong).
- **MC coverage:** MC-5 strongest of all pathways; but requires a legitimate **convener** and an identifiable community — a possible regress (who convenes legitimately?).
- **Best-fit when:** a definable community exists and can be legitimately assembled.

### Pathway P-C — Delegated Authority from a Pre-existing Sovereign *(model FM-4)*
- **Shape:** An already-legitimate external sovereign **delegates** or directly exercises founding authority (MC-1/MC-2 inherited from the delegator), acts (MC-7), and recognition follows from the delegator's standing (MC-5 via the delegator's recognized authority).
- **MC coverage:** Clean MC-1…MC-4 if a genuine delegator exists; **depends on identifying that pre-existing sovereign** (the very thing the corpus lacks internally — must be external).
- **Best-fit when:** a recognized external sovereign with reach over the domain exists.

### Pathway P-D — Multi-Stakeholder Compact *(model FM-6)*
- **Shape:** Several independently-legitimate parties **jointly** constitute the order by agreement (MC-1/MC-2 from each party), co-act (MC-7), and mutual + community recognition supplies MC-5.
- **MC coverage:** Robust MC-5 if parties are themselves legitimate and the compact is accepted; **coordination cost and each party's own legitimacy** must be established first.
- **Best-fit when:** authority over the domain is genuinely distributed among several legitimate parties.

### Pathway P-E — Institutional / Legal-Rational Chartering *(model FM-5)*
- **Shape:** An existing legally-competent entity whose charter reaches the domain exercises founding authority under that charter (MC-1/MC-2 from legal standing), acts (MC-7), and recognition flows from the entity's established legitimacy (MC-5 via legal-rational acceptance).
- **MC coverage:** Strong MC-1…MC-4 and durable MC-5 **if** such an entity exists and its charter genuinely extends to constituting *this* order.
- **Best-fit when:** a competent institution with domain reach exists.

### Common minimum spine (shared by all pathways)
1. **Candidate qualification** — verify intrinsic conditions MC-1, MC-2, MC-6.
2. **Volitional founding act** — MC-7 (deliberate, recorded).
3. **Recognition acquisition** — MC-5 (externally produced, from the governed).
4. **Capacity exercise** — MC-3, MC-4 become exercisable and are demonstrated.
5. **Evidence assembly** — produce the certification evidence (Part 7).

*No pathway is selected. The choice among P-A…P-E depends on facts about the real-world domain that this design program does not possess and does not decide.*

# PART 6 — RECOGNITION MODEL ANALYSIS

MC-5 is the pivotal condition; recognition can be sought at four levels. *(Analysis only.)*

| Recognition level | Who accepts | Strength for MC-5 | Limitation |
|-------------------|-------------|-------------------|------------|
| **Internal recognition** | Participants/actors *within* the system-to-be | Weak alone — internal actors are (or become) constituted; their acceptance risks circularity | Cannot by itself legitimate the founder (the founder precedes them) |
| **External recognition** | Parties outside the system (legal, market, peer, regulatory) | Strong for MC-2 genuineness and durability | May not represent the *governed* specifically |
| **Stakeholder recognition** | Those who own/commission/fund/sponsor the program | Strong for proprietary/institutional pathways (P-A/P-E); directly evidences standing | Narrow if stakeholders ≠ the full governed set |
| **Governance recognition** | Downstream constituted bodies | **Deferred/circular** — those bodies do not yet exist and would be created *by* the founding | Cannot be a precondition; only a *post-hoc* confirmation |

**Recognition design conclusion:** Legitimate MC-5 satisfaction requires recognition from the **governed set as actually defined** — most robustly a combination of **stakeholder + external** recognition (and, where a community exists, **assembly** recognition per P-B). **Governance recognition cannot be a prerequisite** (it is downstream and circular), and **internal recognition alone is insufficient.** The recognition burden is intrinsically **out-of-corpus** and **out-of-this-program**.

# PART 10 — RISK MODEL (risks of premature establishment)

| Risk ID | Risk | Consequence | Trigger to avoid |
|---------|------|-------------|------------------|
| ER-1 | **Void founding** — acting without genuine MC-2 standing | AUTH-06 violation; the founding is void; deadlock returns with lost trust | Assuming the role on self-declared authority |
| ER-2 | **Premature EC-1** — assuming the role (MC-7) before recognition (MC-5) | Illegitimate/contested founding; trust chain (AX03) never starts | Acting at R2 and proceeding to EC-1 without reaching R3/R4 |
| ER-3 | **Partial recognition** — only a subset of the governed accepts | Fractured legitimacy; contested authority; risk of parallel claims | Treating stakeholder-only acceptance as full MC-5 |
| ER-4 | **Circular recognition** — relying on downstream governance bodies to legitimate the founder | Logical circularity; no valid recognition | Using governance recognition as a *pre*-condition |
| ER-5 | **Regress** — a pathway (P-B/P-C/P-D) relies on a convener/delegator that itself lacks legitimacy | Recursion of the same gap one level up | Convening/delegating from an unrecognized source |
| ER-6 | **Scope overreach** — proprietary authority binds beyond its recognized reach (P-A/P-E) | Capture/illegitimacy over the excess scope | Extending precedence/constitution beyond the recognized domain |
| ER-7 | **False certification** — certifying MC satisfaction without complete evidence | An unfounded "certifiable" status; downstream synthesis rests on sand | Certifying at R2/R3 as though R4 |
| ER-8 | **Substance disturbance** — reopening adjudicated positions during establishment | Violates the freeze on decisions; re-work | Altering RAT-01…11 while founding |

**Risk conclusion:** every material risk is a risk of **acting too early or on insufficient/genuine authority**. The design's safeguard is strict adherence to the R0→R5 ordering with MC-5 as the non-skippable gate — and, crucially, keeping all execution external and un-activated until certifiable.

# PART 11 — DETERMINATION

### A. Can establishment be designed?

**YES.** The transformation Potential → Certifiable is a finite, well-defined sequence (Parts 3–9): verify intrinsic conditions (MC-1/2/6), perform the volitional act (MC-7), acquire and evidence recognition (MC-5), demonstrate capacities (MC-3/4), assemble certification evidence. Multiple valid pathways (P-A…P-E) realize it. The design is complete.

### B. Can establishment be certified?

**NO (not by this program).** Certification requires **externally-produced evidence** — above all MC-2 (genuine standing) and MC-5 (recognition by the governed) — which are real-world facts and acts that no design document can produce or attest. This program defines the certification *requirements* (Part 7) but cannot issue the certification.

### C. Can establishment occur without external action?

**NO.** Every readiness transition R0→R5 is an external act (qualification, founding, recognition). Nothing in this program, or in the corpus, can advance the state internally. Establishment is inherently exogenous.

### D. Is EC-1 now authorized?

**NO.** EC-1 remains gated. It becomes possible only at state **R5 (certified constituent authority)**, which lies beyond this design program. Designing the pathway does not authorize or perform it.
