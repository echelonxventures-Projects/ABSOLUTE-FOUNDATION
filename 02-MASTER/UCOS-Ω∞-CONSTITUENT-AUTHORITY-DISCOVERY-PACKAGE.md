# PART 2 — PROBLEM STATEMENT

The corpus imposes an obligation it cannot itself satisfy:

1. It requires that structural change be ratified (INVARIANT Ω-010; LAW CM-007) and that governance be "ratifiable" (GOV-04).

**The CA-001 problem:** Determine whether such an authority *already exists* outside the corpus (in which case identify/justify/evaluate/certify it), or whether it must be *established* (in which case determine the minimum conditions). The program may determine; it may not create.

# PART 3 — CONSTITUENT vs CONSTITUTED AUTHORITY ANALYSIS

**Definitions (standard constitutional theory):**
- **Constituted power** (*pouvoir constitué*) — authority that exists *within* and *according to* an established constitutional order; the power to govern under the rules.
- **Constituent power** (*pouvoir constituant*) — the originating authority that *creates* the constitutional order; by definition it precedes the order and is not derived from it.

**Application to UCOS Ω∞:**

| Dimension | Corpus contains | Corpus lacks |
|-----------|-----------------|--------------|
| Power to *operate* the order | YES — AUTH-01…12, GOV-01…10 | — |
| Power to *originate* the order | — | YES (constituent power absent) |
| Named holder of sovereignty | — | YES (AUTH-02 role defined, bearer unnamed) |
| Rule to break dual supremacy | — | YES (SUP-14 UNRESOLVED) |
| Permission to self-found | — | Explicitly denied (AUTH-06) |

**The circularity (why constituted power cannot become constituent power):** to act, any corpus authority must be authorized (AUTH-04) → authorization derives from sovereignty (AUTH-03) → sovereignty cannot be assumed/fabricated (AUTH-06). No internal actor can therefore self-elevate to found the order. Constituent power must come from **outside** the constituted order — this is a definitional necessity, not a curable defect.

**Analysis conclusion:** The distinction is decisive. The consolidation corpus is a complete specification of *constituted* power awaiting activation by a *constituent* power it neither contains nor can generate.

# PART 4 — AUTHORITY THEORY ANALYSIS

*(Reference frameworks applied for evaluation; none is adopted.)*

Three classical lenses inform the evaluation:

1. **Weber's typology of legitimate authority** — authority is legitimated in one of three ideal-typical ways: **traditional** (sanctified custom/inheritance), **charismatic** (devotion to an exceptional individual), and **legal-rational** (belief in enacted rules and the right of those elevated under them to command). Each implies a different basis on which a would-be founder's authority would be *accepted*.
2. **Constituent-power theory** (Sieyès; later Schmitt, Arendt) — the distinction that a nation/people/founder holds an *originating* power to make a constitution that is itself unbound by the (not-yet-existing) constitution. Legitimacy of this power is grounded in the *identity and will* of the constituent subject and its **recognition** by those it binds.
3. **Social-contract / consent theory** — legitimate political authority derives from the consent (actual or reasonably-presumed) of the governed; a founding is legitimate insofar as it is accepted by those subject to it.

**Common thread for CA-001:** across all three lenses, constituent legitimacy has an **irreducible recognition component** — authority becomes legitimate only when it is *accepted* by the relevant community. Documents can *record* authority; they cannot *manufacture* its acceptance. This is why no documentary artifact (including any produced by this program) can, by itself, certify a constituent authority into existence.

# PART 9 — AUTHORITY SELECTION CRITERIA

*(Criteria against which candidates are evaluated in Part 10. These are evaluation criteria only; the program applies them to determine sufficiency, not to select or appoint.)*

- **C-1 Source of legitimacy** — is the authority's legitimacy original (constituent) and grounded, or merely asserted?
- **C-2 Scope of authority** — does its authority actually extend over the UCOS Ω∞ domain and all it would bind?
- **C-3 Ability to perform EC-1** — can it identify a sovereign seat, empower a ratifier, and fix document precedence (SUP-14) with binding effect?
- **C-4 Constitutional compatibility** — is it consistent with the corpus's own sovereignty rules (AUTH-02/03/06), i.e., genuine and non-fabricated?
- **C-5 Governance compatibility** — can it stand up the Tier-1/Tier-2 governance capabilities (GAP-01…08) legitimately?
- **C-6 Recognition** — is it accepted by the governed community (the acceptance component from Part 4)?
- **C-7 Exogeneity** — does it sit outside the constituted order (not derived from the corpus)?

**Sufficiency rule:** an authority is a *certifiable existing* constituent authority only if it satisfies **all** of C-1…C-7 **on present evidence**. Satisfying some (e.g., de facto scope) while lacking others (e.g., demonstrated recognition) yields only a *latent candidate*, not a certified authority.

# PART 11 — DETERMINATION

### A. Does a legitimate constituent authority already exist?

**NO (not on present evidence).** No externally-recognized constituent authority is evidenced to exist for UCOS Ω∞. The strongest analytical candidate — the **originating/owning stakeholder(s)** (FM-1) — is a **latent locus**: it may hold de facto standing over the system but fails the recognition/legitimacy tests (C-1 genuineness + C-6 acceptance) *as far as this determination program can evidence.* Latent capacity is not certified constituent authority. *(This program identifies the candidate as a category only; it does not name, appoint, or vest it.)*

### B. Can it be derived from the corpus?

**NO.** Re-confirmed from Phase 7: constituent power is by definition non-derived; the AUTH-04→AUTH-03→AUTH-06 chain closes into a circle with no internal seed; and Ω-010/CM-007 trap any internal candidate. Derivation is logically foreclosed.

### C. Must it be externally established?

**YES.** Since no legitimate constituent authority is evidenced to exist and none can be derived, the authority must be **externally established** — meaning a latent candidate (or another legitimate party) must be *activated and recognized* as constituent authority by satisfying the minimum conditions below. "Established" here means *recognized/constituted as legitimate*, not *invented* — and this program performs neither.

### D. What minimum conditions are required?

For a party to become a legitimate constituent authority capable of performing EC-1 (mapping to the Phase-7 capabilities CAC-01…07 and the Part-9 criteria):

- **MC-1 — Original (constituent) legitimacy** [C-1/CAC-01]: an authority whose standing is not derived from the corpus.
- **MC-2 — Genuine sovereign standing** [C-4/CAC-02]: real, non-fabricated authority over the UCOS Ω∞ domain (satisfying AUTH-06).
- **MC-3 — Capacity to constitute & bind** [C-3/CAC-03/05]: power to empower a ratifier and issue binding outputs.
- **MC-4 — Capacity to fix precedence** [C-3/CAC-04]: power to resolve the dual-supremacy (SUP-14) authoritatively.
- **MC-5 — Recognition by the governed** [C-6/CAC-06]: demonstrated acceptance by the community the order would bind (the acceptance component of Part 4).
- **MC-6 — Exogeneity** [C-7/CAC-07]: position outside the constituted order.
- **MC-7 — Volitional assumption of the role**: the latent candidate must *actually act* to assume constituent authority — capacity alone is inert without the deliberate founding act.

**Sufficiency:** MC-1…MC-7 are jointly necessary. Satisfying MC-1/MC-2/MC-3 (a capable owner) without MC-5 (recognition) yields an *illegitimate* founding that is void under the corpus's own rules; hence MC-5 is the pivotal, hardest-to-evidence condition.
